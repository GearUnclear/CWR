APPROACH
1. Only standing water mirrors. Outside puddles the ground gets no screen-space reflection at all (the
   trace gives it weight 0 and skips the march there), its forward film is thin and rough (a dim broad
   glow near a lamp or the fire, no streak), and it shows the rain as darker, deeper colour instead.
2. Puddles are placed by the terrain's shape. The height map (12.5 m grid on Abel and Eden, read with
   the smoothed interpolation the shaders already use, so no crease at its cells) gives:
   - a basin term: the hollow against the 4 neighbours one cell away plus a quarter of the hollow
     against 4 diagonal points 2.5 cells away (a wider basin). Hollows raise the puddle field by up to
     kPuddleBasin, crests lower it by at most kPuddleCrest of that.
   - the slope: the puddle threshold rises with it (gentle ground holds a little more, steeper less) and
     puddles fade out between a 14 % and a 25 % slope (none on steep ground).
3. Natural outlines below the map's cells: the micro relief is the old three value-noise octaves, but
   over a warped position and with each octave turned off the world's axes, so no water line runs
   straight along a grid line. Its finer octaves still fade below the pixel's footprint.
4. Soft damp rim and murky water: the damp margin is wider (0.06 -> 0.1 of the field) and darker
   (0.9 -> 0.8); under a puddle the ground's texture grain fades 60 % toward the land's own colour (the
   ground field), so a puddle reads as water even where it mirrors something as dark as the ground (the
   jeep problem below).
The forward pass and the trace share wetPuddle as before, so their puddles agree pixel for pixel.

WHAT I FOUND (probe evidence)
- Probe override (work/mkprobe1.py, mkprobe2.py; shots_probe1, shots_probe2; test look_probe_p2_*)
  wrote the height map's slope and hollow into the albedo debug view (debug=7). The values pass
  through the post chain, so they are rough (two encodings of the same slope disagreed by about 20 %),
  but the picture is clear:
  - The town square in town_night_rain, seam_b, seam_d, seam_day sits on a gentle convex rise: slope
    about 3 to 6 %, hollow -0.12 to -0.19 m (a crest) in the foreground; the ground behind the tree and
    toward the left houses is flatter and level or slightly hollow.
  - The Eden rain_day meadow is a slope of roughly 10 to 14 % with a hollow of about +0.2 m left of
    the jeep.
  - Round 3's puddle field barely used the shape: kPuddleHollow 0.1 per metre meant a 0.15 m crest
    moved the field by 0.015 (noise range about 0.25 to 0.75), and the flatness gate kept puddles up
    to about 20 % slope.
- shots_probe2 (first version of this change, before tuning): with only shape, the crest square lost
  almost all its puddles; the matte ground killed every non-puddle streak (no wall or lamp streaks left).
- The rain_day "hard straight edge" by the jeep: the probe shows the puddle runs on under the jeep; the
  trace's rays there go behind the jeep's hull and take its dark underside, which matched the grass, so
  the visible puddle stopped at the mirror image of the jeep's front (a vertical line). With the shape
  placement the meadow slope holds only small puddles in its left hollow, none under the jeep in this
  view; the murky water also keeps a puddle visible where it mirrors something dark.

HONEST WEAK POINTS
- The night views read darker and less obviously rainy than round 3. With the ground matte, the rain
  shows as darker ground, a few puddles and the rain streaks in the air; puddles at night mirror a dark
  sky unless a lit wall, the lamp or the fire lines up behind them, and the town square is a crest, so
  this approach puts few puddles in its foreground. At thumbnail size nothing but puddles reflects, but
  the night frames may look "damp" more than "pouring". A larger puddle share (lower kPuddleSlopeFree
  bonus threshold or a smaller kPuddleCrest) is the knob if the owner wants more water.
- rain_day: the meadow is a 10 to 14 % slope, so it now holds only small puddles in its left hollow;
  the old puddle beside the jeep is gone (by design of this approach), not fixed in place.
- No road ruts or wheel tracks: neither pass knows a road's direction (the trace only has the world
  position and a road tag), so ruts would need a per-pixel direction or mask from the forward pass.
- The height map is 12.5 m per texel: it says which areas collect water, not where each puddle sits;
  the outline still comes from noise.
- The trace's "behind a hull" fallback (dark underside) still draws a mirror-image edge where a puddle
  runs under a vehicle; the murky water makes the puddle itself visible but the reflection edge stays.
- Probe numbers pass through the post chain and are approximate; puddle share in the final frames and
  the cost were not measured (4 more height reads and 2 more noise reads per wet ground pixel per pass,
  minus the skipped march on non-puddle ground in the trace, which should be a net saving).
- Until the terrain map is built (under a second after load) puddles fall back to the relief alone.
AI sight and fog link: untouched (Target.cpp and VolumetricFog.cpp have no diff against the base tree).
