CAUSE
Round 3 took the land's grass share and porosity only from the ground field (the per-vertex
average colour, continuous). That removed the seam, but on the town square the field is one
mixed colour, so every pixel got the same class: the same film everywhere, the same lamp streak
roughness everywhere, and in the SSR trace the same reflection weight (0.5 x wet) everywhere.
Result: one lacquered sheet with long clean vertical streaks. Round 2 looked varied only because
its per-pixel class was steep and binary (grass tufts 1, gravel 0), which is also what made the seam.

CHANGE (shader only, no C++ or binding change)
1. wetGroundGloss(xz), new, in the shared Puddles piece: two octaves of the puddles' own value
   noise (6 m and 1.9 m), smoothstep(0.36, 0.64). A pure function of world xz, so it has no edge
   at any square or triangle, and the forward pass and the SSR trace read the same value.
   Low = damp patch, high = glossy wet patch.
2. Forward pass (wetSurface), on the ground:
   - film *= mix(kWetDampFilm 0.25 (land) or kWetRoadDampFilm 0.6 (roads), 1, gloss)
   - rough = mix(max(rough, kWetDampRough 0.32), rough, gloss), so the lamp streaks break into
     pieces across damp patches instead of one long line.
3. SSR trace (land and roads): in damp patches the reflection weight drops to kSsrDampWeight 0.25
   (roads kSsrRoadDampWeight 0.6), roughness rises toward kSsrDampRough 0.8, the cone widens toward
   kSsrDampCone 0.4. Puddles stay full mirrors.
4. Puddles: wetPuddle's noise field gets + kPuddleGlossLift 0.1 x (gloss - 0.5), so water stands
   in the wet patches. Zero on average, so the puddle share holds.
5. Texture grain (the zero-mean detail idea, done so it cannot step): the land main sets
   g_wetGrain = wetGreenness(texel at max(lod, 0.5)) - wetGreenness(same texture at
   max(lod + 3, 3.5)), the same mips the cavity term uses. wetGreenness(c) = c.g - 0.5(c.r + c.b)
   is linear, so a coarse mip has the texels' mean lean and the grain is zero on average over
   any stretch of a texture. veg += clamp(grain x kWetVegGrain 12, -1, 1) x
   min(kWetVegGrainMax 0.45, veg, 1 - veg): the swing stays inside the field class's headroom,
   so it is never clipped and cannot shift the mean on a mottled texture against a flat one.
   It drives the film, roughness and darkening (grass tufts matte and less dark, gravel takes
   the film). The puddles keep the field's class alone (vegPud), so the trace's puddles match the
   forward pass pixel for pixel, as in round 3.
Why not "relative to the texture's own average class" as first suggested: on corner-blend
textures the texels are continuous across a square's edge while the two textures' averages
differ, so a detail relative to each texture's global mean steps at that edge. The local coarse
mip has no such jump, and the steep class functions are kept away from the per-pixel term.
