(a) The mist views were a white wall

Cause, with probes:
- probe_depth/depth_half.png colours the fog's view depth: the near grass is under 100 m, the rock
  outcrop 200 to 400 m, the tree line 300 to 500 m, the hill behind 500 to 700 m, the far end 742 m.
  The classic fog keeps the first 300 m or more crisp, which is why vanilla shows the rock.
- The game log gave the medium at setFog 0.3 under the 0.7 deck: weather 2.03e-3 /m before the
  2.8 boost (5.7e-3 with it), grey haze 1.31e-3, valley mist 1.09e-3, humid air 3.4e-4. With the
  boost a level line kept 0.40 at 150 m and 0.15 at 300 m (my mirror of the shader's medium): the
  rock was gone. kSetFogBoost alone at 1 (probe_taa/hud_boost1.png) still left the rock faint,
  because the grey haze and the mist layer stacked on top of setFog's own fog.
- probe_layers/layers_hud.png: volFog=0 brings the rock and tree line back, surfaces=0 and
  photoLight=0 do not. So it is the volume's medium, not the camera or the surfaces.

Change (VolumetricFog):
- kSetFogBoost 2.8 -> 0.75: by day setFog's own fog takes 0.75 of the classic extinction, as rain
  takes kRainFogShare 0.5.
- The grey haze no longer stacks on a day's setFog: its gain is cut by (1 - 0.7 wetAir), wetAir =
  max(rain, smoothstep(0, 0.3, fog) x (1 - night)). It was rain only.
- setFog's share of the valley mist weight: new kMistFogWeight 0.6 by day (was 1.2), kMistFogWeightNight
  1.2 at night.
- kNightFogBoost 1 -> 6.47 (2 x 2.8 / 0.75 - 1), so a foggy night keeps exactly round 3's weather
  fog (town_fog_night, the critics' best view, is unchanged by construction; not reshot).
Result (level line at the fog floor, outside / inside a mist layer): 150 m 0.76 / 0.74 (was
0.40), 300 m 0.56 / 0.49 (was 0.15), 742 m 0.19 / 0.12 (was 0.01). In the shots the near grass is
green again (optics_mist_sight bottom left (76,83,71), was grey), the rock and its texture show
through the mist, the tree line and the hill behind are legible, the far end fades.
Still open: the optics_mist_hud view is still much paler than vanilla at thumbnail size. Most of
that frame is far fill and air, and the air sits at about (210,213,221) under the camera, brighter
than vanilla's fog colour (178,178,171). fogDensity 0.75 on top (probe_taaclip/hud_density075.png)
helps a little more; the real lever is the air's brightness under the camera's exposure, which I
did not touch.

(b) Single green and black pixels over the hidden tree line: NOT fixed

What I proved it is not (each probe with a shader override or a knob, crops in each folder):
- not the overlay snapshot (probe_specks: the world before the overlay and the frame after it do
  not differ at the specks);
- not sky-classified MSAA edge pixels (probe_edge: taking the nearest geometry depth for sky pixels
  changes nothing; the specks sit on geometry);
- not NaN, negative depth or unfogged far pixels in the fog apply (probe_nan: none flagged);
- not the near-field light (probe_near), GTAO and the surface terms (surfaces=0), SSR, the crown
  canopy, the local tone (camLocal=0), bloom.
What changes them: taa=0 with sharpen=0 removes nearly all (probe_post/specks_post.png); a few faint
green pixels stay. Clamping the TAA history to the 3x3 RGB range of the current frame does not help
(probe_taaclip), so the dark or green value is already in the composite's output under the jittered
frame. Likely cause for the next fixer: with jitter, alpha-tested foliage samples change each frame
and a few pixels get a depth (sample 0 of the MSAA depth blit) that does not match their colour, so
they are fogged as another surface; TAA then holds them. Next probe: a min or max depth resolve in
PostResolve, or the fog apply reading the 3x3 depth range at foliage pixels. They are fewer and
fainter in the after shot because the tree line is less hidden.

(c) Grey-day skies were a near-white sheet

Cause: under a full deck the dome draws the painted deck at the rig's full cloud light; under the
camera it sat high in the curve's shoulder, so its relief was compressed to a spread of about 10
levels at (200,203,209). Nothing in the fog flattens the upper sky: in rain the apply leaves the sky
alone (wetSky) and the grey fill only acts below about 8 degrees.
Change (shadeSkyDome, under the camera, by day, grey deck under the weather veil): the deck's light
relative to its row's mean (the 12 row taps the shader already reads) is raised to the power
1 + kDeckRelief, and the deck is kDeckDark of its light, faded in from sin 0.12 to 0.3 of elevation
(7 to 17 degrees). The band the grey-sky horizon ring measures (3.8 to 7 degrees) is untouched, so
the far air, round 3's ridge fading and the halo fixes keep their colour.
Result: rain_day sky (188,191,199) spread 16.3 (was (196,200,208) 10.8; landed 22.0, vanilla 27.2);
overcast_heavy (191,193,199) spread 17.4 (was 10.4; landed 23.6). The cloud base reads at thumbnail
size, the ridges still fade in layers, no seam at the horizon. The rain day still reads lighter than
landed's dark deck; kDeckDark lower would go further.
