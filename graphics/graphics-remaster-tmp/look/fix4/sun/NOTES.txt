(a) THE SUN: A PALE DISC IN A FLAT CREAM PLATEAU
Cause, two parts.
1. The disc's colour. The disc is drawn at kSunDiscLevel (12, past white) in the colour of
   the sunlight through the air, about (1, 0.62, 0.3) at a low sun. The hue-keeping curve
   whitens light past white only by 1 - kChromaKeep * chroma^2 (0.61 for chroma 0.7). That
   gives (255, 237, 220) on paper; the shots measure (255, 238, 219). So the disc was a peach
   dot, never white.
2. The glow. The sky's roll-off has one cap everywhere (kSkyRolloffCap 2.4, about 0.85 of
   white on the curve). Round a low sun the aureole is far brighter than the cap, so it was
   pressed flat. Probe: the radial profile of lowsun_fp (960) round the sun is
   237,207,166 at 8 px, 237,206,164 at 12, 16 and 24 px, 235,206,165 at 32 px: a plateau at
   the cap's value, then the fall. Landed: white out to about 16 px, then a smooth fall.
Change.
- PhotoCamera.hpp/.cpp: toward the sun the roll-off's cap rises from kSkyRolloffCap to
  kSkySunCap (10, past white). The rise is SkySunGlow = 1 / (1 + 2 (1 - cos) / r^2), about
  1 / (1 + (angle / 1.2 deg)^2). New SkySunGlow, SkyRolloffCapToward, SkyRolloffToward
  (C++ mirror for the tests). The GLSL camSkyRolloff(lin, d) takes the direction and every
  caller passes it (the dome, the clouds, the aerial colour, the sky reflection).
- The disc's colour goes kSunDiscWhite (0.85) of the way to white from 4 degrees up, none at
  the horizon (a reddened sun at sunrise keeps its colour). A sensor clips every channel of
  the sun.
Result (960 lowsun_fp, from the sun's centre): 254,253,252 at 0 px, 253,245,234 at 8,
252,234,212 at 12, 248,227,200 at 16, 244,218,184 at 24, 238,210,171 at 32, 223,194,152 at
48. A white core, a warm halo, no plateau. Coastline_sunset: white disc, a small peach core
glow (254,228,203 from 8 to 16 px), then a smooth fall. Disc peak (255,255,254) in every
low-sun view. Bloom picks up the brighter sky round the sun on its own.

(c) THE OVAL DISC
Cause: the disc was drawn by its angle from the sun. A rectilinear projection stretches a
small circle near the frame's corners along the radius (ratio about 1 / cos of the angle
off axis), and the low sun sits near the top-left corner. Measured axis ratio of the disc:
1.23 (lowsun_fp 960), 1.26 (sinai_lowsun_fp 960), 1.27 (lowsun_fp 1080). So 1080 was oval
too, only less visible. Landed draws a screen-aligned sprite, so it is round.
Change (EngineGL33_LightingShaders.cpp camSunDisc): the offset to the sun in pixels is
solved per pixel from the view ray's change per pixel (dFdx, dFdy) in the sky's tangent
plane, times the angle of a pixel of the same solid angle (det^(1/4)). The disc is an
equal-area circle on the screen. Falls back to the angle when the derivatives vanish.
Result: axis ratio 1.02 (lowsun_fp 960), 1.01 (sinai_lowsun_fp 960), 1.00 (both at 1080).

(d) PINE_RAYS: FAINT SHAFTS
Under the camera the screen-space shafts are off. The shafts are the volumetric fog's
near-field pass (psFogNear): the wet air's forward lobe, marched with the sun's visibility.
Probes (pine_rays, shots_p4 to shots_p6):
- debug=8 (the air alone) shows clear shafts, so the beams exist; they are just faint in
  the frame.
- volGain=8 changes nothing (that knob is the old air pass's).
- Taking the pass's soft cap (0.45) before the lit share, or using the lit share linearly:
  no change (box std 24.2 -> 24.2 / 23.9). The cap is not what holds them.
- The beam term times 2.5 (shader override): clear shafts on the dark crowns at thumbnail
  size, the open sky round them unchanged.
So the beams are linear in kFogBeamGain and the gain was too low to read.
Change: VolumetricFog.hpp kFogBeamGain 14 -> 35. Result: shafts read at thumbnail size
(d_pine_rays_shafts.png); no wash over the sky.

(b) DAWN_VALLEY: THE CREAM HAZE OVER THE BAY (NOT FIXED)
Probe results (dawn_valley at 1080, the bay box x 60-98%, y 45-75%, luma std as contrast):
- fogDensity=0: the bay and the far shore are crisp (std 57.9; landed 53.8; ours 34.3).
  So it is the volumetric fog.
- fogDensity=0.5: almost the same as 1 (far shore 216 against 224).
- fogMist=0, fogSun=0, camAerial=0.5, volGain=8: no visible change.
- The humid ground air off (shader override): no change (std 34.3 -> 34.4).
- The ring not measuring the sky within 12 degrees of the sun (was 5): no change (33.2).
- camHaze=1: the sky goes blue and the haze grey-blue, the bay still veiled.
So the cream is the clear haze's in-scatter on the sun side. Its light is the horizon sky
in the sun's azimuth (the ring), which at a low sun is the brightest air in the frame (near
the sky's cap). Against a dark backlit shore even a transmittance of 0.6 leaves a bright
cream veil, so halving the density barely shows. Likely root fix for a later round: the air's
light toward a low sun should follow the phase at the ray's own angle to the sun; the ring
samples the sky 2 degrees or more above the land, closer to the sun than a downward ray over
the bay, so it brings the aureole's flank into the haze. Not done here: it needs the ring's
sample elevation in the injection and a check on every low-sun view. My changes do not make
it worse (bay box std 34.3 before and after).
