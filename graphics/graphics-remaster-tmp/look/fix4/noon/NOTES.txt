(a) NOON SKY WAS SLATE: CAUSE
Numbers are my numpy means, top box = rows 2..10%, x 30..70%.
- Probe 1 (probe1/, knobs on my base build, noon_fields and sinai_desert_noon):
  base (91,114,148); volFog=0 (89,112,145); camWb=0 (93,114,146); camHaze=1 (85,111,147);
  camSat=1.4 (81,114,156); camEv +0.5 (100,126,164). Fog, white balance and haze are not it.
- The overhaul's zenith has the SAME chromaticity as landed's: linear ratios (0.34,0.56,1) against
  landed's (0.36,0.59,1). That is also the real clear sky's chromaticity (zenith near x 0.25, y 0.27
  gives (0.33,0.61,1) in linear sRGB). The sky table is right.
- The difference is light and rendition. Landed's sky is drawn at the original's brightness, about
  2.8x the overhaul's linear light. The overhaul draws the sky at the physical scale, and over a
  bright plain the camera then exposed 0.63 EV under the average scene (kBrightKeep -0.45 times the
  meter's 0.84 EV, plus kCameraHighSunEv -0.25; readout ev=-0.35 in noon_fields). The hue-keeping
  curve keeps the linear ratios exactly. A real camera's per-channel film curve deepens a mid-tone
  blue; without that, a sky at a third of landed's light with the same ratios reads as slate.
- Probe 2 (probe2/, shader override in camSkyLook, chroma raised on the clear sky only): the sky
  reads as clear blue at thumbnail size, at the same luminance. With camEv +0.35 the ground and sky
  both lift. That proved the two parts of the fix.

(b) BRIGHT GROUND WAS DARK: CAUSE
The same exposure term. A bright plain metered 0.84 EV above the average scene (noon_fields mean
-2.16, Sinai -2.26) and kBrightKeep -0.45 took 0.38 EV off on top of the high sun's 0.25 EV.

THE CHANGE
1. Exposure (PhotoCamera.hpp): kBrightKeep -0.45 -> -0.05. Only scenes brighter than the average
   scene change (dark scenes use kDarkKeep). With kCameraHighSunEv a bright plain at noon is still
   about 0.3 EV under the average scene, which keeps the shade and texture. Readout:
   noon_fields ev -0.35 -> -0.01, sinai_desert_noon 0.03 -> 0.33, jeep_close_noon +0.2 EV.
   forest_edge_golden, woods_pine2_evening, town_noon, noon_vista, overcast_broken and town_night
   keep their exposure to 0.02 EV.
2. Clear-sky colour (EngineGL33_LightingShadersSky.cpp, camSkyLook, new camSkyDeepen; C++ mirror
   PhotoCamera.cpp DaySkyDeepen and DaySkyDeepWeight): the clear sky's chroma about its luminance,
   in linear light, is scaled by 1 + kCameraDaySkyDeep * w. Same luminance, same dominant hue (the
   chroma vector is scaled, not turned), no channel under kCameraDaySkyFloor of the luminance. The
   weight w: by day only (none under 8 degrees of sun, all from 25), above the horizon band (sine of
   the view elevation 0.03 to 0.4, so the horizon stays pale), out of the sun's glow (cosine to the
   sun 0.86 to 0.985), none under the weather's veil. It runs on the dome, the aerial-perspective
   colour and the sky the sea mirrors, all through camSkyLook. Clouds keep their own colour.
   I tried 0.8 first (shots_1); 0.65 keeps noon_fields clearly blue and stops noon_vista and
   town_noon from turning vivid azure.
No change to the sun core, the sky roll-off cap (the SUN fixer's area), the fog or the AI link.
