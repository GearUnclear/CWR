(b) The single green and black specks: FIXED

Cause, with probes:
- probe_1 (knobs, same build): the HUD view's specks go from about 40 green and 20 dark to 10 and 4 with taa=0
  and sharpen=0; sharpen=0 alone changes nothing. In the sight view TAA is already off (feat taa=off:
  optics-overlay) and the specks are there all the same, so TAA is not the cause, only an amplifier.
- probe_2 (psPostTaa override writing the raw current frame, no history): the black specks are on the same
  pixels, so they are in the composite's output.
- probe_3 (psFogApply override): both optics shots are overlay-snapshot frames (corner flag yellow), the
  early-out for an overlay's black is never taken (no magenta), and the speck pixels sit inside the clusters
  of near-black world pixels (cyan, max code under 0.04) in the tree line.
- That leaves the snapshot branch of the fog apply, where the overlay's darkening is laid back per channel as
  clamp(over / max(lin0, 1e-5)). Both the frame and the snapshot are RGBA8 resolves of the same RGBA8 MSAA
  frame, so where the overlay did not draw over == lin0; but a world channel at code 0 decodes to 0, giving a
  ratio 0 / 1e-5 = 0, and code 1 decodes to (1/255)^2.2 = 5e-6, under the 1e-5 floor, giving 0.5. The fogged
  pixel is multiplied by that per channel: all channels near black gives a black speck, R and B near black
  with G above gives a green speck. Dark pine needles hit code 0 or 1; under TAA the texture LOD bias picks
  sharper mips, so more texels are that dark (why taa=0 removed most). Round 4's probe flagged differences
  over 0.004 in linear light, far above these values, so it saw nothing.
Change: the ratio is (over + eps) / (lin0 + eps) with eps = 1e-7 (GLSL kSnapRatioEps, C++ kFogSnapRatioEps and
the mirror FogSnapRatio): a channel the overlay left alone keeps ratio 1 at any level, and a black mask over the
darkest world (code 1) still darkens it to 0.02.
Result: my counter over the tree line (isolated pixels off the 5x5 median) 39 green and 16 dark in round 4,
0 and 0 now in the HUD view; the sight view's green clusters are gone (sbs_b_*). One small purple dot at the
blurred sight edge in the sight view is NOT from this: it is on the same pixel in round 4 (open item).

(a) The light-mist views were a pale sheet with the rock nearly erased: IMPROVED

Cause:
- The exposure. The capture log gives ev 1.04 (HUD) and 0.85 (sight) with the meter's bright end (high) at
  -0.58 and -0.39: the highlight guard held the bright end at kHighlightLimit 1.3 of scene light, where the
  curve sits in its shoulder (about 214 of 255). Under a grey sky that bright end is the mist itself (or the
  deck), so the whole mist sat in the shoulder with its contrast pressed flat. The same guard held the grey
  decks of overcast_heavy (1.41) and rain_day (1.32) there.
- The medium. With the exposure fixed alone (shots_1) the air went 208 to 191 but the rock's spread stayed 11.8
  (vanilla 29): the mist was still too thick. A level line at the fog floor under the 0.7 deck had weather fog
  8.2e-4 /m (at kSetFogBoost 0.4), the clear haze 8.1e-4, the humid ground air 3.4e-4 and the valley mist
  5.5e-4: the clear summer haze stood as thick as setFog's own fog under it.
Change:
- PhotoCamera: by day the highlight limit moves from kHighlightLimit 1.3 toward a new kGreyHighlightLimit 0.8
  with the weather's veil (CameraWeatherVeil, the value the camera already uses for saturation and the time of
  day EV); at night (the bright end is a lamp) nothing changes. TargetExposureLog2 takes the veil as a new last
  argument (default 0); the camera stores it per frame (EngineGL33.hpp CameraState::veil).
- VolumetricFog: kSetFogBoost 0.75 -> 0.3, kMistFogWeight 0.6 -> 0.2 by day, and a new kSetFogHazeCut 0.5:
  the clear haze is cut by half under a day's setFog of 0.3 and over (setFog's mist is that humid air; rain
  keeps its haze, the night keeps its haze). kNightFogBoost follows the same rule as round 4,
  2 x 2.8 / kSetFogBoost - 1 = 17.67, so the foggy night keeps round 3's weather fog exactly (not reshot).
Result (HUD view, block means): air (208,211,219) -> (192,195,202), vanilla (179,180,172); the rock block
(x 1650-1900, y 700-950) mean 172 -> 123 (vanilla 104), spread 11.2 -> 13.4 (vanilla 29.2); near grass
(95,102,92) -> (65,71,61) (vanilla 79,90,60). ev 1.04 -> 0.18 (HUD), 0.85 -> 0.11 (sight). At thumbnail the
right-hand hills now stand as grey layers fading with distance and the tree line is a dark silhouette; in the
sight view the trunks, the crowns and the rock's texture read through the mist (sbs_a_optics_mist_sight.png).
Level line at the fog floor (my unit-test mirror at noon, grey deck): 300 m 0.67 (round 4 about 0.45),
500 m 0.51, the classic fog end 767 m 0.36.
Still short of the brief: the rock is legible but still far paler and greyer than vanilla's crisp rock (spread
13 against 29), the trees have lost their green, and the top of the frame is still a light grey sheet at
about 192. The original keeps its first 300 m nearly unfogged; this medium does not.
