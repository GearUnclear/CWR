(a) DAWN_VALLEY: THE CREAM VEIL OVER THE BAY
Cause. The volumetric fog's air light along a column is the horizon ring's colour for that
azimuth (psFogInject, g_air). The ring (psFogRing) measures the sky from 2 degrees above the
land, the darker half of six samples 0.6 to 5.6 degrees above that. Toward a low sun that is
the aureole's flank: in dawn_valley (sun 11.9 degrees up) the ring's samples under the sun are
4 to 9 degrees from it, while the haze over the bay (rays 3 to 8 degrees under the horizon) is
15 to 20 degrees from it. Every column in the sun's azimuth took the ring's colour whatever
its own angle to the sun, so the haze over the bay was lit as if it looked almost at the sun.
The haze is thick enough there to converge to that light (round 4: fogDensity 0.5 barely
changed it), so the bay and the far shore sank into a cream column under the sun.
Probe evidence (dawn_valley 1080, bay box x 60-98%, y 45-75%, luma std / mean):
- shots_p1 base: 32.8 / 153.8; fogDensity=0: 57.1 / 98.4. The veil is the volume's light.
- shots_p1 sunrise_ridge with fogDensity=0: the warm dome is unchanged, so (b) is NOT the
  fog (see below).
- Phase per column (the fix): 37.7 / 128.0; far shore 16.4 / 147.4 (was 13.8 / 172.1).
- shots_p2, the same build with the ring's elevation row ignored (every tap taken as
  measured at the horizon): bay mean 134.1, far shore 156.0. So the measured elevation is
  read and matters (the gain is the phase between the column and where the ring really
  looked).
Change.
- psFogRing writes a second row: r = the sine of the mean elevation of the samples it kept
  (the same darker half, or all of them under a grey sky), a = how sure, blended over frames
  like the colour. EngineGL33_PostFog.cpp: the ring targets are kFogRingBins x kFogRingRows (2).
- psFogInject, per ring tap: the tap's direction from its azimuth and that elevation; from the
  sky table's own two parts there (new fogSkyParts: r the Rayleigh and multiple scattering,
  m the haze's single scattering without its phase) the gain per channel is
  (r + m P(column)) / (r + m P(tap)), P the sky's own skyMiePhase, clamped to 0..kFogRingPhaseMax.
  A column at the ring's own direction keeps the ring's colour exactly (far ground still
  melts into the sky behind it); under the horizon toward a low sun the haze takes less of
  the sun's forward light; away from the sun the phase is flat and nothing changes. Only with
  the sky table on (litAtmo.z), faded out under a grey sky (fogWeather.z, the table is the
  clear sky's) and at night. The forward lobe (g_lobe) reads the corrected light.
- VolumetricFog.hpp/.cpp: kFogRingPhaseMax and the C++ mirror FogRingPhaseGain.
Result: the bay and the far shore read at thumbnail and full size (a_dawn_valley_bay.png),
the sun side keeps a warm glow in the sky and a lighter haze toward the horizon. Still
hazier than landed (bay std 37.7 against landed 53.3): what remains is the sky part (r) of the
air's light, which is physical for a backlit morning haze.

(b) SUNRISE_RIDGE: THE WARM DOME
Cause (not the fog: fogDensity=0 leaves it unchanged). The camera look of the clear sky
(camSkyLook, EngineGL33_LightingShadersSky.cpp) turns a low sun's sky toward the zenith's blue
"away from the sun's glow": none within 10 degrees of the sun, all from 30, by the plain
angle to the sun. That mask is a disc round the sun, so the golden colour filled a disc of
about 20 degrees radius: with the sun at 5 degrees it reached 20 to 25 degrees up, more than
half the frame's sky. Measured before (R-B warmth 20 px boxes from the glow's brightest point
at the horizon): 82 at 200 px up, 47 at 300 px up; 77 at 400 px to the side, 51 at 600 px.
A round dome.
Change. The mask's angle counts the height above the sun kLowSunGlowFlat (3) times and the
sky under the sun's height as at it (new camLowSunGlowCos; C++ mirror PhotoCamera
LowSunGlowCos). The warm glow is a band hugging the horizon round the sun's bearing,
brightest near the sun.
Result: warmth 41 at 200 px up, 1 at 300 px up; 74 at 400 px to the side, 49 at 600 px.
The band is about twice as wide as it is tall (b_sunrise_ridge_glow.png). The upper sky over
the sun is the zenith's blue at the same brightness (the mask moves hue only).
