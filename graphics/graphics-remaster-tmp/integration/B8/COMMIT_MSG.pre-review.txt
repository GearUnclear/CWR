graphics: sun beams, headlight cones, aerial haze and morning valley mist

What the player sees: with the sun low and in view, light shafts fall
through tree crowns, between cypress trunks and over roofs, and the
shadow of a tree line or a ridge cuts through the lit air. In setFog,
in rain and with the sun at the horizon the air is misty and the shafts
fan out; in clear air only narrow beams through gaps show and open
ground toward a low sun gets no glowing band. At night a vehicle's
headlights draw cones in the mist above the road. Distant ground fades
into the sky behind it through air that thins with height, so valleys
sit in haze below clear ridges and the far horizon has depth; at noon
in clear weather only the far haze changes (+0.3% horizon-band
brightness at noon_fields). At dawn (an eastern sun up to 15 degrees)
and in setFog, pink mist pools on valley floors and along shores while
ridges, plains and the open sea stay clear. Nothing changes within
150 m, and the night sky stays as it was.

How it works:
- One post stage, Air (EngineGL33_PostAir.cpp, its GLSL in the GL-free
  EngineGL33_PostAirShaders.cpp), after the occlusion and before the
  bloom. A half-resolution march on the linear depth writes two
  targets: sun beams (single scattering in a ground mist, 24 steps over
  the first 250 m, the sun's visibility from the shadow map, the
  horizon map seen from the point's height and the cloud field, phase
  0.85 HG(0.75) + 0.15 HG(-0.3)), headlight cones (up to four spotlights
  within 150 m at night, the shading's cone, forward scattering toward
  the eye), the haze's sun visibility (12 density-weighted samples
  beyond 150 m) and the valley mist's top and density where the ray
  ends. A depth-aware blur, the beams' depth-matched local mean, then a
  full-resolution apply: haze and mist in closed form, C' = C T + S,
  toward the sky table's fog colour (the colour the forward fog uses),
  then the beams added like light on a screen with the research's
  contrast (what stands above the mean is boosted, the smooth glow
  loses a share, never below zero) and its edge clamp for thin foliage.
  The march's start turns every frame with the TAA sequence and the TAA
  history averages it. The bloom and the composite read the frame with
  the air applied; with the stage off they read the resolved frame as
  before.
- The beams replace the legacy screen-space shafts while they run.
- With the haze on the classic fog keeps only its view-distance edge:
  its start moves out to 55% of its end in clear weather, through a
  per-fragment scale of the vertex fog factor in finishFragment and the
  water main (LitConstants 78 Haze, x = the scale minus 1, 0 = the
  classic fog; the last planned slot, Count 79). The apply puts the
  haze under that edge, so the mist dissolves into the sky toward the
  view distance instead of ending in a hard line at the horizon.
- The medium is pure, Graphics/Remaster/AirMedium.{hpp,cpp}: the
  constants, the weather and time rules (ComputeAirMedium: beam mist
  0.12 in clear air, 1.5 from setFog 0.1, more above, gain 4 and
  high-pass 0.85 in clear air, 2.5 and 0.6 in mist; the haze x4 at
  setFog 1, x2 in rain; the valley mist at dawn and in setFog, 40% of
  it under an overcast, only where the ring taps' mean ground stands 20
  to 60 m above the floor), the closed forms the shaders mirror, the
  valley floor, the classic fog's moved edge, and AirOpticalDepth for a
  line of sight, for AI sight later.
- The march pastes the remastered prelude's LitConstants block and its
  terrain and clouds piece (cloudSunVisibility), the apply the
  LitConstants block and the sky piece: those pieces are now
  kLitConstantsBlockGLSL and kRemasterTerrainCloudsGLSL in
  Poseidon::gl33lighting (the assembled bytes of every existing program
  are unchanged by the move); kPostShaders rows hold up to 7 pieces;
  the table test resolves names over both families and the lab's
  glsl_extract.py loads them together.

Switches and gates: airBeams, airHaze and valleyMist have landed
(11/26). The beams run with a low sun in view, mist, or headlights at
night; the haze always beyond 150 m; the valley mist with an eastern
sun from -4 to 15 degrees (gate shortened from 38, full to 5 degrees,
gone by 15) or setFog. The stage needs the post effects, the world
depth and no screen overlay; interiors, optics and night vision skip
it and the forward fog keeps its classic edge there (a frame later).
Sub-knob volGain under airBeams (-1 = from the weather). Debug view 6
shows the air's in-scatter alone. GPU section air.

From the prototypes: kept the volumetric march, phase, spotlight march,
depth-aware mean with gain, floored high-pass and edge clamp, and the
haze's closed-form height extinction (sigma 1.1e-4, scale height
350 m, chroma 0.85 / 1 / 1.2), valley mist (2.5e-3, 40 m over the
lowest of 15 taps, noise banks), its sun visibility and its light.
Rewritten: one stage instead of a composite rewrite and per-fragment
haze in every pixel shader; TAA as the accumulation; the sky table as
the limit colour (no backdrop blit); the forward fog's edge by a
fragment-side scale (no vertex shader change); the weather-driven
medium; the haze's air without its own sun lobe (its limit already
holds the glow toward the sun; the extra lobe lit far ground toward a
low sun above the sky behind it). Dropped: halos for lamps and fires
(brown sky), volCarve, the un-floored high-pass, CaptureSkyBackdrop,
the variant channel, the other haze variants, the research's own
temporal resolve; the precomputed mist floor was not needed (15 taps
once per half-resolution pixel where the mist runs).

Look check corrections: with the haze notes' morning (to 38 degrees)
and no valley test, lowsun_fp (08:00 in March, 15 degrees, clear) took
a mist veil over the plain and the hillside; with the research's
clear-air beam mist 0.3 and high-pass 0.6 it took a warm band along the
horizon toward the sun (+7% horizon-band brightness from the beams).
Now +2.3% with everything on, the M113's hull red/blue 1.220 to 1.218.

Measured cost (RTX 4060 Ti, 1080p, scale 1.0, 4x MSAA, single readings
of the smoothed air section with one or two other games on the
machine, no clock log): 0.51 to 0.97 ms with beams, 0.12 to 0.25 ms
haze and mist alone, 0.04 to 0.05 ms at noon and at night; the legacy
shafts it replaces took 0.27 to 0.44 ms. The plan budgets 2 to 4 ms on
a 3060 at dawn, dusk and in mist.

Known risks: in setFog the backlit crowns near the sun take a pale
veil (physical in mist, close to "flattened crowns"; volGain is the
lever); at 05:08 the far massif lies under pink mist that glows next
to the sun (the haze notes' "too washed out" risk); the 06:00 to 06:30
valley mist of the haze notes is gone with the shorter morning; the
sunrise views lose 3 to 7% of their red/blue balance where the warm
legacy shaft glow is gone and the mist is paler than the lit ground
(hulls unchanged); with TAA off thin foliage against the glow shows
speckles; half-resolution shafts beside trunks have blocky edges; a
smoke puff in front of far terrain takes that terrain's haze; the
beams and the legacy shafts swap at the beams' gate edge, not seen in
motion; AI sight ignores the medium (AirOpticalDepth is ready); no RTX
3060 measured, no quiet-machine timing.
