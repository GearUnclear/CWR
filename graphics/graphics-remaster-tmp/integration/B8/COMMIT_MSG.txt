graphics: sun beams, headlight cones, aerial haze and morning valley mist

What the player sees: with the sun low and in view, light shafts fall
through tree crowns, between cypress trunks and over roofs, and the
shadow of a tree line or a ridge cuts through the lit air. In setFog,
in rain and with the sun at the horizon the air is misty and the shafts
fan out; in clear air only narrow beams through gaps show and open
ground toward a low sun gets no glowing band. At night a vehicle's
headlights draw cones in the mist above the road. Distant ground fades
into the sky behind it through air that thins with height, so valleys
sit in haze below clear ridges, a ridge's shadow in the haze stays
grey and the far horizon has depth; at noon in clear weather only the
far haze changes (+0.3% horizon-band brightness at noon_fields). At
dawn (an eastern sun up to 10 degrees) and in setFog, pink mist pools
on valley floors and along shores while ridges, plains and the open
sea stay clear. Nothing changes within 150 m, and the night sky stays
as it was.

How it works:
- One post stage, Air (EngineGL33_PostAir.cpp, its GLSL in the GL-free
  EngineGL33_PostAirShaders.cpp), after the occlusion and before the
  bloom. A half-resolution march on the linear depth writes two
  targets: sun beams (single scattering in a ground mist, 24 steps over
  the first 250 m, the sun's visibility from the shadow map, the
  horizon map seen from the point's height and the cloud field, phase
  0.85 HG(0.75) + 0.15 HG(-0.3)) and the same scattering as if the
  whole ray saw the sun, headlight cones (up to four spotlights within
  150 m at night, the shading's cone, forward scattering toward the
  eye), the haze's sun visibility (12 density-weighted samples beyond
  150 m) and the valley mist's top and density where the ray ends. A
  depth-aware blur, the beams' depth-matched local mean, then a
  full-resolution apply: haze and mist in closed form, C' = C T + S,
  toward the sky table's fog colour (the colour the forward fog uses),
  then the beams added like light on a screen with the research's
  contrast (what stands above the mean is boosted, the smooth glow
  loses a share, never below zero) and its edge clamp for thin
  foliage. The contrast works on the share of each ray's scattering
  that sees the sun (BeamShaft), not on the scattering itself, so a
  ray that only runs longer through the mist, as over open ground
  toward the horizon, is not boosted into a band. Air that a ridge or
  a cloud shades keeps a dimmer zenith-coloured sky light (0.55 of the
  sky's luminance). The march's start turns every frame with the TAA
  sequence and the TAA history averages it. The bloom and the
  composite read the frame with the air applied; with the stage off
  they read the resolved frame as before.
- The beams replace the legacy screen-space shafts while they run.
- With the haze on the classic fog keeps only its view-distance edge:
  its start moves out to 55% of its end in clear weather, through a
  per-fragment scale of the vertex fog factor in finishFragment, which
  the sea's main ends in too (LitConstants 78 Haze, x = the scale minus 1, 0 = the
  classic fog; the last planned slot, Count 79). The apply puts the
  haze under that edge, so the mist dissolves into the sky toward the
  view distance instead of ending in a hard line at the horizon.
- The medium is pure, Graphics/Remaster/AirMedium.{hpp,cpp}: the
  constants, the weather and time rules (ComputeAirMedium: beam mist
  0.12 in clear air, 1.5 from setFog 0.1, more above, gain 4 and
  high-pass 0.85 in clear air, 2.5 and 0.6 in mist; the haze x4 at
  setFog 1, x2 in rain; the valley mist full from 1 to 6 degrees of an
  eastern sun and gone by 10, and in setFog, 40% of it under an
  overcast, only where the ring taps' mean ground stands 20 to 60 m
  above the floor), the beams' contrast, the closed forms the shaders
  mirror, the valley floor, the classic fog's moved edge, and
  AirOpticalDepth for a line of sight, for AI sight later.
- The march pastes the remastered prelude's LitConstants block and its
  terrain and clouds piece (cloudSunVisibility), the apply the
  LitConstants block and the sky piece: those pieces are now
  kLitConstantsBlockGLSL and kRemasterTerrainCloudsGLSL in
  Poseidon::gl33lighting (the assembled bytes of every existing program
  are unchanged by the move); kPostShaders rows hold up to 7 pieces;
  the table test resolves names over both families and the lab's
  glsl_extract.py loads them together. The march binds the horizon and
  height maps whenever the shading reads them (terrain shadows or
  terrain occlusion on).

Switches and gates: airBeams, airHaze and valleyMist have landed

From the prototypes: kept the volumetric march, phase, spotlight march,
depth-aware mean with gain, floored high-pass and edge clamp, and the
haze's closed-form height extinction (sigma 1.1e-4, scale height
350 m, chroma 0.85 / 1 / 1.2), valley mist (2.5e-3, 40 m over the
lowest of 15 taps, noise banks), its sun visibility and its light.
Rewritten: one stage instead of a composite rewrite and per-fragment
haze in every pixel shader; TAA as the accumulation; the sky table as
the limit colour (no backdrop blit); the forward fog's edge by a
fragment-side scale (no vertex shader change); the weather-driven
medium; the beam contrast on the lit share; the haze's air without its
own sun lobe (its limit already holds the glow toward the sun; the
extra lobe lit far ground toward a low sun above the sky behind it).
Dropped: halos for lamps and fires (brown sky), volCarve, the
un-floored high-pass, CaptureSkyBackdrop, the variant channel, the
other haze variants, the research's own temporal resolve; the
precomputed mist floor was not needed (15 taps once per
half-resolution pixel where the mist runs).

Measured cost (RTX 4060 Ti, 1080p, scale 1.0, 4x MSAA, single readings
of the smoothed air section with one other game on the machine, no
clock log): 0.62 to 1.05 ms with beams, 0.16 to 0.28 ms haze and mist
alone, 0.04 to 0.05 ms at noon and at night; the legacy shafts it
replaces took 0.27 to 0.43 ms in the same views. The plan budgets 2 to
4 ms on a 3060 at dawn, dusk and in mist.

Known risks: in setFog the backlit crowns near the sun take a pale
veil (physical in mist, close to "flattened crowns"; volGain is the
lever); at sunrise the far massif lies under pink mist that glows next
to the sun (the haze notes' "too washed out" risk); in clear air with
a low sun in view the legacy shafts' glow is gone and the thin beams
give back only part of it (lowsun_fp -1.2 / -2.1 / -0.1% per band, the
M113's backlit side -2.1% with its hue kept; pine_eve's frame R/B
0.951 to 0.939; keeping the legacy shafts in clear air is the lever);
the 06:00 to 06:30 valley mist of the haze notes is gone with the
shorter morning; the dawn views lose 3 to 7% of their red/blue balance
where the warm legacy shaft glow is gone and the mist is paler than the
lit ground; with TAA off thin foliage against the glow shows speckles;
half-resolution shafts beside trunks have blocky edges; a smoke puff
in front of far terrain takes that terrain's haze; the beams and the
legacy shafts swap at the beams' gate edge, not seen in motion; AI
sight ignores the medium (AirOpticalDepth is ready); no RTX 3060
measured, no quiet-machine timing.
