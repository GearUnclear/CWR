graphics: fires, flares and shell bursts light the men and cast shadows

What the player sees: at night a camp fire, a burning wreck, a shell
burst, a flare, a muzzle flash, a tracer and a missile motor now light
the ground and the men around them as real light sources, warm and
bright near the source and falling off with distance, and the four
strongest throw long radial shadows from the men and vehicles. A shell
burst whitens the frame for as long as Classic's does: at eye height the
first flash frame reads 57.0 against Classic's 55.5 and the frame 0.13 s
later 56.9 against 57.8; from 5 m up both frames read brighter than
Classic (117.1 and 115.6 against 72.7 and 79.6). The flash is white-hot
and cools to a fire's warmth, so the ground it lights reads warm white
and sand, not khaki (red over blue of the lit ground 1.22 to 1.36). Smoke
and dust round a burst glow from inside, and the air round a flash takes
a faint glow. A burning wreck's black smoke glows at its foot and stands
dark against the night sky above it, as in Classic, with a small warm
halo over the embers; a camp fire's pale smoke is lit higher up. By day
the same lights keep a fifth of their strength (bursts) or 12% (fires),
so a noon shell still marks the crater; the shadows run only at night. A
paused game holds every light where it was.

How it works: Graphics/Rendering/Lighting/CombatLights (pure) keeps the
combat lights of the world in one set, ticked by the simulation step in
World::Simulate (so a paused frame does not age them). Bursts (muzzle,
cannon, explosion) are spawned and forgotten; continuous lights (wreck
embers, camp fires, tracers, flares, missile motors) are owned by their
object through a CombatLightHandle. The sources are the existing
gameplay hooks: WeaponLightSource and WeaponFireSource (the firing
vehicles and soldiers now pass the round's hit, which sets the muzzle
strength), SmokeSourceVehicle (the fuel burst and the wreck's embers),
Explosion (a burst sized by the explosion), ShotBullet (tracers),
Missile (the motor while it thrusts), IlluminatingShell (flares) and
Fireplace. Each kind has its envelope (EvaluateCombatLight: a 30 ms
muzzle; an explosion whose white-hot flash holds 0.15 s
(kExplosionFlashHold), then falls in 50 ms while a flicker fades in and
the colour cools to the fire's; a fire that breathes on two slow sines
and smooth noise, never a new brightness every frame) and a ball
falloff E = I * window / (1 + d^2 / r0^2) that ends at the light's
range (at most 120 m, a flare 400 m). The fire's colour is
(1.0, 0.70, 0.45); burst colours stay at or under its red over blue.

The lights enter A6's tiled list as up to 12 HDR entries at the head of
the frame's list, strongest at the camera first (MergeCombatFrameLights,
packed as kind 2 with the window range), and the forward shader shades
them in linear light outside the classic clamp (combatLights), with a
flash highlight on glossy materials but not on terrain. While they are
on, the classic light of the same source lights nothing: the tiled list
and the smoke skip the Combat and Fire kinds at selection (the classic
light stays for its glow sprite and for the switch off). So that no
steady source goes dark when more than 12 lights are in view, the list
keeps every fire, flare and missile motor first and cuts the weakest
bursts and tracers (KeepCombatLights). Smoke and dust particles take the
combat lights in Object::DrawDecal; a fire's light reaches a particle of
its own column through the smoke below it (CombatSmokeTransmittance:
exp(-(d - r0) / reach), the reach 1.5 core radii for black soot and
longer by 14 core radii times the square of the particle's albedo, the
luminance of its own colour), so a wreck's black column glows at its
base only. The shadows: up to four lights are picked for the four
channels of a half-resolution RGBA8 mask, with hysteresis (a light keeps
its channel unless another is 1.5 times stronger for 0.3 s; tracers and
motors cast none). At the start of each world pass EngineGL33_PostCombat
traces from each mask texel toward each shadowed light through the
previous frame's half-res linear depth (24 steps over at most 25 m),
then blurs the mask across and down with a depth-aware 7-tap filter.
The forward shader maps its fragment into last frame's screen through
three rows in the FrameLights block (CombatMaskRows) and takes one
bilinear tap for all four lights. The flash exposure: explosions and
cannon at the camera open the exposure in proportion to their
irradiance there, at night only, at most 4x, held with a 0.12 s decay.
The composite adds the in-scatter of up to 8 lights (an analytic
integral along each pixel's ray through the light's smoke ball; a
fire's is a 1.7 m halo at a quarter of a flash's share). Debug view 7
("Combat shadow mask") shows the mask.

The switch and its gates: combatLights (Combat light, Night and combat)
and combatShadows (Combat light shadows), both landed (11/26), with the
sub-knobs combatFlash, combatFire, combatSmoke and combatScatter (0 to
3, default 1). combatLights is on with the light list and a combat light
within 60 m of the camera (its range included); without the light list
it is off and the classic lights light the frame as before.
combatShadows also needs a night above 0.3, the post effects (the mask
reads the post pass's linear depth, which LinearDepth now runs for) and
the world in view. The simulation does not depend on either switch, so
off and on shoot the same paused frame. GPU sections: the mask pass
shows as "combatmask", the shading in "world".

Kept, rewritten and dropped from the research prototype: kept the
envelopes' shapes, the ball falloff and its window, the day scaling, the
gameplay hooks, the analytic in-scatter, smoke lit from inside, the
glossy flash and the night-eye lift. Rewritten: the lights are A6's
Combat and Fire kinds at the head of the tiled list instead of an HDR
flag through the 8 per-draw slots; the per-fragment trace through last
frame's depth became the half-res mask pass with hysteresis and blur;
strengths come from the round's hit and the explosion size, not a
table; the explosion's flash holds 0.15 s instead of falling from its
birth; the fire, fireball, muzzle and cannon colours are less saturated
(the research's fireball cooled to (1.0, 0.40, 0.11)); a fire lights
its own smoke only as far as the smoke lets the light through, and its
lit air is a small halo. Dropped: LitConstants slots 59 to 64 (the rows
ride in the FrameLights block; slot 72 is not used either), unit 6 (the
mask is on unit 14), the 2x2 ordered jitter, the combatWreck knob, and
the prototype's own light flags in Lights.hpp. Corrections to plan
section 5 B4 are recorded in the plan.

The critic list: the seam down the screen centre was the terrain, not
the mask: the capture camera stood on the segment line x = 7600, one
segment's material is specular and the other not, and the low flash's
glint drew the line. Terrain takes no flash glint now; the centre
columns of the final shots show no step above the texture's own, and
the mask mapping is tested at the centre and edges for even and odd
frame sizes. Shadow flipping between two fires: the hysteresis (no flip
over 600 frames of two flickering fires in the unit test). The flash
frame: a probe of the capture showed a shell's burst is first drawn at
age 1/30 s and the second flash shot at 0.13 s, where Classic still
whitens the frame; the research's envelope had fallen there and read 24

lower third of combat_fires and 1.38 in the timing rounds (the first B4
build, with the fire at (1.0, 0.66, 0.39): 1.43 and 1.44).

Measured cost (RTX 4060 Ti, 1920x1080, scale 1.0, 4x MSAA; three
interleaved rounds, GPU clock not logged): the camp fire and burning
wreck view: mask 0.10 to 0.11 ms, frame on minus off -0.25 to +0.03 ms
(noise); a shell burst paused at eye height: mask 0.20 ms, frame -0.01
to +0.23 ms (minimum frame +0.16 to +0.33); that reading is the first
build's, with only this lane's game in a process list during the run.
After the review fixes, with three other lanes' games running, the mask
read 0.09 to 0.18 ms and 0.12 to 0.15 ms and the frame times were too
noisy to read; the fixes add a square root and an exponential per smoke
particle per fire on the CPU and nothing on the GPU. The plan allows 1
to 3 ms in fights. By day the mask gate is off ("day (night below
0.3)"); with no combat light near, both gates are off and sunrise_ridge
and road_night draw the same frame (frame means 85.8 and 29.3 off and
on).

Known risks: the burst's tail is longer and warmer than Classic's
(0.27 s after a burst at eye height 32.4 against 21.5, from above 62.4
against 33.3); a noon shell adds 8.4 levels to the frame mean on its
first frame, with a pale orange ring round the crater and a faint glow
ball; a flare low over the ground blows the ground out white beside it
(75.4 against Classic's 50.0). The brightest part of the camp fire's
pool still reads red over blue 1.46 (rows 560 to 700, columns 150 to
700 of combat_fires, pixels above 25; the first build 1.53, off 1.09),
over the plan's 1.4, while the lit lower third reads 1.35: one more step
toward white would bring it under, for the owner to decide. A camp
fire's smoke reads whiter and less tan than Classic's above the flames
(as in the first build). Past 12 combat lights in view, the cut bursts
and tracers light nothing that frame (their classic lights stay
skipped; tracers have none), and with more than 12 fires, flares and
motors in view the weakest of those go dark. A shell burst near the
wreck lights its black column for a moment (0.27 s after a burst at eye
height the plume region reads 7.6 against 3.1 off, the sky 10.1). The
shadows lag moving men by a frame (the mask uses last frame's depth),
and the mask keeps no history of its own: TAA averages its noise, and
the far ground at grazing angles shows a hatch in debug view 7. Combat
lights take up to 12 of a tile's 16 entries, so a busy tile can cut a
classic light. A muzzle flash shows in one captured frame of four (the
30 ms burst falls in one 1/30 s step), as intended. The explosion
cloudlets are yellow with the switch off too (not this change). Not
tested: tank cannon, interiors, MP, rain, smoke_eden and
smoke_sinai_lobo, a moving capture, an RTX 3060.
