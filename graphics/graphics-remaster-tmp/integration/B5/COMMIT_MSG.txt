graphics: the sea gets wave relief and a broken, glittering sun path

What the player sees: the sea is no longer a smooth sheet under a blurred
sheen. Rows of waves run to the horizon, the low sun lays a narrow path of
glints across them instead of a soft copper column, crests catch the sky
and troughs show the water, and the waves move with the sea texture's own
animation without popping every 0.14 s. At noon the sea keeps the
original's green-grey instead of turning blue-grey: over the water of
sea_noon the hue is 126.1 against the original's 133.7 degrees (the base
was 209.8) and the mean 150.1 against 143.8 (the base 126.2); at mid-sun
(16:30, sun ahead) 72.2 against 76.4 degrees and 164.3 against 169.7.
Wave detail (sd of a 9x9 high-pass over the water) rises from 2.0 to 20.7
at the sunset hero, 3.8 to 8.2 at noon and 2.7 to 9.2 at mid-sun. The
beach, the sky and Classic are unchanged.

How it works: the sea is the landscape's own faces, which carry the
specular layer alone and drew through the detail shader like the terrain;
the face spec has no free bit for an IsWater-style flag (RenderFlags.hpp
asserts that its three categories cover all 32), so the route is a draw
scope. Landscape::DrawWater opens Engine::BeginSea around its sea draws
and EndSea after them. With the switch on and gated on in the remastered
world pass, the backend (EngineGL33_Sea.cpp) sets
BuildContext::remasteredSea, BuildRenderPassDescriptor routes an opaque 3D
face with the specular layer alone to ShaderFamily::Water (detail texgen,
specular_dx on unit 1), and s_psWaterRemasterMain draws it with the
research's variant C: the sea texture's brightness is the wave height, the
texture, a swell copy at 0.27x frequency rotated 37 degrees and the
specular_dx ripples give the normal (the texture-to-world mapping solved
per pixel from screen derivatives, the step matched to the filter's
footprint, faded from 400 m to 3 km), the lit texture sits under the sky
reflected by Fresnel on that normal with a low mean share and the waves'
share at full weight, and the sun lays a broad lobe and a sparkle that
widens with the footprint. The sky is the A7 table's in the reflected
direction while the sky table is on (skyReflection in the sky piece), else
the classic fog colour with its sun glow, the sky the player then sees.
The original's noon green-yellow is its per-vertex sheen in the yellow
classic sun colour, so the material's broad lobe keeps that hue
(SeaSheenLight: the rig sun's luminance, the original sun's display
chroma) and, between 15 and 50 degrees of sun, grows from 0.35 to 2.4 and
moves to the flat normal (on the wave normal it split into yellow facets
and blue troughs); the mean sky share halves there and the reflected sky
takes 0.6 of the fog colour; the far sea's mean sky share fades to half
between 1 and 3 km. The animation: AnimateTexture switches frames at
floor(phase * 7); SeaAnimationBlend spreads the frames evenly over the
phase instead, binds the bound frame's neighbour on unit 11 (with unit 0's
sampler object) and mixes it in, continuously in the phase, dividing out
the relief a mix loses (consecutive frames' gradients correlate 0.65 to
0.82; 0.75 in the shader). Graphics/Remaster/SeaWaves holds the math, pure.

Switch and gates: seaWaves (landed, step B5), gated per sea fragment: on by
day and night in the remastered world pass, off in Classic, night vision,
portraits and 3D previews. Sub-knobs seaRelief, seaSky and seaSheen
(multipliers, 1). LitConstants slots 74 (Water0: relief, the next frame's
share, mean sky weight, sky tint) and 75 (Water1: sheen light, the sheen's
flat-normal share) land inside B11's padding, which splits into
litPlanned[6] (68 to 73) and litPlanned2 (76) around them (Count stays
78); unit 11 (kSeaNextFrameUnit, kLitSamplers row seaNext) holds a white
stand-in outside the sea's draws. Off (seaWaves=0, Classic) never opens the scope:
the sea draws through the detail program as before.

From the prototype: kept the wave normal and its three layers, the Fresnel
sky with a low mean and full wave-driven share, the broad and tight lobes,
the 400 m to 3 km fade, the anisotropy guard and the Toksvig-style
sparkle. Rewritten: the sea detection by material specular (isSea()) is an
explicit route; the sky from the A7 table; the local-light clamp is F9's
1.0 through the shared lights piece; the sheen in the original sun's hue
and flat at a high sun; the cross-fade; the constants in two slots and
three sub-knobs; the shader ends in finishFragment like the detail program.
Dropped: variants A and B, the old water main's constant bump normal (the
detail texture read as one), the waterBump texture nothing sampled
(misc\waveBumpMap.paa is not in the Remaster data) with
GetWaterBumpMap(); the README's Water row is rewritten.

Known risks: the hero view's eye 3 m above sea level puts the sea from 2 m
out in frame, which the research never shot, and there the relief reads
as large smooth swells; the noon near band is beige-grey (159, 160, 150)
where the original's is yellow-green-grey (154, 159, 146), and the hue of
so grey a sea swings 30 to 40 degrees for 0.2 of seaSheen; the 7-frame
ping-pong animation now moves the normals (about 10 times off's
frame-to-frame change in the water) and has no travelling waves; at night
on is 5 levels darker than off with twice the detail, and there is no moon
glint; the cost is about five times the plan's estimate; Eden, Sinai with
@LoBo, rain, overcast and the shoreline are unverified; a model face that
carries IsWater (none known) now draws with this model.
