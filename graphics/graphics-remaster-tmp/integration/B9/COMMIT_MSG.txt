graphics: every tree casts a long golden-hour shadow at any distance

What the player sees: at a low sun every tree, bush, forest block and
building on the island casts its long shadow beyond the reach of the sun
shadow maps too (about 200 m at a 2 km view distance), so whole hillsides
of trees stand on their shadows at golden hour instead of floating, and
distant groves get a lit and a shaded side. On the Malden hill at 17:30
(sun 9 deg) the far groves gain shadow streaks running away from the sun
and shade between their trees; through the telephoto onto the wooded
slope every tree has its long shadow. Where the canopy shades, the ground
falls to 0.80 to 0.83 of its lit value (display luma; R 0.72 to 0.77,
B 0.92 to 0.93): shade, not black, and cooler than the lit grass because
the shade is the sky fill. The ground band of the hero view loses 0.5%
of its mean brightness, the telephoto 1.8%; the sky band is unchanged and
the horizon band moves 0.14% at most. The near range, where B1's shadow
maps draw every tree, is unchanged, and so are noon, overcast and the
night (the gate holds the feature off there).

How it works:
- The canopy (Graphics/Remaster/Canopy): every Primary landscape object
  whose map type shades the ground at a distance (tree 0.8, small tree
  0.65, bush 0.5, forest block 0.95, buildings, churches, towers,
  bunkers and rocks 1.0; 84,395 of 98,198 objects on Abel) is drawn
  top-down at 6.25 m a texel (2048x2048 on Abel, 4x4 samples a texel):
  crowns as ellipses at 0.85 of their box, forest blocks and buildings
  as the convex hull of their geometry level. A texel stores the absolute
  top (its own ground plus the tallest covering object's height above the
  ground at that object's centre, so a forest block on a slope follows
  the slope and one fetch a step holds the terrain too) and the occluder
  density, 1 - the product of each object's (1 - opacity * coverage), as
  RG16. Each mip level keeps the mean ground plus the tallest covered
  child's height, and the mean density, so a far occluder blurs into a
  wider, lighter penumbra. The objects are gathered on the main thread
  at island load (18 to 30 ms on Abel) and rasterised on the terrain
  horizon map's worker (332 to 505 ms); the build counts as pending for
  triSettleScene. A destroyed object (Object::SetDestroyed of a Primary
  object) takes its footprint out and only the texels it covered are
  rasterised again, at every level (CanopyRaster::Remove, uploaded with
  glTexSubImage2D).
- The sun-visibility map (EngineGL33_Canopy.cpp, psCanopySun in
  EngineGL33_PostCanopyShaders.cpp): a world-space RGBA8 map around the
  camera, 2048 texels a side at the view distance / 900 per texel
  (1.5625 to 3.125 m), filled by one fullscreen pass that cone-traces the
  canopy toward the sun from every texel (step max(texel, 0.15 t), the
  mip level following the step, softness over a 0.8 deg cone, a partly
  covered texel crossed with the probability sqrt(density), up to 45 m /
  tan(sun) or 900 m). A texel stores the ground's visibility, the
  visibility of a raised receiver 1.5 m up that skips its first 6 m (its
  own crown), and the envelope of the heights above the ground from which
  raised receivers (crowns, walls, roofs) leave a neighbour's shadow,
  with its softness; a thin sample sinks out of that envelope smoothly.
  The map is rebuilt before the world only when the sun has moved 0.05
  deg (about every 12 s at the normal clock), the camera has left the
  middle eighth of the map, the view distance changed the texel, or the
  canopy changed. It is world-locked: no jitter, no history, nothing to
  crawl, and a cost that does not grow with the screen.
- The read (canopySunVisibility, the TerrainClouds piece of the
  remastered prelude): one bilinear fetch per fragment, multiplied into
  the sun visibility after the terrain lookup, weighted in over the
  shadow maps' far fade (without shadow maps from 100 to 150 m, so the
  near range never gets canopy slabs) and faded out at the map's edge;
  a receiver between 0.5 and 1.5 m above the ground blends the ground
  and the raised reads.
- LitConstants slot 76 (Canopy: strength, the map's origin, 1 / its
  extent) lands as planned (on the lane the padding litPlanned shrank
  to 68 to 75; on the merged tree litCanopy replaces B5's litPlanned2).
  Unit 10 holds the canopy (sampled only by the build pass) and unit 15
  the map (kLitSamplers row canopySun), both with 1x1 stand-ins from
  init on; the build binds the stand-in on unit 15 while it draws into
  the map. GPU marks CanopyBegin and CanopyEnd, section canopy (frames
  that rebuild; the build runs at the frame's first upload of the lit
  constants, in the remastered sky's hook before the sky section, or
  without the sky pass just inside the world section, which then counts
  it too). triCanopyCheck <texels> reads the map back and compares
  it with its C++ mirror (CanopySunAt, CanopyReceiverVisibility).

Known risks: it only takes light away (no bounce), and the shade is the
cool sky fill (B/R in the shade 0.63 to 0.68 against 0.52 to 0.56 lit),
the research's "cool shade on pink rock". The canopy is a column from the
ground to the top: the ground under a crown is shaded at any sun (no
crown base height, no trunk term), and the far shadows are 6 to 12 m wide
soft streaks rather than tree shapes. A raised receiver deeper than 6 m
inside a wide crown or roof reads its own far side as shade. Its hand-over
to the shadow maps at their far fade swaps tree-shaped shadows for these
streaks over the fade. A repaired object, or a mission restarted on the
same island, keeps a destroyed object out of the canopy. The world-space
map covers 3.2 to 6.4 km around the camera and fades out at its edge.
Canopy shadows on far vehicles and men do not exist (only landscape
objects cast). The cost is not measured on a quiet machine or on an RTX
3060.
