graphics: calm water and wet streets reflect what is on screen

What the player sees: a calm bay mirrors the boat under itself and the
hill behind it, broken up by the sea's own waves and by small ripples
that move with the sea clock. In the rain, roads and bare ground turn
dark and wet and take a soft, streaked reflection of the vehicles,
houses and sky; level hollows hold puddles that mirror the houses and
lamp posts sharply; grass only darkens a little. Nothing changes where
nothing is reflected: the open sunset sea keeps its colour (its mean
over the water is the same to 0.1 level with the switch on and off),
so there is no pale lilac wash, and a dry noon field is untouched (the
gate holds the trace off). Far ground that the fog has swallowed stays
the fog colour, so fogged trees and hills look as they did. A vehicle's
reflection in a wet road is whole: its windows reflect as the window
looks, not as a pale box of sky. At the bay hero the boat's reflection
goes from (105, 116, 123) to (45, 48, 51) and the band where the hill
is mirrored from (111, 123, 129) to (110, 108, 109); the wet ground's
mean changes by -2 to -15 levels with puddles and sheen in it (the day
street's road -2.3, the dusk street's road -14.9, the rain field -3.7).

How it works: every world draw already writes the stencil (REPLACE 0)
for Classic's projected-shadow exclusion, which counts in the low bits.
While the switch is on, the remastered world pass writes a surface tag
in the top bits instead (ApplyPassState asks SsrStencilForDraw of
Graphics/Remaster/ScreenReflections, from the draw's descriptor): bits
7 and 6 the surface (terrain 0x40 for the land's detail and grass
layers, water 0x80 for the sea's water program, road 0xC0 for
on-surface draws), bit 5 a cover flag for blended draws that write
depth (a vehicle's windows, glass, signs, blended cards; alpha-tested
bushes and trees are opaque and clear the tag). An opaque draw clears
the tag, a draw without a depth write keeps it (it writes the low five
bits only), and the projected shadows compare the low five bits
(GLDepthStencilState StencilTag on every depth-stencil bundle; stencil
clears write every bit first). Two post stages run before bloom, so
reflected highlights bloom: TagReadout reads the tags out of the
resolved stencil into an R8 texture and measures the screen share of
water and wettable ground on a 32 x 18 grid, read back through a ring
of three pixel-pack buffers a few frames later (never waited on) for
the frame gate; Ssr blits the resolved frame into a mip chain (the
cone's blur source), traces one ray per half-resolution texel over
A3's half-resolution linear depth (48 steps at most, a
perspective-correct overlap test with a distance-scaled thickness, a
5-step refine; a ray that slips behind a surface within 3 m of its
start sees that surface's unlit side; sky found after a stretch behind
a surface is trusted less), gathers rough reflections sideways over the
width of each ray's cone at what it reflects (a tap per half-resolution
texel, up to 24 a side, same surface, similar depth, taps of a narrower
spread weighted down so puddle edges stay sharp: no hard-edged box
under a vehicle), and lays the result on the full-resolution frame with
a depth- and surface-aware upsample into its own target, which the
air pass lays its haze, mist and beams over and the shafts read; the
bloom and the composite read the air pass's output when it ran, else
that target. The reflection fades with the
world's own distance fog: BeginPass copies the world pass's fog start
and range, and the trace uses the world vertex shader's formula, so a
fully fogged pixel is left as the fog colour. A ray from the ground
ignores its own plane (a candidate within 0.03 m + 0.004 m per metre of
depth of the start's plane): the half-resolution depth's nearest
samples could put a road just in front of a grazing ray, a regular
pattern of dark dots on wet roads. A ray that meets a covered panel
goes on and lays the panel over what it finds beyond, as much as the
panel hides: fully when the first uncovered texel after it stands in
front of the ray (the panel is set in something solid, a window in a
canvas: the ray would meet the vehicle's inside) or the march ends on
it, otherwise by how far the panel's colour is from that texel, the
background next to it, so a card's transparent parts let the ray
through (no box under a bush). Reflections are read along the screen
vertical (a streak toward the viewer) with taps no farther apart than
the mip level's footprint, so no ladder of copies on water.
On water the trace recomputes the sea program's sky in the reflected
direction (skyReflection with its fog tint, the mean sky weight and its
far fade), estimates that sky's share of the pixel's light from the
blurred sea, and where a ray meets something swaps that share for the
reflection per channel: lin * ((1 - x) + x R / S), x = the share times
ssrWater, capped at 0.95. Rays that see sky or leave the frame leave
the sea as the sea program drew it. Ripples (four octaves of drifting
noise) move the mirror image by re-projecting the rippled direction at
the traced distance, and the slope finer than a pixel stretches it
vertically. On the ground: Fresnel with a roughness cap, wet roads and
bare ground darken (0.8) and reflect with a 0.10 and 0.22 rad cone,
puddles (footprint-filtered value noise, level ground only, roads at
0.61, bare ground at 0.67, none on grass, faded out from 150 to 400 m)
are dark mirrors with rain rings; only a ray going up falls back to
the sky when the reflection is unknown, and that sky is the frame's
own open sky above where it points, else the sky table's. Under TAA
the march start turns every frame and A5's history averages it.

Switch and gates: ssr (landed, step B6, GPU sections tags and ssr).
Gated on while water or wet ground covers more than 1% of the screen
(the ground counts only while it is wet: ssrWet when it forces a
value, else the rain density, until B7's wetness state); a share older
than 30 frames counts as not measured and leaves the gate on; while
the gate is off the readout runs every 8th frame only. Off in Classic,
night vision, portraits and 3D previews (no tags), in vehicle
interiors (no world depth) and under optics (the trace). Water
reflections need the sea program (seaWaves on and gated on). Sub-knobs
ssrWater 1.5 (the share of the sea's sky light the reflection takes,
as a multiple of the estimate) and ssrWet -1 (the weather; 0 to 1
forces it). Debug views 9 (the tags) and 10 (the reflection alone).
No LitConstants slot and no world texture unit: the stages use
kPostUnits and the sky table on unit 8. Off (ssr=0) writes REPLACE 0
into every stencil bit and plans the post stages of before.

From the prototype: kept the stencil tags in the top bits with the
shadow test on the low bits, the readout, the mip cone, the march and
its miss handling (the hidden side under a vehicle, the fallback to
sky only for rays going up), water as one mirror trace with ripple
re-projection and vertical stretch, the footprint-filtered puddles on
level roads and bare ground and none on grass, the rough Fresnel.
Rewritten: the tag table (surface plus a cover bit instead of vehicle
and glossy classes) from the draw descriptor, with no global class
flag and no extra FlushQueues; the trace at half resolution with an
upsample; frame edges fade the reflection over 6% instead of the
edge-hit rules; the bush-card boxes are the covered panels' opacity
rule (the plan's "treat low-coverage hits as misses", with the
coverage read from the frame: no blended bush card is in the Abel or
Eden views shot here, the research's clutter bush is alpha-tested in
this build, so that case rests on the tests); the water composite
replaces the sea's sky share instead of blending over the frame (the
pale sunset sea); the ripples at 0.35 of the research's slopes (its
calm setting: full slopes smeared the hero hill into a brown band);
the rough blur, the frame-sky fallback, the own-plane test and the
streak's tap spacing are new. Dropped: ssrGlossy paint reflections (a
null result) and their classes, the debug logs in TransportCore, the
queue and the vertex buffer, ssrDebug (now debug views), the ssrWave
and ssrRough knobs.

Known risks: the hero hill's reflection is a grey band (110, 108, 109
where it was measured) on the blue-grey sea; in the round before the
review ssrWater 2.5 showed the hill more clearly but warmer, toward
khaki, so the owner should judge the default. Wet ground turns darker;
the dusk town reads darker except for its puddles, which show the base
build's crimson rain sky and read as red pools.
Puddles take the rain rings' slope at a grazing angle, so their
content changes with time (dark in one still, pale with dark flecks in
the motion frames). The covered-panel rule judges a card's opacity from
the colour of the background next to it: a card in front of a
background of its own colour lets more through, and a free-standing
card that differs from its background reflects whole; no blended card
was in the views shot. Reflections of a vehicle's windows show the
window as the camera sees it (what lies through it), not the vehicle's
inside. The rough blur is horizontal only; at a vehicle's wheels the
outline stays sharp by design and the shadow's own edge still shows
under it; the own-plane test leaves the lowest few centimetres of an
object standing on the wet ground (0.05 m at 5 m, 0.09 m at 15 m) to
the under-vehicle handling. Puddles are
value noise on level ground, may read procedural, and the wetness is
the rain density (no drying) until B7. SSR shows only what is on
screen and fades at the frame's edges; particles and rain drawn over
wet ground get the reflection under them. Nights get nothing (lamp
streaks are B7's). With TAA off the per-frame march turn is not
averaged (not looked at). The Eden view's shots started while the view
was still loading (UNSETTLED); Sinai with @LoBo, smoke_eden and
smoke_sinai_lobo are unverified, and the cost was read under
contention.
