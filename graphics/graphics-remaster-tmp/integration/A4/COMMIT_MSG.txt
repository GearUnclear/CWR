graphics: long views draw the same frame from a fraction of the draw calls

What the player sees: the same picture, faster. From the ridge south of
the Abel airfield at sunrise the frame issues 1,912 draw calls instead
of 12,910 and the GPU's world pass takes 2.5 ms instead of 18.2; the
game runs at 72 fps on the CPU instead of 34. Every sunrise view and
the 120-frame walk over the ridge are pixel-identical with the two
switches off and on; the street lamps of a town at night differ in one
pixel by one level.

How it works, drawGrouping: instanced runs no longer stop at the first
light slot in the scene. Abel's street lamps hold 12 slots by day, so
until now every object drew alone at any hour. The rule is per object:
RunEntry.localLit (InstancedRunRules.hpp) is set from the light list
the object's own draw would select (Scene::SelectLights, asked only
when a slot exists), and CanHead and CanJoin keep a lit object out of
runs, so a run's empty light list is right for every member. The same
switch stops resending unchanged constants: Graphics/Core/UboMirror.hpp
records what the LitConstants and VSConstants buffers hold, every
upload, whole or partial, goes through EngineGL33::UploadLitSlots or
its VS twin, and a flush sends only the range of slots that changed,
or nothing (1,236 kB and 3,731 kB a frame at sunrise_ridge become 3.6
and 9.5). A new buffer starts unknown, so its first flush is whole; the
VS world slots never count while WorldInstances exists (no shader reads
them).

How it works, hillCull: World/Scene/TerrainOcclusion (pure) copies the
terrain grid once per island (1,024 x 1,024 in 1.7 ms) and keeps, from
the eye, per azimuth wedge (512) and chord (25 m apart to 500 m, 50 m
to 1,500 m, 100 m beyond), the steepest slope the lowest drawn terrain
along the chord blocks. The surface is the drawn triangles, so the
lowest point of a chord is exact; a notch narrower than a wedge cannot
hide anything, and an object below the eye is judged by its far side
(the prototype's single rays got both wrong). An object is hidden when
every wedge its sphere (x1.25 plus 1 m) spans blocks a steeper slope
than any point of the sphere; static objects only, never within 75 m,
never the camera's own. Wedges are built when an object first asks, for
the exact eye of the frame. The cull runs after LOD selection and the
object occlusion pass and only flags the object (hillHidden): it keeps
its draw LOD and its place in every sort, the shadow-map pass still
casts it (long dawn shadows of trees behind the ridge still fall on
ground in view), Object::PrepareDrawTextures gives its mipmap hints
without the draw, and the draw loops skip it. CullGovernor idles the
cull after 30 frames that tested fewer than 200 objects or hid under
10%, and probes once every 30 frames.

A leak older than this change made the cull visible at first: the
shadow-map pass's alpha-tested casters sample their textures with
whatever sampler the last draw before the pass left on unit 0, and that
draw is the last road or decal blend section of Pass2's pre-shadow
loop. Hiding that road changed the cutout of every leaf-card shadow in
view (3,291 pixels over 2 levels at sunrise_ridge, 13,829 under the
sun-visibility view). A fixed sampler only moved the difference to the
other side of the switch. The last overlay of that loop is now drawn
even when hidden, and in a view without overlays the last object Pass1
draws (KeepLastPass1Draw): 2 objects a frame, counted as kept.

Counters: GpuTimings splits the world pass into ground, pass1 (the
opaque objects) and rest, from marks Landscape::DrawRect sets after its
existing queue flushes, and keeps each section's minimum ("| min
frame=..."). PerfCounters (runs, lit objects, tested, hidden, kept,
table builds and wedges, the CPU of the cull, LOD selection and
occlusion, uniform uploads and skips) rides in triPerfStats after
" || ", averaged over the profiler's window; triSceneStats adds
culled=, and objects= still counts hidden objects so capture tables
stay comparable. make_pairs.py and summarize_timing.py read the new
columns.

The switch and its gates: drawGrouping and hillCull (perf, Frame
cost), landed. Their gates are on whenever the remastered lighting
draws the world (night vision included) and off with the classic
lighting, so Classic draws as before. Off, each one takes exactly its
old path: runs only with no light slot, whole-block uploads, no cull.

Kept, rewritten and dropped from the prototype (research perf track):
kept the per-object light gate (flag 4), terrain horizon occlusion
(flag 256), both UBO mirrors (flags 1 and 2), the ground / pass1 /
rest sections and the minimum. Rewritten: the light gate as
RunEntry.localLit; the horizon table as exact wedges and chords in a
pure module, built lazily per frame instead of a worker thread for a
raised eye; the cull after LOD selection, flagging instead of removing;
the mirrors behind one upload helper per block; the research counters
as one PerfCounters struct. Dropped: CWR_PERF_PIN_LOD (F1 has
triPinObjectDetail), flags 8, 16, 32, 64 (landed as F6) and 128, the
debug=100+flags channel, the shader-side cutout and tap experiments.

Known risks: PerfCounters.{hpp,cpp}, UboMirror.hpp,
TerrainOcclusion.{hpp,cpp} and three test files are new and must be
added with the commit. The kept-draw rule assumes the last overlay's
blend draw issues triangles; if it were clipped or fogged away the
leaf-shadow cutout could still follow the cull in that view. The cull
assumes the terrain draws at full grid detail (LandCache asks for
segment LOD 0); a coarser far terrain would need the lowest vertex per
coarse cell. The table is rebuilt for every moved eye, costing up to
about 1 ms of CPU a frame on this machine while walking. The live
soldier's head pose differed between the forest shots; whether the
faster frame changes the AI's timing was not verified. Timing is one
reading without the GPU clock sampler; the full timing run comes at the
end of the branch. No RTX 3060 was measured. Object.hpp keeps its
file's tab style for the new declaration (clang-format-diff would
re-indent it with spaces).
