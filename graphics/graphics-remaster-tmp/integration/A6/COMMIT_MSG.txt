graphics: one tiled light list feeds every local light

What the player sees: the town at night as before. In Saint Louis with
its six street lamps and two parked Jeeps' headlights on, 1% of the
frame changes, by about 5 levels where it does: a tree crown beside a
lamp and a few far window frames now rank the lamps nearest to each
pixel rather than the lamps nearest to their object's centre. By day
nothing runs and nothing changes. The work is underneath: every
remastered surface now takes its lamps, headlights and fires from one
list of the frame's lights, binned into 32-pixel screen tiles, instead
of the list each draw selected for itself, so the night, combat and
wet-street steps (B3, B4, B7) shade from one place, and terrain, roads
and objects draw from the same lights.

How it works: Graphics/Rendering/Lighting/FrameLights (pure) takes the
scene's active lights (on, within the horizon range plus 500 m, the 16
strongest at the camera), ranks them as Light::Compare does and bins
each by its reach, a sphere of 10 start distances, into tiles over the
whole frame target from pixel (0, 0): the screen box of the projected
sphere from the tangent planes per axis, nothing for a reach behind the
eye or outside a side plane of the view, the whole viewport for one
that crosses the eye plane. A tile lists up to 16 lights in rank order.
EngineGL33::UpdateLightTiles builds the list once per frame at the
world's first 3D pass while the gate is on and uploads the lights to a
4 KB uniform block (FrameLights, binding 4) and the tile lists to an
RGBA8UI texture on unit 13 (a light index plus one, 0 after the last),
each only when it changed. The fragment finds its tile from
gl_FragCoord (litLightCount.y holds the tile size) and shades with the
one classic model both paths now share (localLight: full strength
inside the start distance, the quadratic falloff, the smooth end at 10
start distances, the smoothed 14 deg cone, the display-space sum and
its 1.0 clamp), scaled by the draw's night factor (litLightCount.z, 1
for a material without sun, as the per-draw upload gave it).

The classic budget: Scene::SelectLights gives each object or terrain
segment its 7 strongest lights by sort brightness over squared distance.
The tiled path keeps 7 per fragment, ranked by the same rule at the
fragment, faded out toward the edge of each light's reach (where a tile
does not list a light it must not rank, or the set would change at a
tile edge), with the 7th and 8th blended when they are within 25% of
each other, so the set changes smoothly across a surface instead of at
object edges. Without the budget, lamp-lit walls and crowns in Saint
Louis came out 16 levels brighter over nearly a quarter of the frame: a
street lamp's start distance is 50 m, so it reaches 500 m, and its
ambient term lit every wall the cap of 7 had held back. Spotlights are
binned by their whole reach for the same reason: the classic rule spends
its slots on near headlights wherever they reach, and binning them by
their cone brought the bright walls back.

While the tiles light the pass, a draw's material upload sends only
slot 22 (101.1 kB of LitConstants a frame at the lamps view become 5.8
kB), and Pass1 lets lit objects join instanced runs
(Engine::LocalLightsFromTiles; with drawGrouping=0 the pre-A4 rule
stays). Lights carry a kind (Light::Kind: street lamps Lamp, camp fires
Fire, weapon flashes, explosions, burning wrecks, flares and missile
motors Combat, spotlights Spot) for the steps that give a kind its own
model; at A6 every kind keeps the classic one. Debug view 6 (debug=6,
"Light tiles") draws the lights per tile and the grid. triPerfStats
gained "tiles lights= refs= tilecpu=" and make_pairs.py its columns.

The switch and its gates: lightTiles (light list, Night and combat),
landed; its GPU time shows in the world section. The gate (A1) is on
with a local light lit at night (from B4 also a combat light within 60
m). By day, in Classic, under night vision and in the passes after the
world (portraits, 3D previews) every draw lights from its own list
through the per-draw slots exactly as before, and lightTiles=0 is that
path.

Kept, rewritten and dropped: not a research track. It replaces the three
lists the combat, night and materials prototypes each built (combat's
HDR flag through the 8 per-draw slots, night's 8 strongest at the camera
handed to every draw, materials' localSpec over the same 8). Corrections
to plan section 5 A6, recorded in the plan: no slot 70 (the tile size
and the night factor ride in litLightCount.yz); a uniform block instead
of a texture buffer (a samplerBuffer build took 1.6 ms of world time
over the per-draw path at the lamps view with another lane's game
running, the block about 0.5); RGBA8UI with 16 entries per tile instead
of RGBA16UI with 8 (8 would cut a lamp at a tile edge when more lights
overlap); spotlights binned by their whole reach, not their cone; every
kind keeps the clamp at A6 (moving Fire and Combat to linear HDR changes
their look, which is B4's to tune); owner exclusion and ParticleLight
left to B3 or B4 (the owner id is kept on the CPU).

Known risks: the world pass costs about 0.5 to 1.1 ms more where many
lights cover the screen (more on an RTX 3060, not measured); each
fragment ranks up to 16 lights. The tiles hold 16 lights and the active
list 16 today; when B4 adds combat lights past 16 a full tile would
drop one and could show a seam at a tile edge. The budget ranks at the
fragment, not the object's centre, so large objects and terrain can take
a different 7 than before (the 1% above). A spotlight takes a budget
slot over its whole reach, as the classic rule did. One timing run (not
the others) showed single CPU frames of 23 to 39 ms in the windows
after switching the tiles on. Lit objects now join instanced runs at
night; A4's kept-draw rule concerns Pass2's pre-shadow loop, not runs,
and was not checked with B3's moon shadow pass. Seen for A4 at the
lamps view: drawGrouping=1 costs CPU there (frame 9.2 to 10.2 ms against
7.4 to 8.0 with it off). FrameLights.{hpp,cpp} and test_frame_lights.cpp
are new files to add with the commit. Lights.hpp keeps its file's tab
style; the added lines are in clang-format style and clang-format-diff
clean.
