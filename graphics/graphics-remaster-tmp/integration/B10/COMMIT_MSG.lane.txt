graphics: sky clouds glow on their sun side and their shadows match the sky

What the player sees: at dawn and dusk the painted sky becomes lit
cloud. The sun-facing edges of the cloud bands turn peach and gold and
their far sides and bellies slate; cloud bases facing a rising sun catch
pink; thin rims near the sun get a silver lining and thick cloud in
front of it goes dark. The light goes both ways and the sky keeps its
brightness: at the hero view a third of the sky band brightens and a
third darkens, and the sky band's mean luminance stays within -0.6 %
and +0.6 % of the look before this change on the nine views measured.
All of it is the low sun's: it fades out between 25 and 45 degrees of
sun, and from there up the sky is the one before (no embossed and no
greyed midday clouds). Cloud shadows now belong to the clouds in the
sky: under broken cloud the shadow bands on the hills are the clouds
you see, the sun disk fades when a cloud in the sky crosses it at the
same moment the ground at the camera falls into shadow, and the sky's
clouds turn slowly with their drift (about 6 degrees a minute at
overcast 0.5), carrying their shadows across the ground. The shadows
stay put on the ground while the player moves. A clear sky is
unchanged.

How it works: Graphics/Remaster/SkyDensity (pure) reads the sky's own
painted clouds back from the weather sky texture as a cloud-density map
(no new art): whiteness above the clearest sky of the same row is
broken cloud, a row grey all the way round is an overcast deck counted
as far as the weather is overcast, darkness is thickness, blurred 5-tap
binomial so the palette steps do not show. EngineGL33_SkyClouds.cpp
reads each weather sky file once and builds the map (decode, the mix of
two weather skies as the engine mixes the dome's texture, the map and
its shadow coverage) on a worker thread when the weather sky changes;
texture unit 9 holds a no-cloud stand-in until the first one, the last
map stays in use while the next weather builds, and triSettleScene
waits for a pending build. Lit clouds: from each dome pixel a 6-step
march toward the sun through the dome's own texture mapping (obloha.p3d,
u = azimuth / 180 deg, v piecewise over its rings) gives the cloud the
sunlight crosses; the difference to the pixel's own density is
mean-free relief on luminance, tinted only by the sun's colour, plus a
Henyey-Greenstein silver lining on rims, darker thick cloud against the
sun and deeper bellies under a deck. Every one of these terms fades in
one step on the way out of litCloudTerms between 25 and 45 degrees of
sun (SkyDensity's LitCloudTerms is its C++ mirror), and from 45 degrees
up the dome and sheet marches return the identity terms before they
sample. Under the sky table it shades the table's sky and cloud colours
in linear light before their display mapping; without it, the classic
dome colour. DrawClouds' sheets get the same terms from a march in their
own texture. Sky-matched shadows: a lit point is projected along the sun
to a deck at 1800 m and the deck point is looked up in the map in the
direction an anchor near the camera sees it (one density tap per lit
fragment, two while an anchor change fades). The anchor stays until the
camera is 1.5 km away, then steps 1.5 km toward it and cross-fades over
4 s; a cut of 500 m snaps it; captures pin it to the camera
(triPinCloudDrift). The rig keeps the classic overcast dimming on
average over the sky's own covered share of the ground
(LightRigInputs::skyShadowCoverage: the shadowed part of the ground
within 3 km of the anchor at the sun's height, from a table built with
the map): over a larger share more light passes under each cloud, over
a smaller one the rest stays uniform. The dome turns about the zenith
with the sky clouds' own drift (Landscape::CloudsPosition, 0.3 deg per
unit), whenever a clouds switch is on, so a gate opening never makes it
jump. The sky pass hook is A7's: BeginRemasterSky opens for the clouds
too, and a new RemasterSky::Sun pass around the sun disk fades it
(colour and coverage) behind a dome cloud. LitConstants slots 68 (Sky)
and 69 (SkyShadow) land (Count 70); unit 9 gets its kLitSamplers row.

The switches and their gates: litClouds (GPU section sky) and
skyCloudShadows (section world), both FeatureSet members, landed. Lit
clouds run while the sky-through is below 0.85 and the sun above -9
degrees (fading with the sky table's night fade); above 45 degrees they
cost one uniform branch, so they need no high-sun gate. The shadows run
while the cloud cover is above the clear-sky cover (F4) and the sun
above -1.5 degrees, where the rig's sun has faded out, so the hand-over
to the noise field never shows (A1 had 0). Both read "sky density map
building" until a map exists. Off (both switches, a gate, classic
lighting, night vision, a portrait or a 3D preview) the clouds slots
stay zero: the dome keeps its texture coordinate, the sheets and the
sun pass through, the noise field casts the shadows, and every draw is
the one before.

Kept, rewritten and dropped from the prototype: kept the density map
(row-relative whiteness, deck row, darkness, the blur), the march in
direction space through the dome mapping, the mean-free relief, the
silver lining, the darkening against the sun, the belly contrast, the
dome-matched shadows, the sun disk under a dome cloud and the sheets'
own march. Rewritten: the builder as a pure module on a worker thread,
the sky hook A7's, r0..r7 as constants the shaders mirror (deck 1800 m,
threshold 0.55, edge 0.12, relief 1.8, silver 1.0, step 1.2 deg), the
relief and lining on luminance tinted by the sun's chroma only (the
critic's colour fringes), every term faded between 25 and 45 degrees of
sun (the critic's midday emboss; a reviewed build faded only the relief
and the lining, which left the darkening against the sun alone at
midday: looking up at 54 degrees of sun, 35 % of the sky band went
darker by more than 2 levels and none brighter, and the white cumulus
greyed), the shadow anchor with a cross-fade instead of the camera, the
drift from the sky clouds' own integrator, the mean over the sky's own
covered share at the sun's height, the sun disk faded rather than
darkened (a first build drew a dark disk), the shadows' sun threshold.
Dropped: the CLOUDSDOME and CLOUDSSHEET dumps, the unused
CollectCloudSheets scaffolding (sheet shadows are a follow-up), variant
B, the research knobs, the viewport slot (the view ray comes from the
vertex position since A7), the screen-derivative march on the dome (its
seams), the mis-encoded comment hunk. Correction to the plan:
atmosphere keeps its own cloud mask (A7 tuned it, noon cirrus included);
the lit-cloud terms read the map.

Measured cost (single readings: RTX 4060 Ti, 1920x1080, scale 1.0, 4x
MSAA, off then on interleaved, other games running, not quiet-checked,
GPU clock not sampled): at the hero view coast_oc_up (sun 8 degrees,
overcast 0.5, mostly sky) the sky section rises from 0.14 to 0.17 ms to
0.22 to 0.28 ms (+0.08 median over 3 rounds) on the build that lands,
+0.18 in a second run of 2 rounds and +0.26 on the reviewed build (the
low-sun path is the same); the frame +0.27, +0.66 and +0.76 median,
while the control under a clear sky (noon_fields, both gates off, so
on equals off) moved +0.80 and +0.15: the frame figure is within the
noise. From 45 degrees of sun up lit clouds skip the march (one uniform
branch). Plan estimate 0.5 to 1.5 ms.

Known risks: a still under a cloud reads darker (overcast_broken ground
-28 %, the Jeep loses its sun shadow), though the mean over the ground
is the classic one; below about 12 degrees of sun the painted skies'
horizon holds no cloud the row test finds, so golden hour gets the
uniform classic dimming and no sky-matched shadows, and the cloud seen
near a low sun is a classic sheet, which casts none (sheet shadows are
a follow-up); shadow shapes are the painted streaks, long bands rather
than cumulus patches; the dome now turns (a rotation about the zenith,
so clouds near the horizon move as fast across the sky as those
overhead), and it is not turned under night vision; the coverage takes
the deck from sea level, so a camera high above the ground sees
slightly different lookups; between 25 and 45 degrees of sun the
lit-cloud terms are part way (half at 35), and from 45 up an overcast
deck keeps its painted bellies as they were (the prototype deepened
them at any sun height; overcast 0.9 at 14:30 is now the sky before);
golden hour skies gain saturation on the clouds' sun-facing edges
(coast_oc_up 0.111 to 0.124, sunrise 0.183 to 0.224); with the sun
clear in the sky the sea's sun path is a broad bright column the noise
field used to cover (B5 reworks it); a straight sheet edge high in the
sky is Classic's, present with every switch off; the contract tests
match shader source text; the timing was not taken on a quiet machine;
no RTX 3060 was measured.
