graphics: a physically based sky table lights the dome and the fog colour

What the player sees: the sky comes from real air physics under the
original sky textures' clouds. A low sun gets a golden band along the
horizon and a glow around it (the aureole), dusk an orange band under a
mauve sky, sunrise keeps its pink with a coral glow at the sun, noon a
graded blue that keeps its cirrus. Distant ground, the far horizon polygon
and the sea's distance fog fade into the same sky colour in their own
direction (aerial perspective), so the flat band between land or sea and
sky is gone. Each sky row keeps the original dome's brightness on average,
sun glow included: the sky band's mean luminance is within -0.1 % and
+6 % of the original's on the five daytime views measured. Night keeps
the classic sky (the table fades out between -3 and -9 degrees of sun),
and an overcast deck keeps the classic fog colour. The lighting of lit
geometry is unchanged; only its fog colour changes.

How it works: Graphics/Remaster/Atmosphere (pure) holds the clear-sky
model of the research prototype (Rayleigh, Mie, ozone and Hillaire's
multiple scattering, Bruneton constants): a transmittance and a
multiple-scattering table, built on a worker thread (std::async, as the
terrain horizon map) the first time the feature runs and again when the
haze changes, and the sky-view table, built on the GPU in two fullscreen
passes (EngineGL33_SkyTable.cpp, GLSL in the GL-free
EngineGL33_PostSkyTableShaders.cpp as two kPostShaders rows) whenever the
sun has moved 0.02 degrees: regions of Rayleigh plus multiple scattering,
Mie without its phase (applied per pixel, so the aureole stays sharp) and
each row's mean, 192x64 RGBA32F on texture unit 8. The prototype rebuilt
it on the CPU in 8 to 12 ms every few seconds of play; the GPU build takes
0.06 ms. Atmosphere also holds the CPU mirrors of both passes
(SkyViewTexel, SkyViewRowMean, SkyViewRowGlow), and the new tri verb
triSkyTableCheck reads the GPU table back against them.
Landscape::DrawSky and DrawClouds open the sky pass through one hook,
Engine::BeginRemasterSky(RemasterSky::Dome or Clouds) / EndRemasterSky:
the dome and the sky clouds come before the world's first 3D pass, so the
hook opens the remastered programs for them as BeginPass does (the stars,
sun and moon stay classic), computes the frame's gates there, builds the
table and sets the sky mode (litSkyFwd.w) that tells the screen vertex
shader which draws are the dome. That shader now pastes the LitConstants
block (kScreenVertexPieces) and rebuilds each pixel's view ray from its
screen position and the camera basis. The dome (shadeSkyDome) keeps the
texture's clouds as a mask against each row's clearest texel and lights
them from the sun at 2.5 km plus the sky above; the fog of lit and pre-lit
world draws (atmoFogColor) reads the table only where fog shows. Slots 61
to 67 of LitConstants land (58 to 60 become one padding member), and the
GPU timings gain the sky and skytable sections.

The switch and its gates: atmosphere (a FeatureSet member, landed), with
the sub-knobs atmoHue 0.5, atmoSat 1.15 and atmoHaze 1.0. Its gate runs
it while the sun is above -9 degrees and the atmosphere tables exist
("atmosphere tables building" until then; triSettleScene waits for them).
Off (the switch, the gate, classic lighting, a portrait, a 3D preview
after the world, night vision) the hook returns at once and the sky slots
stay zero, so every draw is the one before this change.

Kept, rewritten and dropped from the prototype: kept the model, its
constants and its 40-step march (the discriminants rewritten as (r mu)^2
plus a difference of squares of nearby radii, precise in float near the
ground), the per-row brightness reference, the per-pixel aureole, the
night fade, the fog colour from the table for lit and pre-lit draws and
the horizon polygon, and the critic's hue 0.5 and saturation 1.15.
Rewritten: the table on the GPU, the tables on a worker thread, the hook
in place of the BeginMesh hack and the t1 dome marker, the row reference
with the original sun glow (the prototype read 9 % darker at sunrise), the
hue blend relighting the original hue by the brightness ratio (the
channel-by-channel relight turned the pink horizon opposite a low sun
olive), the cloud mask's dead zone 0.004 instead of 0.015 and a cloud's
colour taken out of the sky behind it (the noon cirrus survives), the
cover mixed in display space. Dropped: atmoLight (blue ground), atmoSun,
atmoExposure, atmoFog, atmoClouds, atmoDebug and the logs; the water's
reflection term is left for the water step. Correction to the plan: the
view ray needs no pillarbox rectangle (it starts from the vertex's own
screen position; checked with a 4:3 world rect).

Measured cost (a single reading: RTX 4060 Ti, 1920x1080, scale 1.0, 4x
MSAA, off then on in 3 interleaved rounds, 0 of 4 tri slots held, no
foreign game in the clock samples): at P0 the whole frame, on minus off,
is +0.49 ms median / +0.48 minimum on sunrise_ridge and +0.53 / +0.55 on
noon_fields, most of it the aerial perspective on fogged world fragments.
The sky dome itself (the sky section) is 0.08 to 0.10 ms at P0; a table
build (a one-minute-per-frame time-lapse at sunrise_ridge) 0.06 ms. A
second run after the fog lookup was limited to fogged fragments read
+0.59 and +0.33 ms on the minimum, on a busier machine (CPU frame 2 to 6
ms longer on the same views), so it does not separate that change from
the noise. coastline_sunset runs at P3 (0.8 to 1.1 GHz), where the sky
section reads 0.27 ms. Plan estimate about 0.5 ms; MENU's 1.5 to 2x rule
gives about 0.5 to 1.2 ms on a 3060.

Known risks: the anti-sun horizon band at a low sun is a warm grey 9 %
darker than the original's pink (the sun side is 34 % brighter, the row
average kept); the sky's faint texture blotches read up to 13 % stronger
than the original's at sunrise and sunset; noon is a more saturated blue
than the original (the owner's 1.15); the 40-step march overstates the
haze by up to 7.5 %; the table builds only while this switch is on
(water, air and clouds widen that when they land); the haze is a fixed
knob, not yet tied to fog or overcast; the 3D sky clouds only take the
fog colour (the clouds step lights them); the pillarbox check had the sun
just outside the 4:3 rect; the contract tests match shader source text;
LitConstants slots 58 to 60 are one padding member that B1 splits when
it lands (A5 landed no slot); RemasterSkyPending reads a flag only the
sky pass clears, so switching atmosphere off during a table build leaves
triSettleScene waiting to its maxFrames (test tooling only, a
follow-up); no RTX 3060 was measured.
