graphics: low-beam headlights, moon shadows and stars at night

What the player sees: a moonless country road is dark under a field of
small, faintly coloured stars, and the Jeep's low beams lay a white pool
on it: the hot spot 20 to 30 m ahead reads 193 levels (106 before, a flat
streak), the verges take the wide spill, and the light stops at a hard
top edge, so the far field away from the beam goes from 27 to 18 levels.
Under a moon the moon casts shadows: the parked Jeep, the bushes and the
town's trees lay moon shadows on the road and the lawn, and the sea
glints toward the moon. Street lamps fall off from the bulb instead of
lighting everything within 50 m at full strength and reaching 500 m: the
moonless town square loses its flat khaki wash (lawn 122 to 42 levels,
saturation 0.11 to 0.03, mean 91 to 45), facades and the birch crown by
the lamp are lit and not black, and the camp fire's smoke is fire-lit
instead of white. A dusk sky with the moon up keeps its glow on the
sun's side. By day, in Classic and under night vision nothing changes.

How it works:
- Moon key (moonShadows): ComputeKeyLight (LightRig) chooses what the
  shader's sun slots carry. Under the moon key it moves a share of F9's
  moon (kMoonFull 0.02, its chroma and sky fill unchanged) out of the
  shadowless litMoon term into litSun/litSunDir, so the shadow map, the
  terrain horizon, the cloud shadows, the glossy highlight and the sea's
  sun lobes follow the moon. MoonKeyShare (NightLights) is 0 unless A1's
  gate is on (sun 3 deg under, moon above 3 deg, phase above 0.25, no
  night vision) and fades in over 3 deg above the sun and moon edges and
  0.1 of phase. The scene renders the shadow pass at night when
  Engine::RemasterMoonKey() says so (the frame's gate), from the moon's
  direction, with B1's stock 12-tap filter (no penumbra shows at a
  fiftieth of the noon sun); the leaf lift stays.
- The sky stays on the sun: LitConstants slot 70 (SkySun, free since A6)
  carries the sun's own direction and elevation, which the sky table and
  its twilight glow, the fog toward the sun, the warm side of the sky
  light, B10's lit clouds, the sun disk behind a cloud and B2's crown
  low-sun factors read (skySunDir, skySunElevation, skySunRadiance, 0
  under the moon key). It equals the key slots whenever the sun is the
  key. Without it the moon key (from 3 deg under) and the sky table
  (down to 9 deg under) overlap and the dusk glow jumps to the moon.
- Physical lamps and low beams (nightLights), a model of A6's tiled
  light list (the gate needs lightTiles). With slot 71 (Night: x on,
  y lamp intensity per start metre, z low-beam peak, w the moon key's
  share) on, street lamps and headlights leave the classic model and its
  budget of 7 (which now ranks the classic kinds among themselves) for
  physicalLight: intensity over d^2 + 0.25 (lamps 1.9 * start *
  nightLamp, headlights 800 * nightSpot * the low-beam pattern); a lamp
  in its authored colour taken to linear, a headlight white at its
  configured colour's linear luminance (PhysicalColour). The low beam
  has a hot band 1.7 deg under its axis, a top edge closing from 0.3 to
  1.4 deg above it, a +-14 deg core and a +-34 deg spill, a faint floor
  to 34 deg under and nothing behind the lamp; it is aimed from the
  vehicle's body (the lamp's heading, the body's pitch along it, 1%
  down; LowBeamAim in UpdateLightTiles). A physical reach (where the
  strongest light falls to 0.002, at most the classic 10 start
  distances; 218 m for a 50 m-start lamp) ends each light in a smooth
  window, and the tiles bin physical lights by it. Rough diffuse
  (Oren-Nayar, sigma 0.5), leaves wrapped with the crown normal and the
  forced diffuse as for the sun, a lamp's classic ambient capped, a
  gleam on glossy materials, the sum linear outside the classic 1.0
  clamp, times the draw's night factor. A fade from 1.0 to 0.25 m off
  the bulb and the back plane keep a lamp off its own housing and
  vehicle (a draw in an instanced run has no single object to exclude
  by id). F9's night eye is unchanged.
- Smoke and dust (Object::DrawDecal) take the same lamps and headlights
  through Engine::ParticleNightLight and FrameLights::ParticleLight,
  summed in linear light; the classic loop skips those two kinds then.
  B4 reuses ParticleLight.
- Stars: DrawPoints draws each star as a 5-vertex fan (MakeStarSplat:
  centre 1.8 times the star's alpha, 0 at a 1.6 px square's corners, a
  cool-to-warm tint per star at unit luminance) while the stars gate is
  on, the 2 px bilinear dot otherwise.
- New Graphics/Remaster/NightLights.{hpp,cpp} (the moon key share, the
  Night slot, the star splat); the physical model, the aim, the reach,
  the colour and ParticleLight are in Graphics/Rendering/Lighting/
  FrameLights. B10's planned padding 70 to 73 became litSkySun, litNight
  and litPlanned[2] (72, 73); LitConst::Count stays 78.

The switches and their gates: nightLights, moonShadows and stars (night
track, Night and combat) have landed (18/26). nightLights and stars run
with the night effect above 0.01, nightLights also needs the light list
switch; moonShadows runs under the moon key, and its gate also opens the
sun shadow gates and B10's sky cloud shadows. Sub-knobs nightLamp 1.0 and
nightSpot 1.0 under nightLights. GPU sections: world (nightLights,
stars), shadow (moonShadows). Off is the look before this step.

Known risks: towns at night are about half as bright as before (the
lever is nightLamp); moonless country away from the beam is darker (the
open night-exposure question); a verge in the beam core 10 to 20 m out
reads brighter than the noon lawn and shows the grass texture's own
pale olive (saturation 0.24); F9's night eye greys medium-lit lamp
light, so pools read grey-warm, not amber; hill roads checked by unit
test only, not in game; physical lights skip the classic budget, so a
tile with many lamps evaluates all of them; lamp light still passes
through walls; the splats make stars plain in the red dusk sky at a sun
7.8 deg under, where the classic dots were faint (the stars gate reads
the night effect, 1.00 there); the first frame of a moon key may sample the previous map with the moon's
direction (the key fades in from 0 there). NightLights.{hpp,cpp} and
test_night_lights.cpp are new files to add with the commit.
