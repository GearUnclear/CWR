AGENT 2 REPORT: capture-loop pacing and temporal history (mist swarm, valley_mist)
==================================================================================
Angle: what happens between two captured shots on the overhaul, how the temporal
filters advance per rendered frame versus per sim step, and whether K render-only
warm-up frames at the held pose (the handoff's candidate capture-only fix) converge
the mist. Verdict: RULED OUT as a fix, and the flicker is a GAME bug that the clip's
capture cadence roughly doubles. K warm-up makes it worse. Rendering exactly one
frame per 1/60 s step (the cadence of real play at 60 fps) halves it. The cause is
the fog's frame-global jitter (agent 3's finding, which I reproduced independently).
Agent 3's per-cell jitter is the fix.


1. WHAT HAPPENS BETWEEN TWO SHOTS (measured, not assumed)
---------------------------------------------------------
Each clip shot is 6 top-level harness statements (capClockAt, capPathAt,
capPropsAt, the step block, triScreenshot, capFrameLog). Trident evaluates one
statement per IPC round trip, and the game's main loop keeps ticking (AppIdle =
World::Simulate at accTime 0 + a full world render + present) while it waits. For a
top-level triScreenshot, Trident also polls for the PNG before it sends the next
statement.
capFrameLog logs the engine frame counter (frame= in the feat line), so the
renders per shot can be read from the log:
  the shot clip v2/runs/ud-valley_mist/udcap.log: 7 (79 shots), 8 (352), 9 (98), 10 (40)
  agent 6's base probe:                          7-10 (mean 8.2)
  wall per shot: 0.55-1.04 s (clip), 0.57 s median (base probe)
One of the renders runs the 1/60 s step. The other 6-9 render the same sim state.
From capPathAt onward they also hold the new pose (triSetView). So the "warm-up
at the held pose" already happens, about 6-8 frames per shot. The varying count
does not drive the flicker: corr(renders per shot, band |d2|) = +0.09 on the clip
(agent 3: -0.03..0.04).

What advances per rendered frame (all keyed on frame counters, none on sim time):
  - froxel fog injection: one jitter per frame for ALL cells (EngineGL33_PostFog.cpp
    PostFog, jz/jx/jy = Weyl sequences of _fog.sequence, ++ per PostFog call),
    blended into the reprojected history at kFogHistoryShare = 0.12; history kept only
    if appliedFrame + 1 == frame (consecutive frames).
  - fog horizon ring: share 0.3 per frame, decay 0.995.
  - near-field beams: interleaved-gradient start offset seq % 64 (per pixel, TAA-averaged).
  - TAA: Halton(2,3) 16-frame cycle (TemporalAA.hpp kTaaJitterCycle); AO/SSR/shaft
    noiseShift = golden ratio of _taa.sequence; GTAO accumulation kGtaoHistoryWeight;
    soft-shadow rotation from _taa.sequence.
  - camera exposure adaptation: WALL clock dt, clamped at 0.25 s per rendered frame
    (EngineGL33_Camera.cpp:241-242).
What advances per sim step only: the mist drift (Glob.time), AI, particles.
triWaitFrames (the head team's warning) draws a CLEARED frame with no world
(InitDraw + FinishDraw + NextFrame), so it breaks every history chain. A
"triSimFrames K" at accTime 0 instead draws the full world K times with no sim
advance. That is the warm-up form tested here.

3. WHY (model and fits)
-----------------------
- The band flicker is one coherent sheet: an SVD of the band blocks' d2 puts 62% of the
  energy in the first component on the clip (91% of blocks share its sign), 81% on K=8
  and 82% on rt10.
- It follows the fog's frame-global jitter. Regressing the band residual on the EMA
  (share 0.12) of the three Weyl jitters with seq = frame counter - lag (my
  tools/jitfit.py): clip R2 0.36 at lag 29 (median over lags 0.05, p99 0.18); the share
  is diagnostic (0.05: 0.39, 0.12: 0.36, 0.3: 0.20, 1.0: 0.04, so a slow history and not
  the ring or TAA); K=8 R2 0.61 at lag 30; rt10 R2 0.62-0.66 at lag 28 and its Fibonacci
  aliases (a 144-frame shift repeats the golden-ratio jitter). Agent 3 got 0.45-0.57 on the clip
  and 0.83-0.91 with a static camera, and band 0.024 with the jitter frozen.
- An EMA cannot converge below its floor. Its residual is a weighted sum of the last
  ~15 jitters, so more renders at a held pose only re-draw the residual
  (tools/simcad.py). That is why K never helps. The golden-ratio sequence changes the
  linear residual by about its own size every frame, so the linear model alone predicts
  little cadence effect (rt 0.9x). The measured 2x comes from the nonlinearity: a thin
  mist layer inside a thick far slice makes the sampled density a step function of jz.
  Its slow Fibonacci harmonics (2 phi mod 1 = 0.236, 5 phi mod 1 = 0.09 cycles per
  frame) pass the 0.12 history. At one render per step they drift smoothly. At 7-17
  renders per shot with a varying count they alias into shot-to-shot jumps.
- The sky grain (bandmetric sky) follows the wall time per shot, not the fog: the
  exposure adapts on the wall clock, clamped at 0.25 s per render, so the
  longer a shot takes, the further the exposure chases the meter's noise (K=8 0.088,
  base 0.034, rt10 0.022, vanilla 0.025). Agent 4 showed that freezing it leaves the band unchanged.

4. VERDICT AND RECOMMENDATION
-----------------------------
- Capture artefact or game bug: GAME BUG, amplified about 2x by the capture cadence.
  At the cadence of real play (one render per 1/60 s step) the pump stays at band
  0.268 (2.6x vanilla, 7.7x noair) with the same jitter signature. Agent 1's true
  real-time recording gives 0.282.
- Do NOT use K render-only warm-up frames: every K from 1 to 8 is worse (band 0.570 to
  0.602 against 0.497/0.506 for the two baselines, +13 to +21%; sky grain up to x2.6
  at K=8) and costs one GPU frame per K per shot.
- Fix at the cause in the engine (graphics-remaster): agent 3's per-cell decorrelated
  jitter in psFogInject (EngineGL33_PostFogShaders.cpp fogSlice/main), with a unit
  test. Under the clip cadence it gives 0.063.
  With it, the real-time cadence gives band 0.036, spikes 0 (rt10pc), the no-air
  floor (noair 0.035) and under vanilla (0.102). Agent 1 found the same in real time:
  percell 0.112 at a 60 fps cadence and 0.045 with the camera static.
- Capture staging (vanilla unchanged: it has no temporal filters, and its step
  needs the triWaitFrames 60 pump): for the overhaul side, use the one-render-per-step
  form above (gen_video_v2.py frames_block: batch every 10 shots of clock, pose,
  props, triScreenshot, step and log into one call statement). It matches what a
  player sees at 60 fps. It also cuts the wall time per shot by about a quarter
  (0.42 vs 0.57 s) and brings the sky grain to the vanilla level. The batch join
  adds one extra accTime-0 render at the held pose every 10 shots (dframe 2 on
  14 of 150 shots). Event lines of other clips (shells, movers) would have to go
  inside the batch too.
- Not recommended: a fixed Fibonacci number of renders per shot (13 or 21) would
  hold the depth jitter's phase almost still from shot to shot (13 x 0.618 = 8.03)
  and hide part of the pump in the capture only. That is a cosmetic capture trick over a real game bug. Averaging M
  presented frames per shot reaches only 0.84x (M=4) / 0.71x (M=8) / 0.53x (M=16) in
  the model.
