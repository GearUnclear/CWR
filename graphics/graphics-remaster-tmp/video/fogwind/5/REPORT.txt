WHY THE TIME AXIS (the mechanism; tools/model5.py)
-------------------------------------------------
Every cell's offset followed the golden-ratio sequence j(t) = fract(0.618 t). Its sawtooth
harmonics alias: harmonic m sits at |m*0.382 - round| cycles per frame, so the Fibonacci harmonics
(5, 8, 13, 21, 34) land at 5.4, 3.3, 2.0, 1.3, 0.8 Hz at 60 fps, and the EMA history (share 0.12)
passes those slow parts with gain 0.2-0.8. That slow residual is the whole problem:
  - frame-wide offset: the slow parts have one phase over whole regions -> the sheet pumps;
  - d91a08e0 adds an IGN offset per cell, and IGN is linear in the cell index (+0.556 phase per cell
    to the right), so each slow harmonic becomes a plane wave with a slowly advancing phase -> it
    travels, about a cell per render (agent 1, fable-2): the WIND;
  - a white per-cell hash (agent 2's A/Ac) stops the travel but keeps the slow parts in place:
    a slow in-place boil (Ac 2x the floor in play).
Model (thin mist layer in 10% of a slice, EMA 0.12, centre read), share of the residual's power
below 3 Hz: frame-wide 0.27, IGN 0.27, white hash 0.27, strict 8-cycle 0.000. A per-cell offset can
only re-phase the slow parts of a quasi-periodic sequence; a periodic sequence has none. Agent 6's
centre read (the shipped read at the jittered point is a leaky sum, 8.3x) shrinks whatever is left.

THE CANDIDATE (tools/assemble5.py builds every variant from the engine's raw strings)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  depth: place = (cycle + (x&1) + 2(y&1) + 4(slice&1)) & 7, offset = (vdc[place] + sub) / 8,
         vdc = 0 4 2 6 1 5 3 7 (van der Corput: little energy at the lowest harmonic),
         sub = a static white hash of (cell, slice), so neighbouring cells use different 8-point
         sets (static, cannot move).
  xy:    placeXY = (cycle + 2((x&1) + 2(y&1))) & 7 on the 8-point lattice ((i+.5)/8, ((3i&7)+.5)/8).
  history: reprojected at the cell centre, read through 4 fixed tetrahedral trilinear taps.
  Variants run: c8 (centre read, no blur), c8b (+-0.25, RECOMMENDED), c8h (+-0.125), c8nh (no
  history: debug proof that the counter advances), prod / prodb (the reference patch's
  uniform-driven shader: compile checks). Built, not run: c8a (far share 0.05), c8z (no sub
  offset), c8t / c8at (+ agent 6's display tent), gcr (d91 + centre read).

RISKS AND OPEN POINTS
---------------------
  - Needs C++ (the cycle uniform) and so the lead's rebuild; the sign-bit counter is a prototype
    only. The unit test [remaster][fog][Shaders] pins the d91 strings (fogCellNoise, g_cellJitter)
    and must be updated with the shader; a C++ mirror test could assert that every 2x2x2 block
    covers all 8 places in every frame and that the per-cell input repeats with period 8.
  - Frame-rate dependence: at 60 fps the fundamental is 7.5 Hz; at 20 fps it is 2.5 Hz. The
    pattern still stands (cannot travel) and is at the floor at 60 fps, but low-fps play is
    untested.
  - Same class as the other passing candidates (q2b, S2z, t4wb, Act25): on these probes they tie;
    c8b's specific property is that no residual component below 7.5 Hz can exist at any cadence
    (play or the 7-10 renders per captured shot), with one tap per cell. The shader-only rivals
    avoid the C++ change.
  - Process: a killed queue of mine kept running (TaskStop stopped only the parent shell), so for
    about 12 minutes two of my games shared RemasterUD-fog-5. The runs m3moving-c8h, static-c8h and
    m3moving-c8b each had a tri retry and mixed two attempts' frames: their region means stand,
    their flicker numbers are not used in this report. Everything in the tables above is from clean
    single runs (or agent 1's recorder).
