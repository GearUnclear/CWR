HEAD FIX RECIPE for the v2 comparison video (both builds)
=========================================================
Lead: agent 8, 2026-10-19 07:45, from the head board (tmp/video/heads/BOARD.txt,
agents 1 to 8). Capture staging and capture settings only: no engine, config or
game change. Apply it to EVERY clip on BOTH sides (van_* and ud_*, and pv_*).
Verified on the shared repro and on two real clips (opening, town_patrol) on
both builds; numbers and crop sheets at the end.


1. THE CAUSE IN PLAIN WORDS
---------------------------
The men look left and right in quick snaps because the capture fast-forwards
their AI.

Every posed soldier and civilian runs the stock AI "look around" routine
(Soldier::AIFire). It turns his head toward his group's formation direction
for a few seconds, then back to straight ahead for a few seconds, and so on.
In real time that is a slow, natural glance every 4 to 12 s.

The clips do not run in real time. Between two shots the game's main loop keeps
ticking, and every tick simulates the REAL wall time that has passed (up to
0.3 s per tick). A shot (screenshot write, script statements, file polls)
takes 0.3 to 1 s of wall time. So each 1/60 s video frame gave the AI 0.31 to
0.47 s of game time on vanilla and 0.44 to 0.97 s on the overhaul: a 20x to
60x fast-forward. Each look phase then lasted a few to 20 frames, and each
turn finished inside one frame. The head snapped out and back, over and over.
triSetSimTime only pins the clock that water and clouds read; it never limited
this. The same fast-forward also ran fires, smoke, flares, shells and idle
body sway 20x to 60x too fast, and at a different speed on each half (the
overhaul spends more wall time per shot).

Ruled out on the board:
  - per-frame setPos/setDir pinning (agent 2)
  - TAA, MSAA, LOD and mip sampling (agent 5)
  - idle animations (agent 6)
  - any overhaul-only engine change (agent 4: identical head code in both builds)
  - an oscillating head-turn controller (MoveHead is a rate limiter and cannot
    overshoot; agent 4)


2. THE FIX: TWO PARTS, BOTH NEEDED
-----------------------------------
A. EXACT TIME STEP. Each video frame advances the world by exactly 1/60 s,
   whatever the wall time. Freeze the world between shots (setAccTime 0), then
   step it once inside ONE harness statement (a single call { } block, so that
   no main-loop tick can slip in at full speed).
B. GAZE HOLD. Every posed man doWatches a point 300 m straight ahead of his own
   facing, at his own eye level. Give the order in the STAGING block (after the
   props, BEFORE the settle frames). The look-around then has nothing to
   alternate between, and both halves hold the same gaze. Without the hold the
   look-around still runs at natural speed, but its random timers differ per
   build, so the two halves would turn heads at different moments.
A alone already removes the snaps (repro: 0 snaps, 0 flips on both builds), and
B alone removes the flips even under the old fast-forward (agents 1 and 7). Use
both: A makes everything else (fire, smoke, shells, sway) play at the same real
speed on both halves, and B keeps the two halves' gazes identical.

(c) frames_block(): in BOTH branches, right after

        vanilla:         out.append("call { setAccTime 0 }")

Why the vanilla step is exact: vanilla has no triSetFixedStep. triWaitFrames 60
pumps 60 render-only frames (no simulation, about 0.3 s of wall time or more).
The single tick that follows then sees more than 0.3 s, so the engine's own cap
makes it exactly 0.3 s, and 0.3 s x 1/18 = 1/60 s. Measured by agent 3: 240 of
240 shots advanced exactly 17 ms (1/60 s after the clock's millisecond
rounding), heads/3/van-fixdt60 and heads/3/van-turn60. 30 pump frames is NOT
enough (14 ms steps). Keep 60.

6. WHAT NOT TO DO
-----------------
- Do not give doWatch (or setFormDir, or any other AI order) after
  capMode/capPin, or per frame. The clips pin the clock BACK after the settle,
  so no AI group "thinks" again during the clip and a late doWatch is silently
  ignored. Agent 4 measured this on vanilla: the late-watched man still
  snapped.
- Do not split the step block into separate top-level statements
  (setAccTime 1 / triSimFrames 1 / setAccTime 0 on three lines). The main loop
  ticks between statements at full speed, so the step is no longer exact
  (agent 4).
- Do not use triSetFixedStep alone on the overhaul. Without setAccTime 0
  between shots the main-loop ticks still run, now 1/60 s each, and how many
  run per shot depends on harness latency.
- No triWaitFrames on the overhaul: the render-only pump breaks the TAA history
  and the whole image shimmers (0.36 px RMS jitter vs 0.03 px, agent 5). On
  vanilla, never use fewer than 60 pump frames.
- Do not try to fix it with per-frame setDir/setPos, switchMove or stance
  changes. None of them stops the look-around (agents 2 and 6).
- setAccTime 0 is safe in these clips: the cutscene camera is committed, not
  "manual" (agent 3). If a clip ever drives a manual camera, use
  setAccTime 0.000001 instead (agent 4: CameraHold divides by the time
  acceleration in its manual branch). The overhaul block with 0.000001 was
  also verified (agents 4 and 7).
