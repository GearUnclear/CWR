## one_line
This report is honest and fully backed by files. Screen-space GI is invisible by day (p99 a few levels; you need x8 change maps to see it) and gives one real but narrow night win: a hull beside a headlight pool goes from near-black to a grey-lit, textured face at x2. That is not an RTX-on look.
## critique
Verification: nothing is fabricated. The overrides demonstrably loaded in every run, the raw shots differ from the gi=0 baseline exactly as described, the repeated gi=0 shot reproduces the baseline to 0.00, and the strafe pairs show no ghosting.

Look, by day: Remastered and +SSGI side by side, even the non-physical mode-6 upper bound, are indistinguishable at normal size. In a 2x crop at low sun, the M113's road wheels pick up a slight cool-grey lift. The noon Jeep's tyres are a touch lighter. A player would not notice either. Physical mode 1 at x1 is a few levels at most. The explorer's own explanation holds: the rig's bright sky fill already supplies the hemisphere, and CWA's sunlit ground sits near the rig's assumed ground albedo. Measured against the four owner areas, this adds nothing to light and shadow, sky and air, or water and shine in daylight.

Look, at night: the headlight case is the only scene where the path-traced cue (a surface next to a bright pool picks up its light) shows. The M113's rear face goes from about 10 to 25 levels to about 22 to 40 at x2, with texture and panel lines kept intact by the v9 detail albedo. It is a real, readable improvement in that shot. Its limits:
- It is double-strength. At x1 it is about half as strong.
- The lift is neutral and almost flat over the whole face, cupola included, so it reads more like a fill light on the hull than light carried off the pool.
- The soldier standing in that same pool stays black, an inconsistency a player would see.
- The campfire scene shows nothing above the smoke-animation noise.
- The stock lamp town gets worse: more wash and paler crowns.
- Smoke sprites leak warm light onto walls.
- The result depends on stand-in albedos, so a production version needs a G-buffer albedo MRT and a particle-free colour copy before it can be trusted.

Cost: the ao+GI column goes from 0.3 to 2.7 to 3.7 ms at 1440x960 on a shared RTX 4060 Ti. That extrapolates to roughly 4 to 6 ms at 1080p on an RTX 3060. Night frames today are 6 to 13 ms, so it fits the 16.6 ms budget only when gated to local-light scenes. Ungated by day it is pure cost for no picture.

Verdict: this is a good, honest negative result with one narrow positive. It is worth keeping as a later add-on for headlights, muzzle flashes, fires and explosions once a real albedo buffer exists, and once the night/lamp track makes local lights physical. It is not the lever that makes CWA look like a different game. The RTX-on jump has to come from sky occlusion that reaches objects, contact-hardening shadows, reflections and volumetrics.
