## one_line
Wet weather (lamp streaks across a rainy square, SSR puddles, low-sun wet glare) is a clear, player-visible step toward the RTX vision, and vbfix is a real, log-proven bug. Dry material reconstruction is almost invisible, and the wet path has blue-cast, corrugation and hard-edged puddle artefacts.
## critique
Provenance is solid. Every cited file exists. The logs show the v7, v8 and v9 overrides loading and compiling cleanly on the gfx-materials build. MATRESEARCH lines prove both the classifier and the vbfix bug: createVehicle props draw with buffer 0, meaning CPU-lit Classic colour inside a Remastered frame, until vbfix gives them a vertex buffer. That finding alone is worth landing. It probably affects every scripted vehicle in Guerrilla Mode, and it invalidates hull judgements other tracks made on the baseline. One overstatement: the low-sun crops cited for 'ghostly to solid' show no visible vbfix change (box deltas under 0.5). The effect is real only on sunlit faces, as in the noon jeep (70.8 to 49.7).

Look. The wet night (BEST_town_night_rain, BEST_crop_town_night_wet) is the one result a player would call a different game. Each lamp and the fire throw a separate glossy streak toward the camera, the road shines, and puddles mirror the houses. It reads as wet in a way Classic and current Remastered (a dry lawn under rain) never do. BEST_lowsun_fp_wet's glare sheet toward a low sun is also a strong, believable wet-tarmac look.

The weaknesses are real, though. Puddles have hard, often triangular outlines that follow the terrain grid, and the SSR edges are pixel-stepped with stray dark fragments. At Abel the SSR puddles read as dark stains rather than mirrors. The water film sits on a grass-textured square. The v7 Eden frame has a black sliver under a bush. The wet M113 gets heavy vertical corrugation and loses its olive. Wet paint shifts blue at noon (B > R) and colder than Classic at low sun, which breaks two owner taste rules. Distant wet ground shows thin white specular dashes that will likely shimmer in motion, and no motion test was done. The night itself is so over-exposed (mean RGB about 120) that it reads as dusk. That belongs to another track, but it means the night result has not been judged in a real night.

Dry materials are close to nothing at full frame: noon_fields differs on 1.9% of pixels, and tank optics is unchanged. What remains is a grainy albedo-emboss on near ground (a modest gain up close, likely to sparkle in motion) and HMMWV windows that turn milky-grey rather than show a convincing sky reflection. There is no bounce light, colour bleeding or sky occlusion, so the RTX-vision movement is limited to the 'water and shine' and 'night' slices, about 4/10.

Cost is fine for an RTX 3060 at 1080p. The pooled paired median is +0.37 ms (IQR -0.27 to +1.75), and SSR adds +0.15 to +0.41 ms in rain, measured honestly on a noisy shared GPU. Scaling 1.38 MP to 2.07 MP still leaves it well inside the budget.

Worth building: yes for vbfix (a must) and for the wetness, SSR and local-light-specular system. It needs soft or noise-broken puddle borders not tied to the terrain triangles, temporally resolved SSR, a per-texture terrain class table instead of colour ratios, a check that wet paint never goes bluer or colder than dry, softer hull streaks, and a motion capture to check shimmer. The dry PBR classifier is low value on its own; keep it only as infrastructure for wetness.
