## one_line
This is a real, working physically based sky that clearly improves low-sun and twilight skies (aureole, golden horizon, orange civil-twilight band) at near-zero GPU cost. It is sky-only: ground, hulls and water do not change. It also has an olive horizon band, loses some noon cirrus and cools the frame with the physical hue, so ship it with hue 0.5 and saturation of at most 1.15.
## critique
**Verification.** Everything the explorer cites exists:
- the run folders, the BEST, ALT4, VARIANTS, REJECTED and OFFLINE panels, prototype.patch and the GLSL dumps;
- the detached scratch worktree, with the same 10 modified files as the patch.

The run5 game_stdout.log confirms the prototype build ran on the RemasterUD-atmosphere data junction:
- the shaders compiled cleanly;
- all knobs returned OK;
- the sky-view table logged a 119.5 ms first build and 8 to 12 ms rebuilds.

My own PIL diffs show the change is real. The top 40 percent of the frame moves 12 to 23 levels against Remastered on every daylight view. The bottom 40 percent moves 0.00 to 1.15 levels, and town_night is identical (0.01 mean). The offline panoramas look like a textbook Bruneton/Hillaire sky, so the model itself is right.

**Look.** This is the first track result where the sky looks like a modern renderer's:
- **lowsun_fp:** a proper Mie aureole and a graded blue sky with sunlit cirrus.
- **firefight_dusk:** a convincing orange civil-twilight band under a violet sky, replacing Classic's uniform salmon.
- **coastline_sunset:** a golden horizon glow replaces the flat pink horizon polygon.

A player would notice these at dawn and dusk. They would not notice much at noon or under overcast.

**Problems I found myself:**
- **Olive band:** away from the sun in coastline_sunset, the blue-to-gold transition is muddy olive (about 134/135/117).
- **Noon clouds:** at noon the cloud mask removes most of the texture's cirrus, so the sky becomes a flatter, over-saturated cyan.
- **Water:** the claimed water reflection is not visible. The sea is unchanged and its mauve horizon now clashes with the gold sky.
- **Aerial perspective:** the fog colour change barely reaches the terrain.

**Against the RTX-on vision.** This delivers one pillar, a physically based sky, well at low sun. It contributes nothing yet to:
- light transport into the scene (atmoLight was rightly rejected for blue ground);
- reflections;
- volumetrics;
- the crushed hulls and grey low-sun ground the owner complained about.

The scene below the horizon looks exactly like current Remastered. Hence an RTX vision score around 4.

**Taste.** The physical hue cools the frame at sunrise, golden hour and dusk compared with Classic's pinks. The half-classic hue variant (ALT4) keeps the warm mood and looks safer.

**Cost.** GPU cost is negligible by construction (a few LUT taps per fragment), and the noisy alternation data does not contradict that. The CPU table rebuild (8 to 12 ms every few seconds) must move to a GPU pass, but that is routine.

**Verdict.** Worth building properly as the sky and air foundation. The sky-view table is also the right input for later sky-light, fog and water work, but only after these fixes:
- remove the olive transition;
- keep the noon cirrus;
- make the water actually reflect it;
- default to hue 0.5 and saturation of 1.15 or less.

On its own it is not a "different game" moment, because the lit world under the new sky is unchanged.
## artefacts_seen
- Olive/khaki horizon band on the anti-sun side in coastline_sunset atmoPS (rows about 220 to 275, x 0 to 300).
- Noon cirrus largely erased by the cloud mask (noon_fields atmoPS against Remastered).
- A sea-sky mismatch at the coastline horizon: gold sky over an unchanged mauve sea.
- No NaN, noise, banding, halos or swimming in the stills. The twilight gradients in firefight_dusk are clean at 8 bits.
- Temporal behaviour could not be judged: these are stills, and the 8 to 12 ms table rebuild every 0.02 degrees of sun movement would be a periodic hitch in play.
