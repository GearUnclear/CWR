## one_line
The crown-volume shader is real, cheap and loaded correctly. Outside the into-the-sun hornbeam shot it mostly makes crowns a bit greener, and the C++ canopy map mostly makes things darker. It is a modest step toward the RTX goal, plus a valuable bug find: shipped shadow maps vanish when the camera faces the sun.
## critique
Verification. The folder holds everything the report cites. The r9 game_stdout.log for both islands shows the ov8 overrides loading (vsTransform, vsScreen, vsShadow, psRemasterCommon) and a clean recompile with the shader cache saved (144 entries), with no compile errors. The canopy map built at load (Abel: 68764 trees/bushes and 1424 forest blocks in 123 ms; Eden: 29374 and 6273 in 131 ms). The per-shot knob sets (f0=1, f2=1.3, f6=150; c0=1, c1=1, c3=0.12 for vHc) appear right before each screenshot. The r3 compile failure is honestly quarantined. The debug image r9/backlit_hornbeam__dbgsunvis.png really is pure white on all geometry, so the finding that shipped cascades contribute nothing when the camera faces a low sun is real. It is the most valuable item in this report, and the shadows track should chase it.

Look. Pixel diffs against current Remastered are small almost everywhere. Mean absolute change is about 1/255, and pixels moving by more than 16/255 are under 3% in every daylight view except overcast_forest (5.4%). Night is unchanged (the 5/255 town_night diff is equal across all labels, so it is noise). The one view where a player would notice is backlit_hornbeam: the crowns go from pale grey-olive to saturated green, and the tree in front of the sun turns light lime. It reads as livelier, but more like greener leaves than light glowing through them. The crown is evenly bright rather than a dark core with a translucent rim, and the per-tree hue variation is the most convincing part. backlit_birch shows a modest yellow-green glow on the bush and cypress next to the sun. forest_edge_golden, noon_grove and overcast_forest are A/B-only differences: slightly greener, slightly darker cores. The overcast_broken flower bush loses its authored blue-grey for yellow-green, which is a recolour, not lighting. I found no artefacts (no NaNs; black-pixel counts match the baseline; no halos, banding or swimming visible in stills, though motion was not tested).

Canopy map. It turns the grey trunk wall at Everon's forest edge into a dark interior, which is a real but tiny win at normal view distance. Elsewhere it applies a broad Beer-Lambert darkening at 6.25 m texel scale. Sparse hornbeams therefore produce a uniformly dim, greyed foreground rather than distinct shadow streaks, and the pine-stand floor only gets darker (-25.6% frame luma). It is not shippable as a look, though the far-field shadow march is a sensible fallback for the missing-cascade case.

Cost. vH2 adds 0 to 1 ms (quiet-machine samples +0.0 to +0.4 ms) at 1440x960 internal with 4x MSAA; vHc adds 0.3 to 0.9 ms more, plus 43 MB of GPU texture and about 125 ms of CPU at island load. Both fit an RTX 3060 at 1080p and 60 fps easily.

Against the RTX-on vision. Leaf translucency and canopy occlusion are the right ideas, but this is still per-fragment shading on an analytic ellipsoid. There are no sun flecks on the ground, no bounce, and no visible light shafts through crowns. Most views would pass a side-by-side as 'the same game, slightly greener'. Worth building only in a reduced form: keep the ellipsoid self-shadow, per-tree variation and the translucency lobe, drop or heavily gate the chlorophyll green lean (it recolours art), and fix the camera-into-sun shadow cascade bug before investing in the canopy map.
