## one_line
A real, verified physical night rig. The moonless low-beam driving view is a large, player-visible win, but the town views are mostly just darker, with black tree crowns, mustard verges and a jeep light leak, and the RTX-style pieces (bounce, volumetrics, local shadows) failed or are missing.
## critique
Verification. Every cited image, override folder, prototype.patch (9 C++ files, 769 lines), the run_timing summary and the gfx-night-rwdi build (PoseidonGame.exe, 7:01 PM) exist. Each run's game_stdout.log shows 'shader override loaded' for the claimed ovr folder: run9 and run_timing use ovr_v8, run10 uses ovr_v9. No compile or link errors appear, and the triSetLighting night=1 knobs and NIGHT: lines are logged. The AFTER images genuinely differ from BEFORE: remastered against v9 mean absolute difference is 15 to 75/255 per view. Night vision is unchanged as claimed (1.7/255). No fabrication was found; the false claims are about interpretation (listed separately).

The look. The moonless country road from the driver's eye is the strongest result and would be noticed at once. In Classic and Remastered the whole landscape at 23:00 is a bright grey, which is plainly wrong. The prototype shows a black field, a dense starfield and a warm low beam with a hot spot and a hard cut-off. Driving at night would feel like a different game. The moon_sky view is a modest gain: stars, and crowns readable against the sky instead of black on black.

Problems:
(1) The headlit verges turn saturated yellow-olive (RGB about 100/86/61), squarely in the owner's mustard/khaki rejection. Olive grass albedo under a warm 2700 K-leaning beam produces it.
(2) The town views mostly halve the brightness. The facades stay evenly lit with no lamp pools, moon direction and shadows have no visible effect (moon at 24 deg against 69 deg differs no more than the smoke animation does), and the birch crown, which has a lamp inside it, becomes a black silhouette with 7 percent pure-black pixels. That fails both the 'only darker' and the 'flattened crowns' rules.
(3) The jeep's rear is floodlit at 5x Classic in the moonlit view. The v9 'fix' did not change it (43.2 to 43.1), and the notes wrongly blame Classic.
(4) The hill road, which is the common case on CWA islands, still lights only the yellow verges.

RTX vision. This is physically motivated direct lighting: inverse-square lamps, a beam pattern, a moon directional light with a shadow map, and Purkinje. Every piece that would make it read as path traced failed or is absent. Lamp bounce had no visible effect, the volumetric haze made a brown wash, local lights have no shadows and leak through walls, and the fire lights only its own smoke, not the ground or the buildings. Score 3.

Cost. In run10 single samples v9 adds 0.8 to 3.3 ms against Remastered at a 1.38 MP internal resolution with 4x MSAA. That is roughly 1.2 to 5 ms at 1080p if fragment bound, which fits a 3060 at 60 fps given 2 to 10 ms frames. The forward 8-light loop on every fragment is wasteful, though, and clustered culling is required. The measurements are noisy on a shared GPU.

Worth building: yes for the moon-key, inverse-square and low-beam foundation plus stars, because Classic's grey nights are simply wrong. It is not ready as is. Production needs a neutral or cooler beam on vegetation (or a lower nightLampWarm), a fix for leaf transmission so crowns near lamps glow, a trace of the jeep-rear leak, local-light shadows and a hill-tolerant beam aim. Score 5.
