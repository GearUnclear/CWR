## critique
Verification: the record is honest and backed by files. All cited artefacts exist, the patch matches the scratch worktree, run 9 was built from the final code, shaders compiled cleanly, and the AFTER shots differ strongly from BEFORE where combat lights exist and match exactly where none do. The quoted means reproduce from the raw 960x640 shots. The explorer also flagged its own weak spots: the darker flash frame, the stipple, the cross-hatch and the unmatched run 9 campfire view (combat x mean 130 against 26, an environment change).

Look: this is the most RTX-like single result I would expect from this track. In BEST_ffh the shell flash throws long radial shadows of every man toward the camera, sharp at the feet and soft at the tips, and the crater glows. Classic and Remastered both show an evenly whitened floor with a pasted sprite. In BEST_flr a parachute flare finally lights the field with falloff and long shadows that swing as it sinks; Classic lights it flat grey. BEST_ffn_fires and the steady ffn frames show the campfire and the burning wreck lighting the men and the ground, where Classic shows near-black. A player would notice this immediately at night. The isolation in run6/ffh_b02_5way shows the shadow is the ingredient that turns flat warm pools into something that reads as path traced. Muzzle (BEST_muz) is a real but fleeting gain, campfire under bright moonlight (BEST_camp) is modest, and by day the effect is correctly almost absent.

Problems a production pass must fix, two of them undisclosed. (a) Shadow-caster flipping: with two comparable fires, ShadowCaster() picks the strongest light by instantaneous flicker with no hysteresis, so the whole shadow set jumps between campfire-cast and Ural-cast on consecutive frames. In motion this is worse than the stipple. A sticky selection, or 2-4 shadowed lights as the production plan already proposes, is required. (b) A seam at the exact screen-centre column, combat mode only, 3-4.5 levels, present since round 5. It looks like a bug in the lighting or in-scatter path and is cheap to find. (c) The stipple at 1:1 is heavy; it needs the temporal or bilateral resolve the explorer lists. (d) The flash frame at eye height is half as bright as Classic in the lower third. The physics are right, but it breaks the owner's 'only darker' rule for the most dramatic moment. The production version needs the brief exposure response or flash fill the explorer proposes, and the before/after frames should be judged together. (e) Warm khaki ground under flash and a horizon glow strip behind fires are taste risks to tune.

RTX vision: it delivers emissive combat light with contact-hardening shadows from existing assets, which is a core RTX-remaster ingredient. It has no bounce or colour bleeding, no reflections of fire in water, glass or wet ground, only one shadowed light at a time, and particles are lit isotropically. That gives about 6 of 10 on the vision for this track.

Cost: the contended machine could not resolve it. The run 9 per-cycle medians reproduce at x-r -0.32 ms and x-w -0.05 ms from timings.json, against noise of several ms. The design (24 dependent depth fetches per fragment, inside one light's range, forward-shaded with overdraw) plausibly costs 1-3 ms at 1080p on a 3060. That fits the 60 fps budget, and the planned move to a half-resolution screen-space pass flattens it. Verdict: worth building properly (7/10). The gain is large and obvious at night, and the flaws are known, engineering-shaped problems rather than a dead end.
