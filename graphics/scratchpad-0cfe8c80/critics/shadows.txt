## one_line
Switching on the tree and building shadow casters (a boot-order bug fix) is the one change a player would see: dappled forest floors and long golden-hour tree shadows. PCSS and contact shadows are close to invisible, the woods get 14-21% darker and greyer than Classic, and the 4096/tight-tier setup has an unflagged near-tier shadow artefact on Sinai.
## critique
Verification. All cited files exist. prototype.patch matches the scratch worktree diff exactly. The exe (built 14:50 at 3c53141) loaded in every run: logs show version 3.05-3c53141, a clean shader compile and OK answers for objShadows, pcssSun, contact and casterLod. Before and after images really differ: rem to prod mean abs diff is 0.109 (woods evening), 0.067 (woods noon), 0.034 (town), 0.019 (forest edge), 0.003 (lowsun_fp) and 0.002 (noon_fields).

What actually changes the picture. Nearly all of the change comes from objShadows, which turns on the engine's existing static-object casters. Those were off in every capture because Scene::_objectShadows defaults to false and LoadAndApplyGraphicsConfig runs before GScene exists. This is a real and cheap finding: every Remastered picture the owner has judged lacked tree and building shadows. In woods_mixed_noon it gives believable dappled sun on the forest floor. In woods_pine2_evening and forest_edge_golden it gives long raking tree shadows. A player would notice this at a glance in wooded terrain.

Everything else in the track barely registers. PCSS at 1 degree, 4096 maps, full-LOD casters and contact shadows add 0.001-0.004 mean abs diff on top of casters. The grounding of the soldier's shadow shows only in a 4x crop. The explorer correctly calls contact shadows a near-negative, and PCSS is effectively one too.

Against the RTX-on vision. This is shadowing of direct sun only. RTX remasters read the way they do because shade is filled with bounced, coloured light. Here shade is blue-sky-only grey. Woodland frames end up 14-21% darker than Classic, the evening woods frame is cooler than Classic, and town_golden goes flat (the lawn and soldier sink into uniform dark olive and lose the readable shadows rem had). On its own it pushes against several owner taste rules. Canopy transmission or a bounce/GI term is needed before the owner will like it. It is still a necessary foundation, since any GI, AO or volumetric work needs correct sun visibility from trees and buildings.

Missed artefact. On Sinai, best's tight near tier (0.04/0.15 on 4096) puts almost the whole foreground in solid camo-net shadow behind a tier-shaped edge. Stock tiers and the doubled range both show the same ground mostly lit. prod shares those tight tiers and was never tested on Sinai. There is also an unexplained GL undefined-behaviour warning (comparison sampler on texture object 0) that appears only in this track's runs and the clouds track's.

Cost is fine for an RTX 3060 at 1080p60. The shadow pass goes from about 0.03 to 0.2-1.4 ms with prod. PCSS at 0-3 ms is unresolved but small, and the frame totals in these captures are noisy, 5-24 ms on a shared GPU.

Recommendation: ship the boot-order caster fix (cheap, high value) together with canopy transmission or a shade-fill term. Keep stock or validated tiers until the near-tier inconsistency is understood. Treat PCSS as optional polish and drop contact shadows.
