## one_line
This is a real, working world-space sky-occlusion system built from the shadow casters: soft contact pools under the Jeep and M113 on overcast close-ups. Elsewhere the frame is barely changed or just a bit darker and greyer, it is not path-traced, and it costs 2 to 4 ms.
## critique
Verification: the evidence holds up. All cited files exist, the patch reverse-applies cleanly, and the r16 logs show the prototype build loaded with its 24 sky maps and every knob accepted. My own pixel diffs match the explorer's measurement table almost exactly. There is no sign of fabrication. The overstatements are the memory figure (about 160 MiB, not 85), the timing noise (maps-off rounds spread up to 2 ms), the missing pine-woods cost (+3.9 ms), and 'trunk bases darker', which the visibility field does not show.

Look: the one honest win is overcast_close. A soft dark pool forms under the Jeep's floor and wheels and along the M113's track skirt, and both vehicles stop hovering on the grass. That is a real grounding improvement a player could notice on an overcast close-up. Even there it reads like a good AO pass, not 'a different game'. In town_day, lowsun_fp, the evening pines, noon_fields, forest_edge and night, panels 3 and 4 are effectively the same picture: under 1.5 levels of mean absolute change, with only a faint pool under the birch and Jeep. In woods the floor dims fairly uniformly and the overcast woods lose their brightest floor patches (p95 128 to 113), so they read flatter and greyer. That is the 'only darker' failure, softened by the 1.15 gain. The church wall in town_street shows blotchy, streaky darkening that a player would see as dirt or a bug. The big jumps between Classic and the final frame in every BEST image come from the baseline Remastered rig plus object shadows (panels 1 to 3), not from this track.

RTX vision: the technique aims at the right physics (world-space, off-screen-aware sky visibility with contact hardening), but in this engine the sky fill is a small share of daylight, so the payoff is inherently small. The 'one bounce' is a grey fill with no colour bleeding. Nothing touches reflections, volumetrics, sky or emissives. I score it 2 of 10 on the vision.

Cost: measured at +1.6 to +3.9 ms on what the notes call an RTX 4060 Ti at 1.38 MP. At 1080p on a 3060 (about 1.5x the pixels and a slower GPU), expect roughly 2.5 to 5 ms plus about 160 MiB of VRAM. That fits the 60 fps budget, but it is poor value for a mostly sub-perceptual change. The per-fragment forward march also scales with overdraw: leaf receivers had to be excluded because they cost +10 ms.

Worth building properly? Only as infrastructure. A camera-anchored, caster-set sky-visibility volume could later feed GI, a sky light for SSR fallback, or local exposure. On its own it is a 'nice AO' feature, not the RTX-on leap. If pursued, it needs half-res deferred evaluation, temporal rotation of the direction set to kill the wall blotches, real caster albedo for colour bleeding, and an exposure answer for woods. Score 3.
