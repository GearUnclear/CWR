## one_line
Real, working TAA infrastructure that removes the dotting from 4-tap stochastic PCSS and AO cheaply, but a player would barely see any change on its own: the only visible effect is a softer soldier shadow, exaggerated by the 2.5 deg sun.
## critique
Verification. The work is genuine. All cited files exist, the patch matches the scratch worktree, the binary post-dates the patched sources, the shaders compiled cleanly, and the TAA knobs are logged during the captures. Pixel diffs confirm the AFTER frames differ from BEFORE: mean abs 0.007 to 0.016 per view, with 33 to 40 % of pixels changed by more than 2/255. That change is tiny next to Classic to Remastered (0.074).

What a player would see. On full frames, almost nothing. BEST_town_night and BEST_overcast are indistinguishable from current apart from particle state. BEST_sinai differs only in softer, fainter leg shadows. BEST_forest shows the one real change: the soldier's shadow on the slope goes soft toward the head. The convergence and strafe sheets prove the enabler claim honestly. 4-tap stochastic PCSS without TAA is visibly dotted. Under TAA it is clean by frame 1 to 2, matches the 16-tap reference and stays clean under strafe and pan. That is a solid technical result. Two caveats undercut the presentation, though. First, the convergence sheet's 'current' panel is a glitched capture with a truncated shadow, which inflates the apparent gain; the strafe sheet's bottom row is the honest reference. Second, the 2.5 deg sun is 5x the real sun, so the softness is stylised rather than path-traced-looking. On Sinai it erases readable long shadows. TAA as AA is a sideways move: 23 to 25 % steadier on foliage, about 4 % on town and overcast, worse on overcast with MSAA, and always softer. Only TAA with 4x MSAA kept underneath preserves the antennas. Ghosting was tested only at walking pace (0.12 m per frame). Fast vehicles, rotors, the first-person weapon, particles and muzzle flashes are untested. Vehicle interiors simply turn TAA off, and the resolve runs on post-tonemap RGBA8.

RTX vision. TAA itself contributes nothing visible toward bounce light, sky occlusion, reflections or volumetrics. Its value is purely as the accumulator those effects need at 1 to 2 samples per pixel. The PCSS piece nudges the 'soft contact-hardening shadows' item, and it would look more physical with a 0.5 to 1 deg sun and per-object motion vectors.

Cost. +0.07 to 0.14 ms for the resolve is believable and trivial against a 16.7 ms budget on an RTX 3060. The world-pass numbers are noise-dominated (TAA modes repeatedly measured slower, which is likely contention). The headline saving from dropping 150 % SSAA was never measured.

Verdict. Build it properly only as shared infrastructure, bundled with a consumer (SSGI, SSR, volumetrics or stochastic AO) that turns it into a visible jump. As a standalone feature it is not worth the softness risk. For a production pass, retune the sun to 0.5 to 1 deg, add per-object motion vectors and a reactive mask, and resolve in HDR.
## artefacts_seen
No NaN, black-pixel or banding artefacts (black-pixel fraction 0.0000 to 0.0005, the same as current). There are no halos from the bounded sharpen at 0.5 to 0.8. Problems seen: 4-tap PCSS and AO dotting when TAA is off, as expected. TAA itself softens the frame. The 4-tap unblurred AO keeps a faint residual grain, as the explorer reports. Sub-pixel antennas break into fragments without MSAA. Low-sun shadows on Sinai lose contrast. No ghost trail was visible at walking-pace object motion; faster motion and particles are untested.
