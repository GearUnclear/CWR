## one_line
This is a real, working ray march occluded by the shadow map. It gives clearly visible god rays in woods at dawn and dusk and striking headlight cones at night. But the settings that show beams also veil the crowns, the clear-weather fix darkens backlit hulls, and blocky glow patches remain.
## critique
Verification: the evidence is genuine. The patch is identical to the scratch worktree diff, every probe log shows the volumetric programs compiling and the knobs accepted, and the pixel diffs are far above the run-to-run noise. Nothing is fabricated. The weaknesses are in a few over-generous self-assessments.

What works: BEST_pinewood_morn (gain 4) and BEST_pinewood_mist have diagonal shafts slanting through the pine crowns onto the grass, with a glow behind the crowns. A player would notice this at once, and it is the strongest daytime step toward the RTX look in this track. BEST_pine_eve has a strong warm beam through the cypress gap that lights the ground stripe. BEST_hl_front (VL4) is the most 'different game' frame: the Jeep's cones glow in night mist, where Remastered shows only a ground pool. In sunrise_ridge, the cypress shadow volume running across the ground mist is dramatic. The technique is the right industry approach: shadow-map, horizon and cloud occluded single scattering with HG phase, the rig's own sun chroma (so evenings stay warm), and a scene-light hookup for spotlights. The cost (0.4 to 2.4 ms contended at 1440x960, maybe 1 to 3 ms at 1080p, plus the now-real shadow pass) fits an RTX 3060 at 60 fps.

What does not: (1) The settings that show beams (mist 1.5) also lay a pale beige veil over backlit crowns. In town_eve the right-hand trees go flat and the roofs fog over. In sunrise_ridge the far cypresses become translucent ghosts and the hill washes out. In lowsun_fp the horizon band glows. These are close to the owner's 'wash' and 'flattened crowns' rejections. (2) The proposed cure, mist 0.3, loses most of the forest fans. It is also measurably darker than current Remastered on backlit hulls (15 to 19 percent), because the old screen-space shafts are removed. So the 'only adds light' claim fails exactly where it was meant to make things safe. (3) Blocky rectangular glow patches with hard vertical edges survive beside the cypresses in both final pine_eve images, contrary to the probe 6 claim. The headlight beam shows stair-stepped half-res silhouettes and a flat shelf. (4) Even with the sun behind the camera, the sunlit crowns get a mild desaturating lift. (5) Night point lights fail (brown sky). The beams are pink-white, and from the side they nearly vanish (mean-abs 0.87). (6) Fast motion and moving occluders are untested. The 0.3-degree-per-frame pan proves little.

Against the RTX vision: volumetric light is one ingredient of that look, and this delivers it in narrow situations (low sun behind trees, headlights in mist). In stills it still reads like a good modern god-ray effect with a haze problem, not path-traced air. The veil and the blockiness give it away at full size. Rough rtx_vision is 4 out of 10.

Worth building properly (6 out of 10): yes, as infrastructure. The march, the shadow-map binding and the spotlight path are the expensive groundwork, and headlights in mist are nearly shippable. Production needs: a medium driven by weather and time of day that does not darken against the old shafts (compensate, or keep a sun-glow term); a fix for the box artefacts (probably the depth-matched mean or the 250 m cap boundary); full-res or froxel edge handling; and a better answer for backlit crowns than a global mist knob, for example limiting in-scatter in front of near opaque geometry, or a height and density field that is thinner on hilltops. Object shadows must also be on by default, or nothing here works.
