## critique
Verification: the files are all real. run13 game_stdout.log shows the override dir ovr_final, psRemasterCommon.glsl (25453 B) and psPostComposite.glsl (2350 B) loaded, and the shader cache compiled with no errors. The lazy vertex buffer fired for spawned shapes (237 to 3438 vertices), and every __final shot was preceded by triSetLighting moonlight=1.01. run13s ran the untouched dist/lighting-rwdi with no override. The patch matches the scratch worktree diff, and the per-view measure tables exist and agree with the NOTES.

What the pictures show: the strongest result is a bug fix, not a lighting model. In BEST_sinai_lowsun_fp and BEST_lowsun_fp the shipped HMMWV, Jeep and M113 are grey, hazy cut-outs. In the prototype they are solid painted vehicles with readable wheels, panels and markings, because the depth-weighted in-scatter (1-exp(-z/300 m)) removes the shaft veil and the lazy buffer puts runtime-spawned vehicles on the per-pixel path. At 2x crop this is obvious, not a squint. The same-run cloud-floor fix (run14, forest_edge_noon +17% luma, slope back to Classic's sunlight) also fixes a real defect that would randomly dim about 4% of any clear day. Elsewhere the change is small. In the side-lit boxes (dawn_box, dawn_box5, lowsun_box) the prototype and shipped frames are nearly identical (mean diff about 2/255, frame luma equal). The measured shade/lit gain from 0.38-0.46 to 0.58-0.62 is real but confined to hull rear faces, and it comes from a uniform fill multiplier rather than any bounce or occlusion. The crowns get a little greener and darker with no gain in volume. Two of the BEST panels (forest_edge_noon, coastline_sunset) oversell the work because shipped and final come from runs with different cloud positions; the run13 rig-off shot already looks like final.

Artefacts: at 3x crop I found no halos at hull silhouettes from the depth lookup, no NaN, banding or noise. Watch for silhouette fringing if the AO target that carries depth is half resolution. In production, use a static buffer rather than VBDynamic for static spawned shapes.

Owner taste: no blue wash (the veil removal is the opposite of a wash), no grading, no new art. Evening hull lit faces are as warm as Classic or warmer except a 3-6% red shortfall at 4.8 deg. Low-sun ground is pink-tan, not mustard. Shade reaches the 0.6 target. The risks are the only-darker items: noon spawned-vehicle sides at 0.60 (darker than Classic), golden crowns darker than shipped, and the sunrise foreground losing its shafts.

RTX vision: nothing here adds bounce colour, reflections, contact-hardening shadows, sky occlusion on hulls or volumetrics. The fill term is a hand-tuned ambient boost, and the in-scatter change removes shaft light rather than adding volumetric depth. Score 2.

Worth building: yes, 7. The lazy vertex buffer is a correctness prerequisite. Guerrilla Mode spawns nearly every vehicle at runtime, so without it every other lighting track's work never reaches those vehicles. The clear-sky cloud floor and the depth-weighted shafts are physically right and cheap. Cost analysis: uniforms computed once per frame, about 20 ALU per foliage fragment, one extra fetch per pixel. That fits an RTX 3060 1080p60 budget easily, but a clean timing pass on an idle machine is still owed. Ship the three fixes and the hue/fill calibration as the base layer, re-pin the unit tests as the explorer lists, and don't present this as the RTX-on step.
