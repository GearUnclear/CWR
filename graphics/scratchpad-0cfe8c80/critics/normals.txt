## one_line
Real, honestly documented, but small. The load-time normal maps are a negative result on CWA's flat-painted vehicles. The useful pieces are a shader-only, low-sun gravel micro-relief that is taste-risky on sand and grass, and a valuable side finding that runtime-spawned vehicles never get Remastered per-pixel shading.
## critique
Verification: every cited file exists, and the numbers match the logs. In r6 the override_v6 shaders load and compile in both worlds ('shader cache saved (144 entries)', no errors). The prototype logs 592 and 552 companion-map generations. The late-VB path logs that it skips the sky, sun halo and clouds. The variant knob (saturation 101.12 / 1.12 / 81.12 / 91.12 / 61.12) is set before each shot. My PIL diff confirms the AFTER images really differ: as shipped to V6 mean absolute difference is 4.7 (lowsun_fp), 3.5 (Sinai lowsun), 3.4 (hull_close), 2.5 (forest edge) and 0.9 (noon). 28% of lowsun_fp pixels change by more than 4 levels, and 10% by more than 16.

Look: the one real gain is close sunlit ground at a low sun. In BEST_lowsun_fp the near gravel gets visible pebbles with lit faces and small cast shadows, the mean stays put, and the gain shows without squinting in the foreground. Everything else is a wash or worse. Hulls are unchanged at x1 and x2. Noon is unchanged. Shaded ground is unchanged, and so is the tree-covered hillside. Sinai sand becomes cobbles. The forest-edge dirt road gains black pepper specks. A player would say 'the ground has more grain at sunrise', not 'this looks like a different game'. It moves almost nothing toward the RTX-on vision: no bounce, no colour bleeding, no macro sky occlusion, no reflections, no volumetrics, no emissive light. The cavity term is texel-scale AO on a detail texture. Hence rtx_vision 2.

The most important technical point the report underplays: the best result does not use the C++ load-time normal map. Terrain gets kBumpTerrNmap = 0, and relief comes from the detail texture alpha in the shader. The companion maps, with their 5 to 6.6 s render-thread generation and RGBA16F memory, feed only objects, where there is no visible gain. Production should drop the whole texture-generation path. The terrain relief is shader-only. If it is wanted, it needs per-surface strength (much weaker on sand and grass), a distance fade, and a proper shimmer test at 60 fps with real camera motion. The 5-frame strips do not establish temporal stability for near-black, texel-frequency micro shadows.

The side finding is worth more than the track. Vehicles spawned after Shapes.OptimizeAll draw on the CPU T&L path with pre-lit vertex colour in the shipped Remastered build: the magenta diagnostic on dist/lighting-rwdi shows M113, Jeep and interior all on that path. So no per-pixel sun, no shadow receiving and no specular for every capture prop and Guerrilla spawn. That invalidates hull judgements in earlier baselines and deserves its own fix and re-review. The grey-to-tan slat shift that comes with it must be root-caused (a dropped material tint, or a CPU-path tint) before anyone credits it as evening warmth.

Cost: fine against an RTX 3060 at 1080p. Measured deltas are within about 1 to 2 ms on a noisy shared GPU, and V6 adds roughly 44 taps per terrain pixel. The load-time CPU generation is not fine (230 ms hitches), but as noted it is unnecessary for the best result. Worth building properly: the late-VB fix, yes. Terrain micro-relief: maybe, as a cheap shader polish with heavy taste tuning. Load-time normal maps: no. Overall 4.
