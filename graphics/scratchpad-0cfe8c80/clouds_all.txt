# Clouds track: lit sky clouds and sky-consistent cloud shadows

Status: done (research prototype, C++ scratch build). Verdict: promising.

## What was found about the existing sky (before any change)

- **The sky never reaches the remastered shaders.** `Landscape::Draw` draws the
  sky dome, stars, sun, moon and cloud sheets as pre-lit TL vertices *before*
  the first hardware-TL mesh calls `PrepareMeshTL -> BeginPass`, so they render
  in the screen pass through the classic programs, whatever the Lighting
  setting. Any sky work needs the remastered set forced around those draws
  (`EngineGL33::SetRemasterSkyMode`). A first prototype build that only set a
  uniform showed no change at all (run1_eden, debug views identical).
- **Two cloud systems.** (1) The sky dome `data3D\obloha.p3d` (53 points, 4
  rings) textured with the weather sky (`jasno.pac` clear, `oblacno.pac`
  broken at overcast 0.5, `zatazeno.pac` overcast from 0.66; CPU-interpolated
  between them). This is what dominates the visible sky. (2) Four huge cloud
  billboards `mrak1-4.p3d` (6.7 to 10.8 km across, flat, y = 0) placed on a
  6 km grid at 2000/4000/6000 m x CloudsBrightness, 50% occupancy per level,
  drawn at 0.1 scale toward the camera (angles preserved, so they are
  effectively at their true world positions).
- **Dome UV mapping** (dumped from the mesh in game, `CLOUDSDOME` log lines):
  u = azimuth / 180 deg (the texture wraps twice around the horizon, REPEAT),
  v piecewise by ring: elevation -15 / 0.9 / 29.5 / 59.5 / 89 deg ->
  v 1.0 / 0.987 / 0.543 / 0.174 / 0.036.
- **Why overcast 0.5 shows no cloud shadows today.** At overcast 0.5 the
  classic weather table gives skyThrough 0.7, so cover 0.3 with transmission
  0.2. The noise field tile is 6 km (`kCloudScale = 1/6000`, 256 texels), so
  its blobs are 1 to 3 km across: from eye level the whole valley is usually
  inside one lit gap (diag1 `ob_dbgsun`: almost all white), and only a high
  vantage shows the km-scale blobs (diag1 `high_dbgsun`). The field also has
  no relation to the clouds drawn in the sky.
- **Even a "clear" overcast 0 is not cloud-free:** the weather table
  interpolates overcast 0 between -0.08 (jasno, through 1.0) and 0.5
  (oblacno, through 0.7), so skyThrough is 0.959 and the cloud-shadow path is
  active (cover 0.041 > 0.02).

## The prototype (C++ scratch build, `prototype.patch`)

Scratch worktree `D:\Arma_CWA_worktrees\gfx-clouds` (detached at the lighting
branch tip), build `build/gfx-clouds-rwdi`, exe `dist/gfx-clouds-rwdi`.
Everything is gated by two new tuning knobs, so one build shoots Classic,
current Remastered and the prototype in the same game session:
`triSetLighting "skyClouds=0|1"` and `"cloudShadowSource=0|1"` (0/0 is the
current Remastered exactly). Research knobs `r0..r7` reach the shaders as
`litKnob0/1` (0 = built-in default): r0 deck altitude (m, 1800), r1 shadow
density threshold (0.55), r2 its half width (0.12), r3 dome march step (UV,
0.006), r4 relief gain (1.8), r5 silver-lining gain (1.0), r6 sky drift
(degrees), r7 sky debug view (1 density, 2 relief, 3 view direction, 4 march
direction).

1. **Remastered programs for the sky.** `Engine::SetRemasterSkyMode(mode)`
   (new virtual, GL33 override) is called by `Landscape::DrawSky` /
   `DrawClouds` around the dome (mode 1), the cloud sheets (mode 2) and the
   sun disk (mode 3). It flushes the queue, forces the remastered program set
   for those pre-lit draws (they come before `BeginPass`), uploads the mode and
   the camera basis (`litSkyRight/Up/Fwd`, viewport) and restores afterwards.
   The VSScreen fragments get their world view direction from `gl_FragCoord`.
2. **A cloud-density map derived from the weather sky texture**
   (`EngineGL33::UpdateSkyDensityMap`, CPU, rebuilt only when the sky texture
   or its weather interpolation changes; `TextureGL33::GetPixels` decodes the
   whole mip once, with the interpolation). RGBA8 256x256 on unit 6:
   r = broken cloud (whiteness `(r+g)/2b` above the clearest sky of the same
   row: the skies are painted panoramas whose clear blue pales toward the
   horizon, so a global colour test called the horizon haze cloud and missed
   the bluish streaks overhead, see `assets/density_v3.png`), g = the row is
   grey all the way round (overcast deck candidate, gated in the shader by
   skyThrough so the pale horizon rows of a clear sky are not cloud),
   b = darkness (thicker cloud). No new art: it is computed from the shipped
   textures at runtime.
3. **Lit sky clouds** (`skyCloudShade`, dome and sheets): a 6-tap march toward
   the sun *in texture space* (the great-circle step toward the sun is taken
   to pixels and then to UV through the screen-space UV derivatives, so it
   works for the dome and the billboards alike and for a sun behind the
   camera). `relief = density_here - mean density toward the sun`: positive on
   sun-facing edges (lit, tinted with the rig's sun chroma, so warm at dusk),
   negative on the far side (shaded). It is mean-free over a field, so the sky
   keeps its average brightness (owner taste: no "only darker"). Thick broken
   cloud in front of the sun darkens (seen against the light); the sun-facing
   rim near the sun gets a Henyey-Greenstein (g 0.8) silver lining. Overcast
   (low skyThrough) flattens the direct part.
4. **Sky-consistent cloud shadows** (`domeCloudCover`): a lit point is
   projected along the sun to a deck at r0 metres; the camera's direction to
   that deck point is mapped to the dome's UV (the mesh mapping above) and
   looked up in the density map. The cloud the player sees in the sky is the
   one whose shadow lies on the ground, and the sun disk dims (mode 3) when the
   dome has cloud at the sun's direction, consistent with the shadow on the
   player. The mean transmission is the classic 0.2 under a covered sun, as
   today. One texture tap per lit fragment.
5. **Sky drift (r6)**: the dome texture turns about the zenith by r6 degrees;
   the visible clouds, their lighting and their shadows share the offset, so
   shadows sweep across the ground as the sky moves.

## Results (run5, 960x640, 150% scale, 4x MSAA, same game session per view)

Variants: **A** defaults (deck 1800 m, threshold 0.55, relief 1.8, silver
1.0); **B** stronger relief 2.8 and silver lining 1.8; **C** = A with a lower,
denser shadow deck (1200 m, threshold 0.42).

- `BEST_coast_oc_up.png` (sun 8 deg, overcast 0.5): the sun-facing undersides
  of the cloud bands glow peach and gold, the tops away from the sun go slate
  blue. The clouds read as lit volumes instead of a flat painted sheet. The
  strongest result of the track.
- `BEST_sunrise_oc.png` (sun 5 deg, overcast 0.5): cloud bases facing the sun
  catch pink sunrise light. Classic and today's Remastered show the same
  texture, flatly tinted.
- `BEST_broken_up.png` (sun 54 deg, overcast 0.5, looking up): the streaky sky
  becomes layered and sculpted, with shaded bases and bright sun-side rims.
  The most "different game" frame and also the riskiest: some edges read a
  little glossy or painterly (a bright rim line with faint colour fringes).
- `BEST_broken_high.png`, `BEST_overcast_broken.png`: cloud shadows that belong
  to the clouds drawn in the sky lie across the hills (long soft bands, from
  the broken-sky streaks). Today's noise field also shows shadows from 250 m
  up, but they have no relation to the sky; from eye level the prototype
  shows lit and shaded hills where today's frame is uniformly lit. In the
  matched overcast_broken frame the camera itself ends up under a cloud (the
  dome has cloud at the sun's position), so the foreground is shaded: ground
  band mean luminance 0.341 to 0.247 (`luminance_check.txt`).
- `BEST_overcast_heavy.png` (overcast 0.9): deeper bellies and lifted thin
  gaps in the deck. A modest change.
- `SEQ_broken_high_drift.png` / `.gif`: with the sky drift knob stepped 0 to 15
  deg, the sky turns and the shadow bands sweep across the slope (static knob
  steps standing in for a wind-driven drift).
- `cmp_clear_sanity.png`: the matched clear-sky views (coastline_sunset,
  sunrise_ridge at overcast 0) are unchanged by the prototype.
- Sky brightness is kept: the sky-band mean luminance is within 0.01 of
  today's at midday and 0.02 to 0.04 brighter at golden hour; its standard
  deviation (cloud contrast) rises 30 to 45% (`luminance_check.txt`). Not
  "only darker".

## GPU cost (honest)

The machine ran 3 to 4 other agents' games throughout, so single shots are
noisy (Classic broken_high read 51 ms in one run and 30 ms in the next).
Interleaved test `run5_perf` (same view, modes alternated, 120 frames each,
GPU frame ms):

| view | Remastered now | proto A | sky only | shadows only | now again | proto A again |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| overcast_broken | 9.08 | 9.93 | 9.80 | 7.85 | 7.26 | 7.48 |
| broken_high | 13.69 | 14.25 | 14.17 | 14.73 | 17.59 | 17.95 |
| sunrise_oc | 5.38 | 37.09 | 33.46 | 44.96 | 41.62 | 37.62 |

Back-to-back pairs give **+0.2 to +0.9 ms** per frame. The sunrise view's world
time jumps from 5 to about 40 ms within the session *with the prototype off*
(`remcur2` 41.6 ms), so the earlier "+18 to +40 ms at sunrise" readings were
that drift (the known sunrise_ridge world-pass problem or GPU contention), not
the prototype. The density map is rebuilt twice per session (on weather
change: a 256x256 CPU decode and blur, logged as `sky cloud-density map ...
rebuilt`).

## Verdict

Promising. Lit sky clouds are the biggest visible win of this track: at dawn
and dusk the existing painted sky becomes sun-lit cloud volumes with warm
undersides, for under a millisecond, using only the shipped sky textures.
Sky-consistent cloud shadows work and are cheap (one texture tap per lit
fragment), but they move with the camera (see production needs) and their
shapes are the long streaks of the broken-sky texture, so they read as bands
rather than cumulus patches.

## What a production version needs

- Keep the sky-mode hook, but give the sky draws a proper remastered sky pass
  instead of forcing the program set inside the screen pass. Classic stays
  bit-identical because every new path is gated by `_lightingActive` (the
  `DrawClouds` split is a pure rename); this prototype was not pixel-diffed
  against Classic.
- Move the density-map builder from the GL backend into a pure-math
  `Graphics/Remaster` unit with a test (row baseline, deck flag, blur), built
  on weather change off the render thread.
- Drive the sky drift from the wind integrator that already moves the noise
  field, and decide on purpose whether the visible dome may move (Classic's
  dome never does).
- Shadow anchoring: the dome is camera-centred, so its shadow pattern
  translates with the camera. Anchor the projection to a slowly following
  point (re-centre after a few km with a cross-fade) or turn the cloud layer
  into a real world-space deck. At infantry speed this is invisible; in an
  aircraft or a fast car it would show.
- The `mrak1-4` billboards (world-anchored, 6 to 11 km across) get the lit
  shading but no shadows yet. `Landscape::CollectCloudSheets` (scaffolding from
  the earlier agent, in the patch, unused) lists them at their true positions
  for a sheet shadow pass.
- Tests: the LitConstants layout test for the new slots (`Sky`, `SkyVp`,
  `SkyRight/Up/Fwd`, `Knob0/1`), glslang compile, a density-map unit test on
  the three weather skies, and a capture view looking up at a broken sky.

## Risks against the owner taste list

- No blue wash (sky mean unchanged), and not only darker (sky mean kept; the
  ground is darker only where a cloud really shadows it). Dusk clouds take the
  rig's sun chroma, so they are warm, not neutral.
- The midday broken sky (`broken_up`) can read glossy or painterly: bright rim
  lines with faint colour fringes. Variant A is the safer gain; B is too
  strong at midday but good at dusk. Scaling the silver lining down with sun
  elevation would help.
- A frame where the camera sits under a cloud is darker on the ground (the
  existing cloud transmission of 0.2). With drift this changes over time, but
  a still can look "darker" to the owner.
- The shadow shapes follow the painted texture's streaks: long bands, not
  cumulus patches.

## one_line
The prototype is real and verified. Its lit clouds give a clear win at golden hour: the flat painted sky becomes salmon-lit cloud bands. Midday turns embossed and iridescent, overcast barely changes, and in stills the sky-matched shadows mostly show up as different placement or a darker frame.
## critique
Verification: the evidence is solid. All cited paths exist. The patch matches the scratch worktree diff line for line, the build log ends cleanly, and the game logs show the density map being built and every knob accepted. Pixel diffs against today's Remastered confirm the effect. Sky-band diffs are 11 to 19/255 at golden hour and midday (sunrise_oc, coast_oc_up, broken_up); ground diffs are about 23/255 where the camera falls under a projected cloud. The clear-sky views stay within 0.35/255 of today, so the sanity claim holds. The perf analysis is honest: draw calls climb from 1989 to 11172 in the sunrise session with the prototype off or on, so object streaming explains the jump.

Look: the best result is the low sun. In sunrise_oc and coast_oc_up the existing painted bands pick up salmon and peach light on their sun-facing edges, and their bodies go darker slate. A player would notice this, and it is warm, consistent with the light rig, and adds no new art. Even so, it is an emboss filter on a painted panorama (density here minus density toward the sun), not a volumetric light model. That shows at midday: in broken_up the rims turn into glossy, iridescent streaks (green, yellow and pink fringes in a 2x crop), and the bright white wisps turn into a grey-blue marbled sheet that reads stormy and painterly rather than sunlit cumulus. The overcast deck change (heavy_up) is only visible when you squint, and the matched overcast_heavy ground view does not change.

Cloud shadows: the idea is right (the cloud you see is the cloud whose shadow you stand in), and the drift strip proves the shadows sweep with the sky. In stills, though, today's Remastered already shows noise-field shadows from height (broken_high), so the prototype mostly moves them. In the eye-level overcast_broken frame the main visible change is a darker, flatter foreground: the jeep loses its sun shadow, which risks the owner's 'only darker' rejection. The camera-centred dome projection also means the shadow pattern moves with the camera, which aircraft and fast vehicles would expose. The report states this honestly, but it is a real production problem, not a footnote. The long streak shapes from the painted texture read as bands, not cumulus patches.

RTX vision: this nudges the sky and atmosphere area, giving clouds that respond to the sun at dawn and dusk, but it is not path-traced-looking light. There is no volumetric scattering, no light shafts through cloud gaps, and the cloud shadows are a 2D lookup with the existing 0.2 transmission. Score 3.

Cost: about 0.2 to 0.9 ms in interleaved pairs, with 6 density taps on sky pixels and 1 tap per lit fragment. That fits an RTX 3060 budget easily.

Worth building (5/10): ship the lit sky clouds gated to low sun elevation, scaling relief and silver lining down hard as the sun rises to remove the midday emboss and iridescence. Treat the sky-matched shadows as a second step that needs a world-anchored deck before it beats today's noise field visibly. Add a still where the camera is in sunlight with shadowed hills nearby, so the owner does not see only the darker frame.
## artefacts_seen
Iridescent pastel colour fringes on bright rim streaks at midday (broken_up, A and B), with a glossy, embossed and painterly cloud surface. Some white bands near the sunrise horizon look slightly chrome-like. I saw no NaNs, banding or dome-triangle seams in run5 (the run4 seams and banding are gone). The cloud-shadow edge on the right hill in overcast_broken is soft and plausible. Swimming cannot be judged from stills, but the camera-centred shadow projection will translate with the camera by design.
