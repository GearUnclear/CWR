# SSR track: screen-space reflections (research prototype)

Status: finished for this round (2026-10-17). Final run `runs/r11` (shader `glsl_v12`,
binary built from the scratch worktree `D:\Arma_CWA_worktrees\gfx-ssr`, patch in
`prototype.patch`). Every image named below exists in this folder and was looked at.

## Verdict in one paragraph

Promising, with a clear split. **Water and wet streets are the strongest "RTX on" result
of this track**: a calm bay now mirrors the boat, the shore rocks and the green hill with
moving ripple distortion (`BEST_bay_offshore.png`, `bay_offshore_sequence.gif`), and a
rainy town street becomes wet asphalt with puddles mirroring the houses, the lamp posts and
the dusk sky (`BEST_town_rain.png`, `ALT_town_wet_dusk_lampsoff_r10.png`,
`BEST_town_wet_dusk.png`). Both read as a different game
while still being CWA, and both come from existing models and textures only. Open-sea
sunsets get a real glitter path, but the sea turns pale lilac-grey (`BEST_coastline_sunset.png`),
which may hit the owner's "wash" objection. Vehicle paint reflections are a near-null
result on CWA's matte olive hulls (`detail_lowsun_m113.png`), and the night street is weak
because the Remastered night is already too bright (no lamp highlight streaks).
Cost: the SSR pass (tag readout + frame mip chain + march + composite) adds 0.4 to 1.9 ms
GPU at 1440x960 (960x640 at 1.5 render scale, 4x MSAA).

## Technique

All of it sits at the world/HUD boundary (`EngineGL33::EndWorldScene`), before AO, bloom
and shafts, so reflected highlights bloom.

1. **Surface classification from the stencil (C++).** Every world draw already writes the
   stencil (ALWAYS / REPLACE) for the projected-shadow exclusion. The prototype sets the
   stencil reference to a tag for the draw in flight; a later draw over the same pixel
   replaces it, so the tag always belongs to the visible surface. Codes (top three bits):
   - `0x80` water: the sea is a PSDetail landscape draw (water track finding), told apart
     from terrain by its glossy material (specular luminance about 0.6 vs 0.08);
   - `0x40` terrain (PSDetail / PSGrass mesh draws);
   - `0x60` road / on-surface decal (PSNormal with `SurfaceMode::OnSurface`);
   - `0xA0` vehicle body: `Transport::Draw` raises a global object-class flag around
     `base::Draw` (with `FlushQueues` before and after, because CWA vehicles are drawn
     through the queued non-T&L path, not `EmitDraw`; found with debug logs in
     `runs/dbg1` and `runs/r7`); the GL33 draw sites re-tag when the flag changes;
   - `0x20` other glossy material (specular power > 0: rifles).
   The shadow-volume stencil test now masks only the low 5 bits, and draws that write no
   depth and carry no tag (sun and lamp flares, particles, 2D quads) get a zero stencil
   write mask. Without that mask the sun glare quad wiped the tags in a big screen-aligned
   rectangle (the box artefact in `runs/r2`).
   Some sea segments are drawn with the terrain material; the shader re-classifies a
   terrain pixel as water when it lies at sea level over ground below sea level (terrain
   height texture).
2. **Tag readout:** three fullscreen draws with a stencil EQUAL test into an RGBA8 tag
   texture (the resolved depth-stencil is attached to its FBO).
3. **Cone blur source:** the resolved frame blitted into a full mip chain.
4. **March** (`psPostSsr.glsl`): view-space ray from the depth buffer, linear in screen
   space with perspective-correct depth, up to 80 steps, interval-overlap hit test with a
   distance-scaled thickness, 6-step binary refine. Miss handling mattered more than the
   march itself:
   - a ray that leaves the frame over open sky takes that sky pixel (the sky continues past
     the edge); otherwise a sky estimate from the nearest open sky at the frame edge or the
     remastered fog colour toward that direction;
   - a ray that leaves sideways over geometry standing above its start takes the edge pixel
     (fixed the vertical seam where a mirrored hill met the frame edge);
   - a ray that slipped behind a surface within 3 m of its start (under a jeep, behind a
     kerb) returns a dark estimate instead of the sky behind it (removed the white flecks
     under vehicles on wet roads);
   - paint reflections that leave the frame sideways or back toward the camera get no
     fallback (only upward rays fall back to the sky).
5. **Water:** one mirror trace with the flat surface, then four octaves of drifting
   gradient-noise ripples distort the mirror image: the rippled reflection direction is
   re-projected at the traced distance (correct parallax, no per-pixel re-trace, so no
   holes and no noise), and the sub-pixel ripple slope stretches the reflection
   vertically (7 taps). Fresnel uses the rippled normal with a masking floor (the
   effective cosine never drops below 1.5x the slope rms) so a rough sea does not become a
   perfect grazing mirror. Composite: `mix(frame, reflection, min(F * ssrWater, 0.85))`,
   faded by fog and by shallow water (terrain height). The sun disc and glare are in the
   frame, so the reflected sun becomes a glitter path on its own.
6. **Wet ground:** wetness = `ssrWet` override or the engine rain density (Eden overcast
   0.9 with rain on gave 0.999 in the log). Puddles from footprint-filtered value noise,
   only on flat ground: roads pool water (threshold 0.61), bare ground a little (0.67),
   grass none (grass detected by colour). Puddles are level mirrors with small rain
   ripples; wet asphalt is darkened (x0.8) and takes a rough (cone 0.10 rad) vertically
   streaked reflection; wet grass only a faint sheen. Fresnel with a roughness cap so
   rough wet ground never reaches mirror reflectance at grazing.
7. **Vehicles:** depth-derived normal, rough paint (Fresnel cap 0.65, cone 0.10 rad,
   weight 0.4 dry), wet up-facing panels smoother and stronger.

Knobs (`triSetLighting key=value`): `ssr` (0 off, the default), `ssrWater`, `ssrWave`
(ripple slope, 0.35 = calm), `ssrWet` (-1 = engine rain), `ssrGlossy` (paint), `ssrDebug`
(1 tags, 2 reflection only, 3 hit kind), `ssrRough`.

## GPU cost (r11, `timings_final_r11.md`)

The `ao` timer section holds AO plus the whole SSR pass. SSR adds (SSR shot minus the
Remastered shot of the same view): bay 1.8 ms, coastline 1.2 ms, town_rain 0.9 to 1.2 ms,
town_wet_dusk 1.3 ms, street_night 1.1 ms, town_night 1.5 ms, lowsun_fp 0.35 ms dry (only

## What a production version needs

- A real per-draw material class instead of a global flag in `Transport::Draw` (a pass id
  or a per-object class on the draw descriptor), plus glass (alpha-blended vehicle and house
  window sections) as its own smooth class.
- Coordinate with the water track: the forward sea shader already adds a smooth sky term;
  the SSR result should replace that term (pass it the traced reflection, or let the
  forward shader skip its sky term when SSR is on) instead of blending over the frame.
- Wetness as world state that ramps up in rain and dries afterwards (puddles last longer
  than the sheen), and a puddle mask from terrain curvature and road geometry rather than
  value noise.
- Half-resolution trace with Hi-Z, bilateral upsample, temporal accumulation (the march
  uses a static per-pixel jitter; motion was only checked on water).
- Edge hits faded by distance from the edge (the 2 px line on the bay). The gunner optics
  and the vehicle interior have no world depth and keep SSR off (already gated by
  `worldDepth`).
- Night wet streets need analytic point-light speculars on wet ground (lamp streaks); SSR
  alone cannot supply them, and the night exposure fix comes first.
- Classic bit-identical: tags are applied only when `_remasterPrograms && ssr > 0`, but the
  shadow-volume stencil compare mask was changed from 0xFF to 0x1F unconditionally
  (identical in practice because the top bits are zero in Classic; not yet proven by a
  bit-diff). The debug `LOG_INFO` lines in `TransportCore.cpp`, `EngineGL33_Queue.cpp`
  and `EngineGL33_VertexBuffer.cpp` are prototype only.

## Risks against the owner's taste list

- "Blue haze or a wash": reflective water is lighter and bluer than Classic's slate sea,
  and the open sea at sunset turns pale lilac-grey. `ssrWater 0.6` and the masking floor
  limit it; the owner should judge `BEST_coastline_sunset.png` and `BEST_bay_offshore.png`.
- "Only darker": wet ground is darkened (x0.8 asphalt, x0.8 to x0.92 terrain) but gets the
  reflection on top; on grass the result is mostly a slight darkening.
- Warm evenings: the dusk street keeps the warm sky in the puddles; the sunset sea loses
  some of the warm column.
- The puddle pattern could read as procedural if it repeats; it is world-anchored and
  footprint filtered.

## critique
Verification. Every cited path exists. All seven BEST/ALT images are byte-identical to their source cmp_*.png files in runs/r11, and the ALT image matches runs/r10. The game logs in runs/r11/tri/ssr_abel4 and ssr_eden show the v12 override loaded (20319 bytes), followed by 'GL33 post: SSR prototype programs built' and per-frame 'SSR pass 1440x960' lines. The ssr_eden log reports engine rain at wet 0.9993. The logs contain no errors, and tri passed 2/2. Pixel diffs against Remastered in r11:
- bay ssrcalm: mean abs 16.7, with 40% of pixels changed by more than 24.
- coastline ssrw06: 5.4.
- town_rain ssrwet: 6.4.
- town_wet_dusk: 9.3.
- overcast: 3.3.
- lowsun dry paint: 0.11, with only 0.2% of pixels changed by more than 8. The paint result is null, as the explorer admits.
The four bay sequence frames differ only in the water rows (about 4.5 mean against 0.03 in the sky), so the ripples really animate.

Look. The bay is the honest win. The patrol boat sits on its own reflection, and the shore rocks and green hill are mirrored and broken up by ripples. A player would notice at once, and it reads like a modern water shader on unchanged CWA assets. The daytime rain street is the second win: the half-timbered house, the lamp post and the jeep appear in road puddles, while the grass verge stays dry. The r10 lamps-off dusk frame, with a pink sky in a flooded street and black silhouettes, is the most RTX-like image here. It uses v11, though, and it depends on a lamp state the final run did not reproduce.

The rest is weaker than reported.
- Coastline sunset: the sea turns pale lilac-grey with less contrast and a weaker warm column. That is the owner's 'wash'. The only clear gain is the removal of Remastered's dark wedge.
- Lamp-lit dusk and night streets: the asphalt becomes a light-grey sheet with no lamp streaks. The town_night fire is not reflected at all, so night and combat get essentially nothing.
- Paint: no visible effect.
- Overcast rain: blotchy grey sheen patches and pale puddle-like spots appear on grass. A hill that the fog hid comes back as a pale streaky shape, and a box appears under the bush.
- lowsun wet: hard-edged translucent rectangles under the jeep and around the soldier lighten the shadows.
- Frame edges: both the left and right edges of the bay show vertical seams, and the town views have edge bands or blocks. In motion, with camera pans, these would read as classic SSR edge artefacts. Temporal behaviour under camera motion was never tested; the static per-pixel jitter and the edge seams are the likely failure points.

RTX vision. Reflective water and wet streets are a core part of the RTX look, and the stencil-tag classification is a clever way to get material classes out of a fixed-function era renderer. What is missing: glass, gloss on paint, emissive and lamp reflections at night, and fire on wet ground. The sea also needs to coordinate with the forward water shader rather than blend over it. That puts it mid-scale, around 5.

Cost. The ao+ssr deltas in timings_final_r11.md match the claims: +0.35 to +1.9 ms at 1440x960 internal with 4x MSAA. Scaled to 1080p and with no Hi-Z yet, the worst case is perhaps 2.5 to 3 ms, which fits an RTX 3060 at 60 fps. The FlushQueues around every Transport::Draw is an unmeasured CPU and batching cost.

Worth building properly. Water reflections alone justify a production pass with these parts:
- Hi-Z at half resolution, with a temporal pass.
- Fading by distance from the frame edge.
- Fog-correct composite.
- A sea reflection fed into the forward water shader instead of a blend over the frame.
- A per-draw material class in place of the global flag.
- Puddle masks from road geometry, not value noise.
The sunset-sea tuning has to keep Classic's contrast and warmth, or the owner will reject it as a wash.
