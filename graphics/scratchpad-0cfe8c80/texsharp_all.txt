## Verdict

**Promising, for terrain at 10 to 150 m.** Texture filtering itself is not
broken: anisotropy is already 16x, residency is not the limit, the stored mip
chains are fine. The blur the owner sees is missing information: every terrain
square is one 128x128 texture over 50 m (39 cm per texel), so from about 10 m to
150 m one texel covers 5 to 15 render pixels and bilinear magnification paints a
smeared slope, and the 128 px grayscale detail texture has averaged itself flat
by 20 m. The fix that works is to synthesise the missing band from the same
texture: **locality-preserving self-detail** (each terrain fragment gets the
luminance high-pass of its own square's texture, zoomed 6x, read from within
about 2 m of that spot, so grass gets grass grain and rock gets rock grain).
Combined with keeping the detail texture's contrast at distance and a light
luma-only CAS, the Abel grass slopes go from airbrushed to grassy. It is the
single biggest change on `forest_edge_golden` (see `BEST_forest_edge_golden.png`),
clear on `overcast_broken` meadow and `noon_fields` hillside, modest on Sinai sand
and on the near gravel of `lowsun_fp`. Mean colour and hue are unchanged
(ground mean luminance +0.1% for self-detail alone, +1.0 to 1.5% with detail
kept, R/G and G/B identical to 3 decimals), so it is not a darker or more
mustard look; it only adds texture.

What it does not fix: distant terrain beyond about 300 m (the base texture is
then at about 1 texel per pixel, nothing left to add), and object textures
(vehicles, trees), which are already sampled correctly.

Override check: every run's `game_stdout.log` shows `shader override dir =`
the intended glsl folder and `shader override loaded` for
`psDetailRemasterMain.glsl`, with no `[ERROR]`, compile or link failure, and
every `triSetLighting` knob answered `res=OK` (runs 6, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13).

## The self-detail technique (glsl7 `psDetailRemasterMain.glsl`, `tsSelfDetail`)

In base-texel space p = uv * 128, a grid of cells of 12/zoom texels (2 texels =
78 cm at zoom 6). For each of the 4 cells around the fragment, the patch is the
square's own texture around that cell, zoomed: q = c + R(cell) * (p - c) * zoom
+ jitter(cell), so a cell shows the texture from within 6 texels (2.3 m) of
itself, 6x smaller (R is a random rotation per cell in glsl7). The value kept is
the relative luminance high-pass: (lum(textureGrad(q, 6x derivatives)) -
lum(textureLod(q, log2 6))) / that local mean. The 4 cells are blended
variance-preserving (sum w h / sqrt(sum w^2), Heitz-Neyret) so the blend does not
wash the grain out, the result goes through the soft limiter and multiplies the
albedo (1 + m). Because textureGrad gets the true 6x derivatives, the added
detail mip-maps away by itself once the zoomed sample would be minified past the
zoom scale (about 150 to 300 m), and it never adds anything finer than the
screen can hold. 8 texture fetches per terrain fragment.

Why it suits CWA: the terrain is per-square material textures (grass, rock,
sand, dirt) at 128 px, so the texture at a spot is the best available example of
what finer detail of that material looks like. Locality keeps material borders:
a cell 2 m from a road reads road. Only luminance is modulated relative to the
local mean, so hue and average brightness stay as authored (measured above).
No new art; runtime-derived only.

`K2` "detail kept": the detail texture's deviation from its 1x1-mip mean is
scaled by 2^(K2 * lod / 2) (cap 4x, faded back to 1x between 150 and 400 m) so
the authored grain survives the mip averaging. `K1` adds a second detail sample
on a rotated, rescaled grid (deviation rescaled by 1/sqrt 2) so the 1.56 m tile
does not print as a grid once its contrast is kept.

CAS: AMD FidelityFX CAS (3x3 form) in the composite, before AO and glow; glsl6+
applies it to luma only (`udTexsharp.y`), which stops pale, slightly coloured
ground textures from turning into RGB speckle.

## GPU cost

The machine runs 4 games at once for other agents on one RTX 4060 Ti, so a
single shot's GPU time swings by 2x (see the panels: Remastered stock ranges
17 to 39 ms on noon_fields across runs). Single-shot numbers on the panels are
labelled noisy and should not be read as cost.

Cost probe (run10, `glsl6amp`): the self-detail evaluated 16 times per terrain
fragment vs stock, alternated 6 times per view, paired deltas:
forest_edge_golden +5.64 ms (pairs +5.8, +6.3, +5.5, +5.4, +5.8, +2.7),
lowsun_fp +5.51 ms (+5.8, +2.7, +10.0, +5.2, +4.5, +9.7), noon_fields +4.83 ms
(too noisy: -17.3 to +23.9). So **one self-detail evaluation costs about 0.35 ms**
at 1440x960 with 4x MSAA under contention (likely less on a quiet GPU), about
0.5 ms projected at 1080p native. The single evaluation (shade) was within noise
(-0.8, +1.1, -3.7 ms medians). CAS adds 0.02 ms in the composite (0.03 to 0.05 ms,
consistent across all runs). Detail kept + anti-tiling is 2 more fetches, not
separable from noise. Built up from the probe: about 0.5 to 0.6 ms for the
whole final variant (self-detail 0.35, detail kept and anti-tiling about 3
fetches more, rotation ALU, CAS 0.02).

Direct paired timing of stock vs final, 6 alternations per view (200 frames
each): run9 (unrotated final, busy machine) medians +2.42 / -0.29 / -2.82 ms
(forest / lowsun / noon); run13 (rotated final, quieter machine, stock forest
6.9 ms) medians +1.14 / +2.46 / +1.39 ms with single pairs from -9.5 to +8.6 ms
(`run9/timing_summary.md`, `run13/timing_summary.md`). Honest range: **0.5 to
about 2 ms** at 1440x960 4x MSAA; the amplified probe (large, consistent delta)
points to the low end, the direct pairs cannot resolve it. Needs a quiet-GPU
measurement before production.

## Motion and shimmer

Camera walks 0.25 m per frame for 8 frames (`abel_motion.test.sqf`).
`motion.md` = frame-to-frame change; `shimmer.md` = that change divided by the
spatial detail (Laplacian) per band, vs stock. Added detail that moves
coherently raises both; aliasing raises the ratio. All variants stayed within
0.65x to 1.24x of the stock ratio (run7, run9; run13 rotated: self-detail 0.79x
to 1.20x, final rotated 0.80x to 1.04x, `run13/shimmer.md`);
no variant shows the 2x+ jump that real shimmer would give. Self-detail's near
band ratio is 1.14 to 1.24 (slightly more temporal change per unit detail), its
difference image (`run9/forest_edge_golden__motion_diff.png`) shows coherent
streaks, not sparkle (rotated: `run13/forest_edge_golden__motion_diff.png`,
`run13/noon_fields__motion_diff.png`). The earlier combination with CAS 0.5 and LOD bias -0.5
gave dense speckle in the difference image (`run7/...motion_diff.png`), which is
why the final drops the bias and lowers CAS to 0.3 luma. A real in-game
fly-through is still needed to judge crawl at 1080p without SSAA.

## Risks vs the owner's taste list

- Not darker, not a wash, no hue shift: ground mean luminance +0.1% (self-detail)
  to +1.5% (with detail kept), R/G and G/B unchanged. Classic untouched (the
  Remastered terrain main only; the classic branch of the shader is the stock code).
- "Mustard/khaki ground": the colour does not change, but more visible grain on
  a golden-hour slope can read as drier grass. Judge `BEST_forest_edge_golden.png`.
- Sharpening: CAS 0.3 luma is mild but it is still sharpening; the
  `ALT_selfdetail_only_*` images show the result without it.
- Strength 2 and unrotated cells were rejected here for scaly or striped
  patterns; at 1.5 rotated, at 2x zoom crops, a few cells still show small
  repeated motifs on high-contrast textures.
- Roads and hard material borders inside a square: locality is 2.3 m, so a
  cell next to a sharp border can show a shrunken copy of the border; not seen in
  these views, not specifically tested.

## What a production version needs

1. Move `tsSelfDetail` (rotated, limiter, zoom 6, strength about 1.5) and the
   K2/K1 detail-kept code into the remastered terrain main in
   `EngineGL33_LightingShaders.cpp` with real uniforms (not the research
   `litLightCount.yzw` / `litCamPos.w` slots) and tuning keys next to the existing
   Remastered ones; keep the classic branch untouched.
2. CAS luma-only in the composite with a tuning key (default 0.3), or skip it.
3. Optional cost cut: 3 cells (hex grid) instead of 4, and a distance cutoff
   that skips the evaluation past 300 m where it adds nothing.
4. Correctness extras worth folding in at no visible cost: residency estimate
   scaled by render scale squared (`DrawPoly.cpp`, `texsharp::StreamFix` in the
   patch). Optionally replace the single GL_LINEAR SSAA downscale with a shader
   resolve (Catmull-Rom or Lanczos-2) when render scale > 1; not prototyped.
5. Drop the research-only parts of the patch: texRemip (Lanczos remip), texture
   stats logging, release hook, env vars.

## one_line
An honest, well-evidenced shader-only terrain micro-detail layer: the slope goes from airbrushed to grassy on Abel and Eden at 10 to 150 m for about 0.5 to 2 ms. It is invisible on Sinai sand and near gravel and adds no light at all, so it is a supporting remaster layer, not an RTX-on step.
## critique
Verification: the report is genuine. Every cited file exists. The glsl7 override and the composite override are logged as loaded in run11, run12 and run13, with no compile or link errors and every knob at res=OK. Pixel diffs show real changes. The shader does what it claims: 4 rotated cells, a textureGrad high-pass relative to a log2(zoom) local mean, a Heitz-Neyret blend and a soft limit, applied as a scalar albedo multiply in the Remastered branch only, so hue stays fixed and the Classic else-branch is stock code.

The look: forest_edge_golden is a real change a player would notice. The smeared, painted slope becomes dense dry-grass grain in the full frame, not only in a crop. The overcast_broken meadow gains turf texture, clear but moderate. On noon_fields the pale ground gets a little grittier and objects get crisper, mostly from CAS; the gain is small. sinai_desert_noon and the lowsun_fp gravel are near-identical at 2x, so the gain there is none to small. The self-detail alone adds only 12 to 41% of the proxy; the rest of the "crisper" read comes from CAS 0.3 and the K2 boost.

Artefacts: no NaN, halos, banding or embossing. Rotation does remove the stripes. Negatives:
- The K2 boost turns the forest foreground path into dark blotchy mottling that reads as grime.
- Self-detail amplifies the olive/brown patches on the Eden meadow.
- Grain on a golden slope pushes it toward the khaki/straw read the owner dislikes.

Motion: the shimmer ratio stays near stock, but that metric is normalised by spatial detail. The run13 diff image shows the whole slope becoming a field of fine frame-to-frame change. All shots are 1.5x SSAA plus 4x MSAA, downscaled, which hides aliasing. At native 1080p without SSAA, both the high-frequency grain and CAS (applied at render resolution before the linear downscale) will behave differently, and crawl is untested.

Further limits:
- The cell hash and grid live in per-square UV space. The pattern repeats with the base texture every 50 m. That adds no new tiling, but wrap sampling within about 2.3 m of a square edge breaks locality on transition squares.

The diagnosis work is valuable. It shows aniso is already 16x and that residency, mip quality, LOD bias and Catmull-Rom are dead ends, so nobody should spend time there again.

Against the RTX vision: this touches no light transport (no bounce, occlusion, reflection, volumetrics or emissives). It is the "texture upscale" slice of a Remix-style remaster, and a modest one. Cost is fine for an RTX 3060 at 1080p.

Worth building as a cheap supporting layer, with these changes:
- Self-detail at about 1.2 to 1.5.
- K2 lowered or dropped to avoid grime.
- CAS optional.
- Validated at native 1080p in motion first.
