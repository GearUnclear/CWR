# HFRT: heightfield ray tracing of the terrain (research track `hfrt`)

Short version: tracing the terrain heightfield itself (sun, sky visibility,
one-bounce GI) is a near-negative result on CWA islands. Tracing a fine
**canopy heightfield** built from the landscape objects is a clear positive
one. At golden hour every tree, bush and forest block on the island casts a
long, contact-hardening shadow, at any range, including the near range: the
shipped shadow maps cast no tree shadows at all in any view captured here.
The best images are `BEST_ground_b_1730.png`, `BEST_hill_1730.png` and
`BEST_slope_tele_1730.png`.

## Technique

The terrain is a heightfield (1024x1024 at 12.5 m on the Remaster islands,
already on texture unit 4 as `terrainHeight`), so the GPU can trace it exactly.
The shipped Remastered path reads a baked 16-azimuth horizon map: sun
visibility is the sun elevation against an interpolated horizon angle with a
fixed 1.2 degree penumbra, and sky visibility is one baked, normal-independent
number per texel.

This track has two parts:

1. **Terrain tracing** (override GLSL only): per-fragment march of the height
   texture toward the sun (angular penumbra 0.8 deg, so contact hardening by
   construction), a per-pixel normal-aware sky visibility (8 vertical slices,
   horizon-based cosine integral) and one-bounce terrain GI (the radiance of
   each slice sample that rises above the horizon seen so far, using the
   baked ground tint as albedo).
2. **Canopy heightfield** (scratch C++ + GLSL): every landscape object that can
   shade the ground at a distance is rasterised top-down into a texture, which
   the shader cone-traces toward the sun.
   - Gather (main thread, `TerrainLighting.cpp` `GatherCanopy`): every Primary
     landscape object whose map type is tree, small tree, bush, one of the
     three forest block types, building, house, church, chapel, bunker,
     fortress, tower, lighthouse, fuel station, hospital or rock. On Abel that
     is 84,395 of 98,198 objects, gathered in 16-22 ms (game logs). Footprint:
     an ellipse at 0.85 of the bounding box for crowns, the convex hull of the
     geometry LOD for forest blocks and buildings. Opacity: tree 0.8, small
     tree 0.65, bush 0.5, forest 0.95, building/rock 1.0.
   - Rasterise (worker thread, `RasterizeCanopyFine`): 4x4 samples per texel,
     r = height of the object top above the terrain (max), g = occluder density
     (coverage x opacity, combined as 1 - product of transmissions). Then a mip
     chain on the CPU: r = max of the covered children, g = mean. Uploaded as
     RG16 with all levels on texture unit 8 (`terrainCanopyFine`). Resolution
     is `CWR_HFRT_CANOPY_RES=k` texels per terrain cell: k = 4 gives 4096x4096
     at 3.125 m (608,268 texels covered on Abel), k = 2 gives 2048x2048 at
     6.25 m. The whole canopy plus horizon map is on the GPU about 1.2 s after
     the island loads (log timestamps).
   - Trace (`hfFineSun` in the override): from the receiver toward the sun,
     step length max(texel, 0.15 t), mip level log2(step / texel), so far
     occluders blur into a wider, lighter penumbra and near ones stay crisp.
     A sample occludes when the ray is below the canopy top (soft over the
     sun-disk cone width), transmission per step pow(1 - density, step /
     texel). The trace stops at 45 m / tan(sun elevation) (nothing in the
     canopy is taller than 45 m) or 900 m. Receivers more than 1.5 m above the
     ground (crowns, roofs) skip the first 6 m and get no jitter (v5 stippled
     the crowns; v6 fixed it). Ground facing away from the sun skips the trace.
   - Canopy sky occlusion (`hfFineSky`): 8 azimuths x 4 distances (2..33 m)
     around ground points, cos^2 of the steepest density-weighted canopy top.

Shader modes (`triSetLighting terrainShadows=<mode>`, read from
`litTerrainLift.z` by the override; modes <= 1.5 are the shipped path):

| mode | what it does | where |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | traced terrain sun + per-pixel normal-aware sky visibility | override_v1/v2 |
| 3 | 2 + one-bounce terrain GI | override_v2 |
| 4 | 3 + 12.5 m canopy (sun beyond the shadow maps, sky occlusion) | override_v2 |
| 5 / 6 | 4 at a lite budget / 4 with the bounce x3 | override_v2 |
| 7 / 8 | traced terrain + 12.5 m canopy sun only, baked sky / 8 with a distance-scaled step budget | override_v3 |
| 9 | 7 evaluated per vertex (vsTransformRemaster override) | override_v4 |
| 10 / 11 / 12 | traced terrain (mode 8 budget) x 3.1 m canopy trace beyond the shadow maps / + canopy sky occlusion / canopy also inside the shadow-map range | override_v5 |
| 13 | 12 + canopy sky occlusion everywhere, crown stipple fixed | override_v6 |
| **15** | **baked horizon map for the terrain (as shipped) x 3.1 m canopy trace everywhere + canopy sky occlusion of the ground** | override_v7 |
| 16 | 15 with half the canopy steps | override_v7 |
| 17 | 15 without the canopy sky occlusion | override_v8 |
| 18 / 19 | 15 + the alpha test moved before the lighting / + a 12-step trace for raised receivers | override_v9 |
| **20** | **18 with the crossing density sqrt(coverage)** (the chance that a ray crossing a partly covered texel hits the crown) | override_v10 |

`override_v10/` holds every mode; the C++ side is `prototype.patch` (applies
to the graphics branch tip in the detached scratch worktree
`D:\Arma_CWA_worktrees\gfx-hfrt`, built as `dist\gfx-hfrt2-rwdi`).

## Why it suits CWA content

CWA islands are smooth heightfields carrying tens of thousands of separately
placed trees and forest blocks. The terrain itself is too gentle for terrain
tracing to matter, but the trees are exactly the thing a top-down heightfield
represents well, and the data to build it (positions, bounding boxes, geometry
LODs, map types) already exists. No new art: the canopy is derived at island
load.

## What real data showed

All comparisons are Classic | Remastered (current) | Remastered + HFRT, same
view, same time, same run, 960x640 window at render scale 1.5 (1440x960) with
4x MSAA.

### Terrain tracing (runs smoke1, scout1, main_v2, timing_v2, v3, v4, p): mostly negative

- **Sky visibility from the terrain is about 0.9-1.0 almost everywhere** in
  these views (`DEBUG_*.png` bottom rows): the hills are gentle relative to
  their spacing. A per-pixel, normal-aware version of a number that is already
  near 1 changes nothing visible.
- **One-bounce terrain GI is invisible**: even shown x4 the bounce irradiance is
  dark (`runs/main_v2/overcast_broken__debug.png`), and with the bounce x3
  (mode 6) the frames are still indistinguishable from mode 4
  (`VARIANTS_hill_1730.png`, `VARIANTS_forest_edge_golden.png`). Terrain
  albedo is low and the sky dominates the fill. Negative result.
- **Traced terrain sun shadows** differ from the horizon map at shadow edges and
  in small-scale self-shadowing at a low sun (`DEBUG_hill_0700.png`), and in
  sunrise_ridge the shipped map puts the whole foreground in a grey half
  penumbra where the trace says full sun (`DEBUG_sunrise_ridge.png`). In the
  colour frames this barely shows, because the affected ground faces away from
  a 5-10 degree sun (low N.L). Not worth the cost.
- **12.5 m canopy** (modes 4/7/8): forest blocks shade at a distance, but single
  trees turn into blobs and diamonds (`DEBUG_hill_0700.png`, traced panel).
- **Per vertex** (mode 9, v4) is blobby and not cheaper: best GPU frame
  hill_1730 19.86 ms (mode 9) vs 4.93 (mode 8), forest_edge_golden 23.10 vs
  11.92 (`PERVERTEX_vs_PERPIXEL.png`). The trees' vertex counts make a
  per-vertex trace expensive, and terrain vertices are 12.5 m apart.
- **Variant P** (`CWR_HFRT_CANOPY_HORIZON=1`, canopy folded into the baked
  horizon map, zero runtime cost): distant forest blocks get shadows, single
  trees cannot (16 azimuths at 12.5 m).

### Fine canopy cone trace (runs v5-v10): positive

- **The shipped shadow maps show no tree shadows in any captured view.** In
  forest_edge_golden the sun-visibility debug of the shipped path shows the
  soldier's shadow and nothing from the trees 10-40 m away under a 12 degree
  sun (`runs/v6/forest_edge_golden__v6_debug.png`); the hill views show no tree
  shadows either (`runs/v5/hill_0700__dbgsun_rem.png`). Whatever the cause
  (the casters are gated in `SceneShadowPass.cpp` on the shape's NoShadow flag
  and `Object::CastShadow()`; I did not chase it), the canopy trace is the only
  source of tree shadows in these frames. That is why mode 12 (canopy also
  inside the shadow-map range) is identical to mode 10 in the hill views and
  adds the tree shadows in forest_edge_golden, where mode 10 has none.
- **Golden hour reads like a different game**: `BEST_hill_1730.png`,
  `BEST_slope_tele_1730.png` (telephoto onto the slope), `BEST_ground_a_1730.png`
  and `BEST_ground_b_1730.png` (eye 2.5 m among the summit trees). Every tree on
  the sunlit slopes casts a long shadow away from a 9 degree sun, the trees
  stand on the ground instead of floating, distant forest blocks get a lit and
  a shaded side, and in ground_b the near trees put the whole foreground rock
  slope in their shade. hill_0700 shows the same on the sunlit right slope.
  forest_edge_golden shows soft diagonal tree-shadow bands on the slope.
- **No visible change**: sunrise_ridge (the trees stand on the skyline; the sun
  debug shows no canopy shadow on the visible field, probably because their
  shadows fall behind the crest) and overcast_broken (14:30, half overcast,
  high sun; the run has mode 12, no Eden run of mode 15).
- **Shade level** (mean luminance, `runs/v7`, `runs/v10_*`): ground_b
  foreground box 101.4 (Remastered) to 79.9 (mode 15, k = 4), per channel
  shade/lit about 0.73 / 0.78 / 0.84 in display values. Not crushed; the shade
  is a little cooler than the lit rock because it is lit by the existing
  Remastered sky fill.
- **Canopy resolution** (`K_*.png`, `SQRT_*.png`): at 6.25 m with the linear
  density, thin crowns smear into texels and lose 40-55 % of the darkening
  (ground_b box 88.5 vs 79.9 at 3.1 m). The sqrt crossing density (mode 20)
  restores it: 6.25 m + sqrt gives 78.4 (ground_b), 74.6 (hill_1730 box, 3.1 m
  linear 74.5), 54.1 (slope_tele box, 3.1 m linear 54.8). So **k = 2 with the
  sqrt density matches k = 4** in strength with a quarter of the memory, a
  little softer in shape.
- **Canopy sky occlusion** (mode 17 vs 15): grey halos at tree bases in the
  debug (`runs/v5/hill_1730__v5_debug.png`), but only 0.2-0.4 levels in the
  measured boxes. Not worth keeping.
- **Traced terrain vs baked map under the canopy** (mode 13 vs 15): visually
  the same canopy shadows; the baked map is much cheaper. Mode 15 is the
  candidate.
- Mode 18 (alpha test before lighting) renders the same frame as mode 15 (mean
  absolute difference 0.08-0.30 levels on four views), as it should.
- Two views I guessed blind (forest_side_1815, forest_back_1815, run v6) had no
  trees in frame and the ground in the slope's own shadow: no information.

## GPU cost

RTX 4060 Ti, 1440x960, 4x MSAA. The GPU was shared with up to three other
agents' games for the whole session and GPU timestamps include that
contention: in run p the unchanged shipped shader measured 12-33 ms where other
runs measured 3-9 ms. Every number below is the minimum over interleaved rounds
(remastered and the variants alternate in each round), taken across all runs
(`timings_best_across_runs.md`, per run in `runs/*/timing_rounds.md`), so they
are upper bounds and the deltas are rough (+/- a few ms).

| view | Remastered | mode 8 (terrain + 12.5 m canopy) | mode 15/18/20 (production candidate) | mode 13 (traced terrain + 3.1 m canopy) | modes A/B/Can (terrain sky + GI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| forest_edge_golden | 3.69 | 11.92 | 5.98-7.12 (+2.3 to +3.4) | 13.79 | 26-34 |
| hill_1730 | 3.30 | 4.93 | 8.41-9.91 (+5 to +6.6) | 11.40 | 15.8-18.5 |
| ground_b_1730 (big trees near) | 6.32 | - | 15.32-16.78 (+9 to +10.5) | - | - |
| slope_tele_1730 (telephoto, trees everywhere) | 6.76 | - | 19.66-22.20 (+13 to +15) | 29.50 | - |

- The cost follows the number of lit pixels that run the trace, including
  every alpha-tested leaf-card fragment (the remastered shader lights before
  its alpha test). Moving the alpha test first (mode 18) did not give a
  measurable win under this much contention. A 12-step trace for crowns
  (mode 19) and half the steps (mode 16) did not either.
- **Half resolution**: not measured. I tried timing rounds at render scale 0.75
  in run v6, but `SetRenderScale` clamps below 1.0, so those `_q` rows are at
  scale 1.0 (0.44 of the pixels), and the contention noise was larger than the
  difference. The trace is per pixel and has no screen-space dependency, so a
  half-resolution sun-visibility buffer is the obvious production route, but
  this forward renderer would need a depth pre-pass for it.
- **Per vertex is not enough** (mode 9): blobby on the 12.5 m terrain grid,
  wrong for 3 m crowns, and slower on tree-heavy frames.
- Memory: k = 4 is 64 MiB RG16 plus mips (about 85 MiB); k = 2 is 16 MiB plus
  mips (about 21 MiB). CPU build about 1 s on the worker thread, 16-22 ms on
  the main thread for the gather.

## Verdict

**Promising, for the canopy part.** The terrain-only parts (per-pixel sky
visibility, one-bounce terrain GI, traced terrain sun) do not earn their cost
on these islands: keep the baked horizon map. The canopy heightfield cone trace
is the most "path traced" looking change this track found: low-sun frames gain
long tree shadows across whole landscapes, near and far, from existing data,
and it also fills the gap the shadow maps leave (no tree shadows at all in
these captures). It costs several ms per frame on a mid-range GPU and needs a
resolution-independent pass to be cheap.

## What a production version needs

1. Keep mode 20 at k = 2 (6.25 m, sqrt crossing density, baked horizon map for
   the terrain, no canopy sky occlusion). A dedicated `LitConstants` slot and a
   real option instead of the `terrainShadows` mode hack.
2. Move the trace off the forward pass: a half or quarter resolution sun
   visibility buffer from a depth pre-pass (or last frame's depth,
   reprojected), bilateral upsample, or temporal accumulation of a jittered
   quarter of the pixels per frame. Until then: early alpha test, skip
   back-facing ground (done), cap steps for crowns.
3. One fetch per step: pack the terrain height into the canopy texture (or
   store absolute tops in a second channel) instead of sampling the RGBA32F
   height texture at every step.
4. Keep the canopy current: destroyed trees and buildings need a local
   re-rasterise and a mip update of the affected texels (`glTexSubImage2D`), and
   island or mod changes rebuild with the horizon map (the same async build).
5. Crown base heights for broadleaf trees (shadow starts at the crown, not at
   the trunk), and a thin trunk term, if the near shadows are to hold up next
   to shadow-mapped objects.
6. Decide the near range with whoever owns the shadow maps: either foliage
   starts casting into the shadow maps and the canopy fades in beyond them, or
   the canopy owns tree shadows everywhere (what modes 12-20 do) and buildings
   stay in the shadow maps (today both shade buildings near the camera).
7. Classic stays bit-identical: all new code is in the remastered programs and
   one extra texture binding that the classic programs do not declare. Unit
   tests for the rasteriser and the mip rules.
8. Replace the static interleaved-gradient jitter on ground receivers with blue
   noise plus TAA, or drop it: the soft edges show a faint static dither and
   the cone steps show faint banding in the debug views
   (`runs/v6/forest_edge_golden__v6_debug.png`).

## Risks against the owner taste list

- **"Only darker"**: the change adds shadows, so shaded areas get darker while
  the sunlit ground keeps its level; where tree shadows are blurred by
  distance it can read as darker ground rather than as shadows. Shown at full
  size the structure is visible (BEST crops), but this is the main risk.
- **Cool shade**: the canopy shade is the existing Remastered sky fill, which
  is cooler than the lit ground; on the pink rock of ground_b the shade reads
  blue-grey. It is local shade, not a wash over the frame, but a reviewer who
  rejected blue haze may call it cold.
- **Crushed shade**: no; measured shade/lit about 0.73-0.84 in display values.
- **Tree crowns**: lit crowns are unchanged (raised receivers skip their own
  crown), crowns standing in a neighbour's shadow get darker. That is correct,
  but forests at a distance gain a darker shaded side.
- **Evenings**: warmth is unchanged in lit areas (the sun colour is untouched).
- Overcast and high-sun frames do not change, so the effect is specific to
  golden hour and low sun.

## artefacts_seen
Faint static IGN dither and cone-step banding on soft shadow edges (forest_edge slope, v6 debug).
