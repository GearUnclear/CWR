Gameplay Systems: The AI Rework & Guerrilla Mode

This dossier documents our overhaul: a from-the-ground-up rework of AI perception, combat, and command, culminating in Guerrilla Mode, a brand-new persistent open-world insurgency with swappable islands and factions chosen at new-game. This is a total overhaul of the engine source: we compile our own engine, so C++ engine changes and SQF mission scripting are equally first-class tools here. The inherited Poseidon engine (entity model, ballistics, terrain, weather) is the foundation all of this is built on, summarized briefly near the bottom of this page. The AI rework and Guerrilla Mode are the point of this project.

★ The AI Rework: Thirteen Mod-Plans

The engine/AI layer of the overhaul is thirteen design plans reworking how the AI fights and perceives, implementable without any new art: only engine C++ and the SQF scripting surface. Plans 01 through 08 are the recommended primary focus (AI tactics & perception); 09 and 10 add small new SQF commands that double as test probes for the AI work; 11 and 12 round out simulation and dev tooling; plan 13 is Guerrilla Mode, the umbrella that consumes all of the above.

Treat these as living, AI-drafted design docs, subject to correction as work proceeds. Difficulty and scope are estimates.

Guerrilla Mode: The Flagship Game Mode

A persistent single-player insurgency: grow a guerrilla cell into the army that liberates an occupied island. You are one Resistance (GUER) fighter ambushing patrols, looting gear, recruiting and training fighters, and capturing towns from an occupying faction that escalates as you do. It is built almost entirely in SQF mission scripting over the reworked AI above, with a small, surgical set of engine-side C++ additions where a script workaround would be worse than a real command.

Two interlocking loops: a tactical loop (Scout → Approach → Strike → Loot → Fade) that maps almost natively onto the existing engine, and a strategic meta-loop (territory → faction growth → enemy escalation) that is hand-built in SQF over global variables, since the engine has no native geoscape or town economy.

Swappable Factions & Islands: the New-Game Flow

A core, launch requirement of Guerrilla Mode: a new game begins by selecting the island, the civilian faction, the resistance faction, and the occupying faction. This drives the mode's architecture from day one: every faction- or island-specific fact lives in swappable data, never hardcoded into scripts.

  • The side model stays fixed, the roster doesn't. The player is always the GUER slot, the occupier the EAST slot, and civilians the CIV slot, but any faction's unit classnames can fill any slot, so "the occupier is the US Army" is just WEST-flavored classnames spawned into EAST-side groups.
  • A faction descriptor carries everything scripts would otherwise hardcode: a unit roster by role × War-Level tier (rifleman / officer / MG / AT / crew), a vehicle pool by tier, a civilian model set that doubles as undercover disguise gear, and starting gear plus the loot-to-unlock mapping. Scripts refer to roles and tiers only, never raw classnames.
  • Islands are data plus a thin shell. Each island gets its own mission folder holding little more than a per-island data file (military zone list, camp/start position, tuning); the zone/economy/spawn managers themselves are shared across islands. Town and city zones auto-seed from the island's own place-name data, with no hand-placing required.

Full CWA game data spans four islands (Everon, Malden, Kolgujev, Nogova) and complete EAST/WEST/GUER/CIV rosters. This is what the swappable-faction design targets. The free Demo data used for the current build is a single island with a minimal roster, so it can't exercise swapping yet; de-hardcoding the current fixed GUER-vs-EAST-on-one-island build into this data-driven form is planned as its own milestone, done before any second island or faction is added.

Combat & Targeting: What the AI Rework Builds On

The rework above reshapes a real simulation the engine already runs. Each observer keeps a live record of every target it knows about:

Tracked valueMeaning for the player
spotabilityHow detectable the target is; it fades over time as contact is lost, the hook for the memory-decay plan.
accuracy / sideAccuracyHow well this shooter (and its side) knows the target's position, also decaying, so stale contacts get sloppy.
dammagePerMinuteHow dangerous the target is. Feeds threat ranking.
subjectiveCostHow "worth shooting" the target is: the score the target-priority plan reshapes to make officers and gunners die first.

Aiming leads the target by its velocity and drops it under gravity over the flight time; accuracy tolerance grows with range and per-weapon dispersion; a friendly-fire corridor check that tightens from 2.0 m past 50 m down to 0.5 m under 25 m keeps allies fighting shoulder-to-shoulder without constant blue-on-blue. This is the machinery the suppression and bullet-penetration plans hook into: a near-miss test on passing rounds, and a material-driven pass-through model for hedgerows and thin cover.

The AI Engine

The perception and movement machinery the rework reshapes lives in engine/Poseidon/AI/. Visibility between observers and targets is a sparse sensor matrix, updated lazily so active threats get re-checked often while distant or friendly pairs barely cost anything. This budget is what lets the stance/concealment and shared-intel plans add cost without tanking framerate. Road movement uses an A* search over the road-link graph; off-road, a baked terrain-cost map grades slope, water and forest density so a tank knows it can't climb a ridge a soldier can walk up. A developer diagnostics overlay can already draw steering arrows, path lines, formation slots and the cost grid. Wiring that into a one-click dev-panel tab is mod-plan 12, the recommended first build for exactly this reason: it lets you see the rest of the rework working.

Scripting: C++ and SQF, Both First-Class

Missions, and all of Guerrilla Mode, run on the engine's built-in SQF/SQS scripting virtual machine. Script is still where content, tuning and mission logic live: edit a file, relaunch, no rebuild. This project compiles its own engine from source, so the actual rule is that a small engine change that deletes a large scripting contortion is worth making. Two new commands prove the point: enableAI (the missing inverse of disableAI) and knownTargets (a group's known contacts returned in bulk), both written engine-side, each costing roughly an hour or two, and both already used as test probes for the AI rework. Guerrilla Mode's own new-game flow, per-island world data, and evaluator quality-of-life commands are planned the same way: engine work where it's the better tool, script everywhere else.

Inherited Engine Foundations

Background context: the Poseidon engine we inherited and overhauled already does a lot on its own. Brief context only; see Under the Hood for the full engine-internals writeup.

The Entity Model

A body (EntityAI) and its brain (AIUnit) are separate objects: the brain references the body instead of deriving from it, which is what lets the AI rework change how a unit thinks without touching what it physically is. Soldiers and vehicles share the same body base; crew are real occupants with commander/pilot/gunner links to the brains riding inside.

Vehicles & Crew

Vehicles add path-following, predictive obstacle avoidance, leader-follow convoy formations, and staged braking on top of the shared body. Support vehicles run a real supply model for transferring cargo, fuel and ammo. This is the backbone of rearming and repair.

World & Terrain

One global map doubles as terrain and object database: a heightmap with distance-based LOD, a road network built by scan/connect/optimize, and three layers of visibility (the AI sensor matrix, geometric line-of-sight, and screen-space occlusion). Islands load from real .wrp world data.

Weather & Sky

Overcast, rain and thunderstorms are actively simulated weather that combines with night to shrink engagement and render distance. Day/night runs off a genuine celestial model: axial tilt, latitude, time of year and day drive sun/moon position, a rotating star field, moon phases and tides.

CAVEAT

Game data (the actual soldiers, weapons, vehicles and islands) ships separately under APL-SA, outside this source repository. The mod-plans described above are living design docs, drafted and subject to correction as the work proceeds.
