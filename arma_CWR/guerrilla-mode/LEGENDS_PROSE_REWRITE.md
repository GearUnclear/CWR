# Legends prose rewrite: design

Status: design, not implemented. Date: 2026-10-11. Issue: [#57](https://github.com/GearUnclear/CWR/issues/57). Roadmap item: `guerrilla-legends`.

Scope: the campaign history and character biographies generated by `FactionHistory.cpp` and shown on the journal's History, event and dossier pages. The owner's bar: **the text must read as a real, lived-in world.** Two rewrites (2026-10-07/08, landed as `7b496c3`) missed it. This document says why, fixes the voice, and changes the structure so a third pass can succeed.

## 1. Problem

Seven reviews of `7b496c3` agree:

- **No narrator, no source.** The text knows private hopes and letter contents. About 17 of 36 bios end on an ironic button and about 12 report interiority nobody could know: "The farmhand kept writing poems and still hoped to publish a collection."
- **Universal templates cannot be specific.** Every string must fit every island and faction, so it falls back on carts, charters and salt licences, with no ID cards, curfews, informers, prices, radios or trucks. A header rule forbids any year.
- **Tics.** "near {Place}" about 66 times, including all 18 titles; "families" 36 or more; all six `kOpenC` say "the authorities broke it". No named person in 60 history strings.
- **One mould.** Bios run 26 to 33 words, 32 of 36 are two sentences, and 29 of 36 refer back by job title ("the grocer"), a side effect of the pronoun lint.
- **A narrow cast.** No old people, spouses, believers, wealthy or returnees. 14 of 18 companions never say why they are in the cell. Six enemy "commanders" are a musician, a porter or a clerk; none has a route, billet, escort or weak spot.
- **One plot per beat**, ending 6 of 6 times on someone keeping a list of names. Locals never win or betray each other. The opening pre-summarises beats 0 and 1.
- **Realism errors.** "Enlisted for regular wages" in conscript armies; farmers holding a ridge for eleven hours against a mechanised column; enemy officers who are natives of the occupied hamlet.

**Why two rewrites failed.** Both began with prose. The 2026-10-08 pass took Hemingway as its model and gave each bio to a fresh writer, so each piece was polished alone as a small story; a character's inner life is the one thing a dossier cannot hold. Reviews checked clarity and repetition, never "could the narrator know this?". The structure (six universal strings per cell, the pronoun lint, the no-date rule) stayed fixed, so better writers produced the same shapes. This time: voice and rules, then structure, then gates, then prose.

## 2. Voice and narrator

The dossier is the cell leader's own file, kept for whoever takes over if the leader is killed: who to trust, who to kill, what is owed, what people say happened. The leader is the player character.

| Table | Written as | Typical source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | a note on the present: what is occupied, rationed, forbidden | own observation |
| History beats | the cell's account of the past | named locals, documents, disputes |
| Companion bios | a member card | what the member says, what the cell has seen |
| Enemy bios | a target file | informants, drivers, girlfriends, overheard talk |

**The diary.** Diary lines are telegraphic and first person ("Reached the Camp alone. One rifle."). The dossier is the same hand with more time: full stops, dropped subjects, "I" and "we", no flourish. The History page's current framing, "The long quarrel" / "As the historians tell it." and the fixed glosses ("The ancient grievance.") in `JournalComposePeoplePlaces.cpp`, changes to match: a sourcing subtitle ("What people here told me.") and a gloss taken from the chosen entry's kind.

**Style rules.** Each is checkable by lint, by tag, or by the reviewer's rubric.

1. Record only what someone saw, signed, said or was overheard saying. Interiority only as an attributed claim ("says", "the cousin claims").
2. Name at least one source per entry.
3. Exactly one gap per entry: unknown, unconfirmed, disputed, two versions, a spelling variant, a crossed-out name.
4. One useful detail, chosen for use: route, time, price, debt, relative, habit, key, vehicle.
5. End on logistics or on the gap. Never a reversal, moral, wish or reflection.
6. One concrete agent per sentence (the commandant, the land office, a named elder). "The authorities", "outsiders", "officials" at most once per page.
7. Fragments and dropped subjects are normal. Bios never contain the person's name (the page title carries it), so they need no pronoun and no job-title back-reference.
8. Humour only as the cell's gossip ("the drivers still talk about it"), never authorial.
9. Dates relative or two-digit ("the winter of '83"), inside the setting's window.
10. Period objects only (section 5).
11. British spelling throughout: harbour, licence (noun), neighbour, defence, programme, labourer.
12. Numbers under a hundred spelled out; digits only in the `'NN` year form.

**Banned** (lintable): hoped, someday, dreamed, since then, to this day, even now, never forgot, would later, little did, in the end, a companion, regular wages, enlisted for (unless the occupier is a volunteer army), the authorities as a sentence subject, outsiders, a new authority; interiority verbs (felt, feared, wondered, knew, realised, missed, regretted) in a sentence without an attribution verb; similes ("like a", "as if"); "not X but Y" in one sentence; rhetorical questions; `{Place}` or "near" in a title; em dashes, `--`, and spaced hyphens used as dashes.

## 3. Content architecture

### 3.1 Setting banks and faction grammar

Universal templates are retired. Content splits three ways:

- **Setting bank:** place, local economy, institutions, the past. Settings: `coldwar` (Malden, Everon, Kolgujev, Nogova, Demo), `lebanon`, `sinai`. A template declares `proseSetting` in `CfgGuerrillaZones`, beside the `islandPhrase` key the separate change (section 10) is adding. A setting with no bank keeps the v2 generator until its bank lands.
- **Faction grammar:** how the leader names the occupier. Read by a `FactionGrammarFor` beside `FactionDisplayName` in `WorldNames.cpp`. It extends `displayName` and the `irregular` key that the separate change adds.
- **Occupier kit:** paperwork and vehicles, a compiled table keyed by the grammar's `kit`.

Any faction can occupy any island, so an entry that names something occupier-specific (the Liaison Unit, a reservist call-up) carries an `occ:` tag. It is drawn only for that kit or service. Most entries use grammar and kit slots and fit any occupier.

| Key | EAST | WEST | IDF | Hizballah | FIA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `colloquial` | the Soviets | the Americans | the Israelis | the Party | the FIA |
| `adjective` | Soviet | American | Israeli | Party | FIA |
| `memberNoun` | soldier | soldier | soldier | fighter | fighter |
| `irregular` | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| `service` | conscript | volunteer | reservist | irregular | irregular |
| `kit` | soviet | us | israeli | irregular | irregular |

The Egyptian, Syrian and Jordanian rosters take conscript and `arab_army`; PLO and PLO_East take the irregular column. The writer verifies each value. Kits: `soviet` {Pass "propusk", Jeep "UAZ", Truck "Ural"}, `us` {"pass", "jeep", "deuce-and-a-half"}, `israeli` and `arab_army` {"permit", "jeep", "truck"}, `irregular` {"pass", "pickup", "truck"}. Every kit value takes the article "a".

### 3.2 Slots

A closed set of noun slots; no clause-level slots.

| Slot | Filled from |
| --- | --- |
| `{Place}`, `{Town}` | settlements only, without replacement |
| `{Feature}` | a non-town feature, with its own preposition |
| `{IslandPhrase}`, `{IslandPhraseCap}` | template `islandPhrase` |
| `{Them}`, `{ThemCap}`, `{TheirAdj}`, `{TheirMan}`, `{TheirMen}` | occupier grammar |
| `{Pass}`, `{Jeep}`, `{Truck}` | occupier kit |
| `{Fig0}` to `{Fig3}` | the history cast (3.4) |
| `{Nick}` | the boss's epithet, target files only |
| `{Vehicle}` | the resolved tank hull's display name, tank files only |

Retired from new records: `{Name}`, the zone in bios, and the faction-phrase permutation (`kFactionPhrase`, `phraseIndex`, `BioPhraseIndex`). The dossier page already prints the name as its title, the role in the caption and the zone in pencil.

### 3.3 Whole tagged entries

Composing bios from slots (origin, hook, role, detail) gives combinatorial variety, but the seams show, sentence two cannot know sentence one, the mould returns by construction, and only fragments can be reviewed. Whole entries cost more writing, but each can be read, scored and fixed as written. **Recommendation: whole entries with tags**, with pool size and a seen-ledger (3.8) handling repetition.

```
struct ProseEntry {
    const char* id;      // "cw.b1.harbour-paper": stable, persisted
    int table;           // Opening, Beat0, Beat1, Beat2, Companion, TgtSniper, TgtCommander, TgtTank
    const char* setting; // coldwar | lebanon | sinai | generic
    const char* title;   // beats only
    const char* text;
    const char* tags;    // "needs:res.fishing gives:ctl.pass kind:breach occ:any fig1:any
                         //  src:fig1 gap:disputed end:logistics why:money age:old trade:driver nick:control"
};
```

Source lives in `guerrilla-mode/legends-prose/<setting>.txt`, one block per entry under a tag header. `tools/legend-prose/gen_legend_prose.py` compiles it to `LegendProseBank*.cpp` and has a `--check` mode, the same pattern as `gen_legend_names.py`. Writers edit text, not C++.

### 3.4 History as a causal chain, with a cast

Four entries per campaign, picked in this order: Beat0 (what was lost), Beat1 (the paper and how it broke), Beat2 (what people remember), then the Opening (the present). Picking the opening last means it describes what holds today and never pre-summarises.

Each entry lists the tokens it `needs` and `gives`, and each pick must have its needs covered:

| Beat0 gives | Beat1 examples | Beat1 gives | Beat2 examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `res.fishing` | harbour paper, boat permits | `ctl.pass` | strike, smuggling boom, amnesty |
| `res.land` | fenced pasture, survey | `ctl.closed-area` | eviction, wedding truce |
| `res.water` | pump agreement | `ctl.checkpoint` | drought trucks, ambush, reprisal |
| `res.road` | crossing toll, curfew deal | `ctl.curfew` | taxi strike, detention sweep |
| `res.work` | quarry contract | `ctl.requisition` | sabotage, informer exposed |

Beat2 kinds: military stand (names the attacker), reprisal, local win, betrayal or resistance wrong, disaster, amnesty or deal. The persisted kind supplies the hub gloss.

**The cast.** At seeding, four figures are drawn from the resistance name pool: `{Fig0}` (Beat0 source, often an elder), `{Fig1}` (the negotiator), `{Fig2}` (the Beat2 figure) and `{Fig3}` (a local collaborator). The draw avoids names rows already use. Entries can require a figure's gender, which needs regional female names (open question 2). The cast is persisted in the `HistoryRecord`, so a later bio can be "{Fig1}'s nephew" or a commander can have "signed the {Pass} order after the harbour paper".

### 3.5 Companion bios

Generated at row creation as now, from the setting, both grammars, the cast and the campaign's used entry ids. Each entry has a `why:` tag (loss, money, kin pressure, belief, coercion, drift, revenge, ambition) and states that reason in the text. Draws are without replacement within a campaign. No two bios in a campaign share a `trade`, `relative`, `end` or `hook` tag.

### 3.6 Enemy target files

Boss files move out of `PreRollBossIdentities`, which runs with the "Commander" sentinel, into `ApplyBossResolution`, after `ResolveLegendRoles` has set `roleResolved`, `zoneName` and the tank class. The epithet and the file share one family tag, so new campaigns draw the epithet from families the role allows and then a file from the same family:

| Family | Epithets |
| --- | --- |
| brutal | Cruel, Bloody, Merciless, Brutal, The Butcher, The Burner, The Whip, The Raider |
| control | The Jailor, The Warden, The Vanisher, The Roundup Man, The Overseer, The Enforcer, The Blockader |
| dispossession | The Evictor, The Displacer, The Fence-Builder, The Home-Taker, The Land-Taker, The Land Thief, The Seizer, The Confiscator |
| venal | Greedy, The Profiteer, The Taxman, The Collector |
| contempt | Cold, Heartless, Smug, Rotten, Ruthless, The Oppressor, The Occupier, The Tyrant, The Colonizer, The Slaver |
| local | The Collaborator (locally born enemies only) |

Family tags live in a new fixture beside the name fixtures; the issue #57 attachment stays verbatim. Every brutal, control or dispossession file states one act of power that earned the name. Every file carries at least two of: route, vehicle, billet, habit, escort, informer, weak spot. Service rules: conscripts are never "enlisted"; reservists have civilian trades and call-ups; volunteers may have bonuses and charge sheets. A commander with no stand (failed placement, pre-Change-3 save) still gets a file, because files never mention the post.

### 3.7 Length bands

| Text | Rendered words | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | 50 to 90 | one block |
| History beat | 40 to 100 | a third of each table under 60 |
| Companion bio | 18 to 24 or 34 to 44 | at least 35% in each band; 1 to 5 sentences |
| Target file | 24 to 44 | sentence counts vary |

Bios stay under `kHistoryBioPageWords` (45) with the longest real names, epithets and hull names.

### 3.8 Pool sizes

A stock campaign shows one opening, three beats, one companion bio (`GM_COMP_NAMES = ["Petra"]`, `core/init.sqs:135`) and three target files. With one draw from a table of N, the next campaign repeats it with probability 1/N. Today's seven history slots of six give 1 - (5/6)^7 = 72%.

| Table (per setting) | Floor | Chance campaign 2 repeats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | 10 | 10% |
| Beat0, Beat1, Beat2 | 12 each | any history text: 1 - 0.9 x (11/12)^3 = 31% |
| Companion | 30 | 3%; any repeat across 5 campaigns: 30% |
| Sniper, Commander, Tank | 12, 24, 12 | about 21%; up to 34% when all three degrade to Commander |

That is 124 entries per setting. Every entry needs at least three possible predecessors so the draw stays near uniform.

**Seen-ledger (phase 5).** A per-profile list of the entry ids shown in the last three campaigns, excluded at seeding. At these floors it removes repeats for three campaigns. Seeding becomes a pure function of (seed, ledger), and its output is still persisted verbatim.

## 4. Quality gates

4.1 to 4.3 are pure unit tests (Catch2, `[legends][prose]`), because the generator reads no config and no world. 4.4 is an integration lane.

**4.1 Coverage matrix, per setting.** Companions (30): at least 3 aged over sixty, 3 under twenty, 3 women, 3 well-off or professional, 4 with a named spouse or child, 3 believers, 3 venal, 2 coerced, 2 ex-auxiliaries, 2 returned emigrants, 3 with a security flaw; no `why` value on more than a quarter of entries. Target files (48): at least 6 brutal, 5 venal, 4 control, 3 bureaucratic, 3 reluctant or coerced, 2 locally born. Service tags must match the kit. Every bio carries `src`, `gap`, `end` and detail tags. No `end` value is reflection or reversal, and none covers more than 40% of a table.

**4.2 History outcome matrix, per beat table of 12.** At least 2 local wins or partial wins, 1 betrayal or resistance wrong, 2 non-military events, and 3 concrete period objects. No kind above a third of the table. Every military Beat2 names the attacker. Every Beat0 has at least 3 compatible Beat1 entries, every Beat1 at least 3 compatible Beat2 entries, and every `gives` token at least 2 compatible openings.

**4.3 Lints and simulation.** Per entry: the banned list, British spelling, ASCII only, the `'NN` digit rule inside the setting window, at least one attribution marker and one gap marker, and only known slots, each fillable under the entry's tags. Bands are measured after rendering with the longest real values. Per table: no content word in more than 20% of entries, no trigram in more than two, and "families" at most once per entry.

*Campaign simulation:* for each setting, each shipped faction pair and 2,000 seeds, generate everything one campaign shows and assert:
- no repeated 4-gram across those texts outside slot values;
- no duplicate bios, and distinct `trade`, `relative`, `end` and `hook` tags;
- three different file families;
- the 3.8 repeat rates, measured on seed pairs, within 5 points;
- no entry drawn at more than twice the uniform rate.

*Real names:* the test parses both faction `.hpp` files through ParamFile and builds grammar with the game's own reader, so it renders what the game emits. It fails on doubled nouns, unsplit CamelCase, doubled articles, the wrong island preposition, and leftover braces. This replaces the hand-listed `kShippedNames` in `test_faction_history.cpp`.

**4.4 Visual.** `ui/guerrilla_journal_capture_800` and `_1440` with one fixed seed per setting, to confirm the longest bio fits beside the portrait.

## 6. Cast and event seeds

**People.** A seventy-year-old ex-headman who fought the last occupation and scorns the cell. A smuggler who joined because checkpoints hurt trade and still sells to both sides. A widow whose pension depends on not being seen with the cell, with a cache under her hen coop. A returned emigrant with cash, a Mercedes and contempt for village feuds. A former auxiliary policeman nobody trusts. A believer who treats the cell as a tool.

**Enemies.** A sniper from a hunting family who keeps a tally book. A reservist tank commander on his third call-up. A political officer who reports on his own men. A local collaborator who knows every family's debts. A conscript; a reservist dentist; a career soldier from a military family; a zealot; a careerist.

**Events.** A wedding that ended a feud (the groom later arrested). A taxi strike, the town's last win. A cell ambush that killed a minibus of workers, still argued over. A drought year when army water trucks complicated loyalties. An amnesty half the fighters took. A smuggling boom that made one family rich.

**Keep and recast.** History: mourners held at the posts until dark (the best current piece); a list of the missing, once per setting instead of every ending; four wells and nine generations; pasture fenced in a week; checkpoints never formally cancelled; winter deaths (not Sinai); which guards would listen; trading favours. Bios: the checkpoint officer's gambling debt to a smuggler (trucks, not wagons); the payroll clerk keeping dead men's pay (US kit only); the bicycle repairer, now mopeds and car radios; the seized address book; the bookkeeper and the beating; the grocer who quit a band that robbed neighbours; permits sold through a partner; the engineer's bridge; the bereaved officer shielding a recruit; desertion and freezing under fire; lessons at home. Titles survive without the place.

## 8. Process and save compatibility

**Order of work.**

1. The owner settles section 9.
2. A setting almanac per setting: section 5 expanded, a timeline in relative dates, the local institutions. Reviewed for realism before any entry is written.
3. **Pilot batch** for one setting (4 beats, 6 companions, 6 target files), rendered by hand with real names and shown to the owner. Nothing more is written until it passes.
4. One writer per setting writes the whole bank in one voice, with tags. No fresh writer per entry: a single consistent narrator matters more than variety of authors.
5. The section 4 gates run on the source file. Failures return to the writer before anyone reads them.
6. **Mandatory critical review** by someone who wrote none of it, reading rendered simulated campaigns rather than tables. Per entry: could the leader know this; is a source named; is there exactly one gap; is the detail useful; does it end on logistics or the gap; does it score 0 or 1 on literaryness? Every failure is reported with the quoted line. Entries scoring 2 or more are rewritten, not edited.
7. **Owner review** of a proof sheet: 10 seeded campaigns per setting, rendered as the journal shows them. Each entry is marked keep, fix or cut, and fixes go back through steps 5 and 6.
8. Land with the build, the unit lanes, the two capture lanes, and a `guerrilla-legends` roadmap log line.

**Writer brief:** section 2 verbatim, the almanac, the slots, the tag schema, the floors the batch must fill, and the section 7 entries. The job is a file kept by a tired person, not a story.

**Save compatibility.** History and bios are stored as resolved prose, so existing campaigns keep their text. `kHistoryVersion` becomes 3. New keys, all default-tolerant: `set`, `ent0` to `ent3`, `kind0` to `kind2` and `cast0` to `cast3` on the record; `bioId` on `LegendRow`. A v1 or v2 record keeps its prose and its old glosses. If a companion row is created later in such a campaign, its new-voice bio is drawn only from entries without cast or hook references. No stock template appends companions mid-campaign, so this mix should not occur in practice.

## 9. Decisions for the owner

**D1. Enemy names.** `EnemyNamePool` maps every occupier outside western, british and israeli to `western`, as issue #57 asks ("modern Western male names"). The result is "Bloody Henry Moore" commanding a Soviet garrison. Options:
- (a) keep the spec;
- (b) draw from the occupier's own pool (`eastern_europe`, `north_africa`, `levant`);
- (c) add a per-faction `enemyNamePool` key that defaults to (b).

**Recommend (c), for new campaigns only.** A dedicated Soviet pool should follow, because `eastern_europe` mixes Polish and Ukrainian names.

**D2. The founding-date rule.** Both the header rule and the digit check in `CheckSharedLints` ban every number. Options:
- (a) keep the ban;
- (b) allow `'NN` years inside a per-setting window;
- (c) allow any year.

**Recommend (b).**

**D3. Banks.** Options:

**D6. Scope and order.** Phase 0 is the separate fixes plus D1 to D5. Phase 1 is the Cold War almanac and pilot batch, followed by the owner's verdict. ## 11. Open questions

1. **Sinai period.** The IDF occupation of the Sinai ended in April 1982, but the project's working date is 1985 (the portrait rig uses 1985-06-15). Should the bank be set around '80, or as an alternate history with no withdrawal?
2. **Female names.** The `female` pool is international and chosen per faction. Widows, mothers and women companions need regional female first names. Should they be added to `additional-names.json`?
3. **Target file layout.** Should it stay as prose under 45 words, or become typed rows ("Billet:", "Moves:", "Weak spot:") on a second page?
4. **Seen-ledger.** Should it live in the profile or beside the saves? Is cross-campaign state acceptable at all?
5. **Diary and objectives.** "fell near <Zone>" and "Eliminate X, role, near Y" keep the "near" tic alive. Should they join this pass?
6. **Recruits.** Only `GM_COMP_*` companions get bios. Should recruits get short cards later?
7. **Hull names.** Some LoBo hull display names may not read well in prose. Does `{Vehicle}` need a per-kit fallback ("the tank")?
